# 31. Terrain v3 -- the generated island

The route is `/terrain-v3`, the map instrument `/terrain-v3-map`, the code `src/v3/`, the gate `scripts/check-v3.mjs`. This section is the plan and, as each step lands, the record of what was built; a step that is not yet built is marked so.

## What v3 is

v3 is a new AUTHOR OF THE FIELD for the v2 engine, not a new renderer. v2 (§18) draws whatever square Float32 heightmap it is handed through `Heightmap.fromRaw` (the leafkin village room is the precedent), puts procedural detail under the image's own resolution on top of it, and reads lakes, rivers and roads out of a layers document. v3 generates that heightmap and that document from a seed, in the browser, at boot, and supplies the detail term as well: one ladder of jitter, its coarse rungs baked into a 4097² image at 2 m and its last three rungs handed to v2 as a `detail` object it evaluates per sample (`src/v3/fine.js`). So the whole cascade from 1024 m to 1 m is v3's, and v2's own fitted roughness stands aside on this world. Nothing in `src/v2/terrain/` is forked.

The island: roughly circular, ocean all round it running out to the box edge, a coast with bays and headlands of many sizes, hills rising inward to a jagged snowy massif at the centre, one or two broad arms off the massif, and inland valleys wide enough to hold lakes -- lakes between mountains and low-lying ones, not only lakes that open on the sea.

## The two representations (§18's rule, applied)

Stored is small and parametric; runtime is baked and O(1) to read.

| Thing | Stored | Runtime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The ground | seed + algorithm version | 4097² Float32 metres at 2 m, cached in IndexedDB as an 8 m base plus 256 m tiles of 2 m detail (only the base and the tiles near the eye are resident), plus the three rungs under the image's floor re-evaluated per sample |
| Biomes | boundary polygons (`biomes[]` in the v3 record; the v2 doc's `serialize()` drops keys it does not know, so they move into the doc when the doc learns them) | class grid rasterised from the polygons in the worker, shipped to the mesh workers beside the heightmap |
| Rivers | doc `rivers[]` polylines, found from the eroded grid | v2's A*-routed splines (`route.js`), as today |
| Lakes | doc `lakes[]`, the sea one uncarved rectangle at y = 0 | v2 `WaterSurfaces`, as today |
| Props | nothing | scattered live from the grid and the biome class, as today |

The biome grid is not a texture: it is a `Uint8Array` the mesher reads per vertex (`GroundTint` in `src/v2/layers/ground.js`, bilinear over a per-class palette), and `shade()` in `chunk-mesh-v2.js` takes that colour as its grass base in place of the grass-to-scrub altitude lerp. Bit-identical to today when the grid is absent.

## Where the work runs

Everything runs at boot in a Web Worker, every layer of it -- macro shape, octaves, biomes, hydrology, rivers. It is cached in IndexedDB (`aurora-v3`, key `seed:algorithmVersion:tune`) as the finished field plus the doc, so the second boot is a read; the tune's signature is in the key so that /terrain-v3's layer switches flip back to an island already made instead of overwriting the one it was being compared against, and only the BAKED rungs are in that signature -- a slot is the 4 MB base record plus 69 MB of tiles (the elevation pyramid, below), and a rung the image is too coarse to hold changes nothing in it; `?regen` throws the cache away and `?seed=N` picks the island. Nothing is pre-baked into `public/` until the algorithm is LOCKED (step E): a layer that is baked is a layer that cannot be revised from the page, and revising is the whole of the next few weeks. The same generator module runs in node for the gate and the PNG script -- `src/v3/` is three-free and DOM-free, the constraint that makes that possible.

The cost is one worker run per client per algorithm version, and the 2 m grid is what it is now spent on. Desktop node measures 4097²: 5.1 s of cone and jitter, 4.1 s of biomes, 11.7 s of hydrology (8.7 s of it the drain, whose four global priority floods are 1.9 s each), 5.2 s of instruments, 26 s in all. Fifteen times the 1.7 s the 1025² grid takes, against sixteen times the texels: everything in the pipeline is per texel and the drain's sweeps are per texel several times over. `scripts/check-v3.mjs` is about a minute of the `npm run check` chain at this grid, and `/terrain-v3-map` is a 26 s wait per tune change, since it generates at the full 4097² on every one.

37 s is at the top of the 15-40 s a Quest 2 was budgeted, on desktop and not on the headset, and the escape is the one step E was always for: bake at lock time. The compression measurement below says what to bake -- not the field, which is 67 MB, but the seed plus the drain's delta, because the cone and the jitter are a pure function of the seed and cost 6.6 s to re-run on the client.

WHAT A 4097² FIELD COSTS TO STORE, measured on the 1025² field and scaled. Raw Float32 is 67 MB. The rg16 PNG v2 ships is 33.6 MB, and its filters are already delta coders -- but splitting a 16-bit height across two 8-bit channels interleaves the high and low byte, and the low byte of a neighbour predicts nothing, so rg16 gzips to only 1.84 MB at 1025² against 1.19 MB for the same values in two PLANES and 1.14 MB for a Paeth predictor run on the 16-bit values themselves (43% saved, residual entropy 7.46 bits a texel, so about 15 MB at 4097²). Delta coding is worth having and is not the win. The win is not storing the procedural part at all: the hydrology moves a twentieth of the texels and 70.9% of the grid is sea the drain never touches, both pure functions of the seed, so seed + the drain's delta gzips to 0.31 MB at 1025² and about 5 MB at 4097². Whatever is baked at lock time should be that.

## Scale

`WORLD_SIZE` stays 8192 m so nothing in v2 has to be told. 4097 texels at 2 m (`TEXELS` in `generate.js`). The island's mean coast radius is 2.5 km, so the sea runs 1.2-1.7 km out from the coast to the box edge and keeps falling all the way.

The generator owns the WHOLE jitter cascade, 1024 m down to 1 m, eleven rungs, each moving its lattice by a quarter of its own spacing down to 32 m and an eighth from 16 m to 2 m (step B). What the grid decides is not where the cascade stops but where it is STORED. Four texels to a lattice node is the coarsest an image can record a rung rather than alias it, so a 2 m image holds the rungs down to 8 m -- eight of them, baked -- and the last three (4, 2 and 1 m) are evaluated per sample at read time by `src/v3/fine.js`, which v2 takes as its `detail` term. Halving the texel from 8 m to 2 m moved that seam down two octaves, from 32 m to 8 m, and it is why the grid was widened: the image cannot hold what it cannot resolve, and a 24 m trunk bed is 12 texels at 2 m against 3 at 8 m.

The read-time rungs are not a separate recipe. `octaveTable` builds all eleven and `splitOctaves` cuts the list at four texels to a node, so which side a rung falls on is a storage decision and nothing else -- the rung's rotation and hash are fixed by its index, so moving the cut moves no ground. `FineJitter.at(x, z, cell, ...)` then fades each rung in over `cell*2` to `cell*4`, which is the LOD: a 128 m chunk vertex sees none of them, a 0.5 m collision probe sees all three, and `cell = 0` (collision, picking, the editor) is every rung at full weight.

v2's own fitted roughness does not merely stand aside on this world, it cannot stand at all, and `field.js` now says so: with a `detail` override present nothing is calibrated and `V2Height.calibration` is null. `calibrateRough` extrapolates the field's curvature from lags of 4 and 8 m down to a quarter metre and sizes its octave table to the deficit, and at 2 m texels there is no deficit left -- the bicubic over the image already supplies 79% of that extrapolated curvature with the fine rungs at a quarter of their spacing and 183% with them at an eighth, and over 100% is the case `calibrateRough` throws on. The override reads the ground bilinearly besides, so the bicubic the fit measures is not a surface anything samples. Both halves are asserted: the supplied term stands and fits nothing, and the bare field REFUSES the 2 m import rather than laying a second ladder over the one the image already carries.

## The steps

Each step is its own look on the map page and in 3D before the next goes on, because the lesson of §14 is that shape complaints must become numbers before they become code, and the number has to be read off the layer that is wrong, not off the sum.

### Step 0 -- skeleton and instruments (built)

`src/v3/island.js` is the field, `src/v3/generate.js` the pipeline (`generate({ seed, n, log, tune })` -> `{ v, seed, n, cell, height, meta, doc, biomes, ground, stats, tune, ms }`), `src/v3/paint.js` the hillshade and layer painters, `src/v3/worker.js` the worker, `src/v3/store.js` the IndexedDB cache. `/terrain-v3-map` runs the generator in the worker every time (and so is what fills the cache) and draws it as relief, elevation, slope, closed bowls, biomes and water with a hover readout and the stats; its sidebar carries a field per warp octave and per jitter octave (`tune`, metres; the URL carries them as `?warp=` and `?jitter=` and a `defaults` button clears them), and the island made from edited numbers is cached under the seed's own key, so `/terrain-v3` flies whatever the map page last made. `scripts/island-png.mjs` writes any layer to a PNG; `scripts/check-v3.mjs` asserts determinism, finiteness, land fraction, a summit near the centre, a sea floor that falls, an irregular coast, a doc that validates, the rg16 round trip, and that `V2Height` will stand on the field. `/terrain-v3` boots the v2 engine on the result behind the cache with the game's own `Player`: walking by default, space to fly and hold to climb, speed rising with height, double-tap to land. Its sidebar carries a checkbox per layer -- one per jitter rung (the last three labelled read-time), one for the hydrology, one for the cliff tabling, and C for the read-time scarp -- and unticking one regenerates the island without it and stands the world back up where you were, so a layer can be judged by its absence; each combination of the BAKED rungs is its own cache slot, so going back is instant, and a read-time rung costs no regenerate at all, and `regen` throws the current slot away for when the algorithm rather than a switch has changed. The sun is held at noon there: a moving sky is a distraction from the shape. Its near plane steps with height above ground (0.1 m on foot, up to 8 m at 200 m up) so the shoreline stays crisp from altitude instead of z-fighting the sea; `window.__v3` exposes the rig, camera, look, player, height and clock for the console and the headless probe.

### Step A -- the cone (built)

`MACRO` in `island.js`. A circular cone standing in a sea that falls away, and nothing smaller than the coast's own features, so the map view is judging only whether the island's silhouette and its skirt are right.

1. **The warp.** Each texel's position is displaced by three octaves of simplex (1600 m / 150 m, 520 m / 45 m, 170 m / 15 m amplitude), and every later term reads the warped position. This is what puts bays and headlands of three sizes on a coast that would otherwise be a circle. Amplitude stays under a tenth of the wavelength: past that the warp folds the plane over itself, and every fold is a crease of terrain running inland.
2. **The coast radius.** `R(theta) = R0 * (1 + a1 n1(theta) + a2 n2(theta))`, noise sampled on a circle so it is periodic by construction: a low-order term at +-32% for the big peninsulas and the deep bays, a higher-order term at +-9% for the middling ones. A peninsula is an arm: because height is a function of `r / R(theta)`, ground that reaches further out reaches further out AT HEIGHT, and the ridge runs down it on its own. The same rule would run every middling bay inward as a valley converging on the summit, so the higher-order term is skin-deep: full from 0.9 R0 outward, gone inside 0.35 R0.
3. **The profile.** `d = r / R(theta)`, 0 at the centre and 1 at the coast. Land is `H (1 - d)^1.2` with H = 450 m: nearly a straight cone, so the slope the cone contributes is much the same at the coast as under the massif and the hills the jitter puts on it read alike everywhere (at an exponent of 1.7 the interior was a steep rough massif over a flat skirt, which is not the island wanted). The centre is not a point: the top 8% of the cone is capped by a tangent parabola, flat at the top, so the pedestal tops out at 414 m as a massif and not a spike. Below 0.12 H the profile is bent toward a quadratic apron that keeps 15% of the cone's slope at the waterline, so the last few hundred metres before the shore are lowland but never a plane the sea can fight. Sea is a shelf 6 m under reached by a 24 m drop, 120 m wide, then a slope of 1:9 that keeps falling to the box edge: -229 m on average along the edge, deeper in the corners, against an encoding floor of -600 m (rg16 quantum 2.4 cm).

Cliff coasts were tried as sectors of the circle where the cone is held up at 110 m to the waterline and dropped to the shelf across 32 m, and rejected: a wall that long and that even along the coast's own curve is not a cliff. If the island is to have cliffs they will have to come from something the ground does -- a fault, or what step D's carving leaves standing -- not from the coast's parameterisation.

Instruments: land fraction, summit height and offset from the centre, coast irregularity (the shoreline's length against the circumference of the circle with the island's area), the relief-by-scale table (rms of what a box blur at 32, 128, 512 and 2048 m removes over land), and the closed bowls (what a priority flood would pond over a metre deep, grouped, over 2 ha).

### Step B -- the jitter (built)

`JITTER` in `island.js`. Eleven rungs of value noise, one per scale from 1024 m down to 1 m, halving each time. A rung is a square lattice of nodes at that spacing; every node is moved up or down by a random amount up to the rung's entry in `amps` (256, 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 2, 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.25 m, and the numbers the map page's jitter fields edit). A quarter of the spacing holds from 1024 m down to 32 m, which is the shape of the island -- the peninsulas, the archipelagos, the variance between flat country and bumpy. From 16 m to 2 m it is an EIGHTH: a quarter there read as grit underfoot rather than landform, so those four rungs were halved, and the 1 m rung was left where it was because it is barely detectable either way; the ground between nodes is a smoothstep between the four corners; and the rungs are added, so each refines the one before it. A node's move is a hash of its lattice coordinates and the seed, so the same seed always gives the same island and any texel can be asked for on its own. Two details keep the lattice from showing: every octave's lattice is turned by the golden angle from the last so no two share axes, and the interpolation is a smoothstep rather than a straight lerp, because a lerp is continuous but its slope is not, and the lattice lines read as creases across every hillside (`interp: 'linear'` is kept so this can be seen).

Why 1024 m and not the whole box: the recipe cannot start at 4096 m, since +-1024 m of jitter at that scale is the island, not a refinement of it. The cone owns the shape above 1024 m; the jitter owns everything under it.

Why it stops at 1 m: that is the finest relief a walker reads as ground rather than as texture, and v2's props and its own micro stack own what is under it.

WHERE THE LADDER IS CUT, which is a different question from where it stops. `splitOctaves(octaveTable(seed), cell)` divides the eleven rungs at `TEXELS_PER_NODE * cell` -- four texels to a lattice node. Above that line a rung is baked into the image; below it, it is not stored at all and `FineJitter` evaluates it per sample. Four is the floor because a lattice node is an independent random number: a two-texel node spacing is the Nyquist period exactly and samples as a two-texel sawtooth, and a one-texel spacing is white noise with the rung's golden-angle rotation beating against the texel grid as moire. Both were tried in the image and both read as a row of identical humps marching along every crest: grain, not landform, doubling the field's curvature and its slope reversals (10.5% of texels to 22.2%) for 0.3 m on the mean step. So the image records down to `4 * cell` and the rest is read-time. This is a limit on the IMAGE and not on the terrain -- the same rung is the same ground either side of the cut, hashed and rotated by its index, so widening the grid from 8 m to 2 m moved the cut from 32 m down to 8 m and moved no ground.

The rungs below the cut reach v2 through `field.js`'s `detail` option, which is why nothing in `src/v2/` is forked: v2 already had a slot for a term that invents everything under the image's resolution, and v3 puts its own last rungs in it instead of a fitted roughness. Three consequences, all deliberate. The reconstruction goes BILINEAR when a detail override is present (`_attachReconstruction`), because a bicubic over a lattice sampled four texels to a node rounds off the very rungs the image was widened to carry. The term is not calibrated, not slope-boosted and not eased at the shore, unlike v2's own: it is a continuation of the generator, and the amplitudes are the ladder's, not a fit. And it travels to the mesh workers as a `{ seed, cell, jitter }` descriptor they rebuild it from, on the same footing as the layers document, because a detail term that reached the mesher and not the main thread would make the ground she is drawn on and the ground she collides with two different surfaces.

The read-time rungs are LOD-gated, which is what makes them affordable: `FineJitter.at` weights each rung by `smoothstep(cell*2, cell*4, spacing)`, so a rung is only evaluated where the sample spacing can record it. A 128 m chunk vertex gets none of the three, a 2 m one gets the 4 m rung fading in, and the 0.5 m collision probe gets all three. `cell = 0` means full weight on every rung and is what collision, picking and the editor pass.

The jitter is the same everywhere inland, so a region by the coast is as hilly as one under the massif. It eases only in the last 20% of the radius (`shore.band`), down to 30% of itself at the waterline and over the sea (`shore.floor`), because full jitter there is the whole ladder's +-508 m on a 54 m apron, and its top rung alone +-256 m, which shreds the coast into islets; 30% still cuts the apron into coves and skerries, which is most of the coastline's character. Scaling the jitter by height instead (full on the summit, 30% at the shore) was tried and rejected: with the steeper profile it made the interior rough and the lowlands smooth, an island steep in the middle and flat toward its shores.

Seed 20260824 measures, on the raw 2 m field: land 28.9% (19.4 km2), summit 560 m all but on the centre, coast 47.9 km at 2.41x its circle, sea floor -101 m a kilometre out and -233 m at the box edge, 8 bowls over 2 ha ponding 1.01 km2 (largest 0.29 km2, 92 m deep, deepest of them 139 m), relief 3.4 / 15.1 / 54.8 / 155.0 m at 32 / 128 / 512 / 2048 m, the field spanning -468 to 560 m. Halving the four fine rungs is visible in the first and the last of those and nowhere else: the 32 m relief fell 3.7 to 3.4 and the coast, which is a count of texel edges and so reads the finest rung that reaches it, shortened 52 km to 47.9. The summit sits at the centre because the ladder is smooth where the dome is steepest, not because the lattices meet there: every rung's rotation turns about the origin, so the origin is a node of all eleven, but the jitter there is +147 m where a quarter of the box is further from zero and the field across it is flat to a metre. The bowls are measured on the raw field, before step D: they are what full jitter to the coast leaves in ground with no drainage yet, and step D chooses its lakes among them and drains the rest. `scripts/island-png.mjs --jitter interp=linear` renders an experiment without touching the cache.

### Step C -- climate and biomes (built)

`src/v3/biomes.js`, run in the worker after the octaves, about 0.2 s. Temperature is a base less a latitude gradient across the box (colder north) less a lapse of 1 per 1300 m, plus a little noise. Moisture is a westerly marched texel by texel across each row: it evaporates over the sea, rains out in proportion to the ground rising under it, dries a little every texel, and the rain that falls is box-blurred 300 m; to that is added the remaining humidity and a coast term (the sea mask blurred 500 m). So the west is wet and forested, the lee of the massif is plains, and the coast is damper than the interior. The seven classes are thresholds on the pair set at the LAND'S OWN QUANTILES, so each holds a seventh of the land whatever the seed: the coldest seventh is arctic; the next two sevenths temperate, split at their median moisture into plains and forest; the warmest four sevenths hot, split at their moisture quartiles into desert, canyon, jungle and swamp. The snow line in the doc is the height a seventh of the land lies above, so v2's snow and the arctic class roughly agree. The lookup is noise-warped (70 m at 260 m) so no border is smooth, and patches under 6 ha are given to their neighbours.

The classes are then TRACED: marching squares per class, each loop simplified to 10 m by Douglas-Peucker, and the polygons (world metres, to the decimetre) are what the v3 record stores. The grid is rebuilt from them by scanline fill in class order with the slivers the simplification leaves taken by the nearest painted texel; the gate asserts the rebuilt grid agrees with the traced one on more than 98% of texels (99.5% on seed 20260824) and that the cached grid IS the rasterisation of the cached polygons. Each class gets a flat distinct ground colour through `GroundTint` and `shade()` until the props exist to tell them apart; the map page's biomes layer paints the same palette over the hillshade.

Seed 20260824 after the warp and the despeckle: every class between 13.8% and 15.0% of the land, the snow line at 299 m with 14.3% of the land above it; forest and swamp on the wet west, desert and canyon in the massif's lee to the east, jungle across the hot south, arctic on the peaks and the cold north. `CLIMATE` shapes WHERE each class falls; the shares it cannot change.

### Step D -- hydrology (built)

`src/v3/basins.js` and `src/v3/hydrology.js`, run in the worker after the biomes on the raw field. The water is DRAINED, THEN READ: the jitter's own closed dips are emptied first, and then one priority flood on the drained ground reads the level every remaining pool stands at and an acyclic way off every flat, off which come the lakes and the rivers. ONE THING IN THE STEP MOVES THE FIELD AND IT ONLY EVER LOWERS IT -- the drain's rim dish. Nothing raises a texel and nothing else cuts one, so a height is the jitter's own unless the drain took a rim off it, and the rivers reach the doc as polylines that v2 cuts a bed for at render time (§2 `paths.js` `carveRivers`). Phase A's toolkit (`src/sim/hydrology.js`: priority flood, D8 routing, accumulation) is the whole of the reading; nothing here calls `phase-a.js`. The rule the gate asserts is unchanged: every drop of rain on land has a path that never climbs to the sea, a lake or another river, and every lake is a bowl.

1. **Basins** (`basins.js`, `BASINS`). The jitter's own problem, and the first thing the step does. Eleven octaves of lattice jitter make a landscape that reads well dry and falls apart the moment water is put in it: 585 closed dips over a metre deep on the 2 m grid, which flooded to their spills put whole biomes under water. So they are drained, and THE ONLY THING A BASIN IS RANKED ON IS HOW DEEP IT IS -- not its area, not its shape, not how round it is. The `keep` (20) deepest are marked for retention and every other one for a full drain to under `pond` (1 m). THE ONE OTHER THING ASKED OF A KEPT BASIN IS THAT THE LAND HOLDS IT: ground within `keepRim` (24 m) of its water may not fall `keepDrop` (20 m) below that water. A pit on a hillside can be twenty metres deep behind a rim two texels wide, and the water in one is drawn as a sheet standing over the slope beside it -- the plateau reported at -406,946 and -720,908 on seed 1, whose lakes perched 4 m and 6 m above the ground a rim away. Such a body is drained like any other basin, nothing is promoted in its place, and the count is `perched` (none on seed 20260824). A KEPT BASIN IS STILL DRAINED, to `keepDepth` (30 m): left alone the deepest basins on the island flood to their spills and take a biome with them, and held to thirty metres they are lakes in a valley rather than the valley itself. THE RIM IS CUT WITH A BROAD DISH AND NOTHING ELSE. A pond's lowest rim point is found and a dish `brush` (0.5) of the pond's own width across, floored at `minBrush` (40 m) and capped at `maxBrush` (320 m), lowers it by a step: `target = max(want, elev - drop * (1 - smoothstep(r/R)))` written as `elev = min(elev, target)`, which takes a saddle out of the rim where a narrower brush cuts a slot down it and reads as a tunnel. `want` is the round's own floor and it is what stops a pond no wider than its dish from eroding for ever: the pond's bed lies inside the disc, so a cut clamped only at the rim's new height takes the bed down with the rim, the water stands as deep as it did, and the basin cuts tens of metres over hundreds of rounds without draining. Then the pond is re-solved and the cut repeated -- 0.7 rounds at the mean and 126 at the most -- until its water is under its target. IT ONLY EVER LOWERS A TEXEL, so `raised` is a count and the gate asserts it is zero. ONE FLOOD PER SWEEP, THEN LOCAL WORK: the global priority flood is 1.9 s at 2 m and there is no budget to repeat it per round, so `esc[c]` -- the level at which water standing on a cell gets away to the sea -- is taken from that one flood and then kept true by hand, and because a cut only ever lowers ground a stale `esc` is never an underestimate. Basins are worked lowest spill first, so whatever a basin spills into has already been settled, and `solvePond` grows from a seed over its lowest frontier cell and breaks at the first cell under the level whose `esc` is lower -- a few thousand cells a round instead of sixteen million. Three cases in it are structural rather than tunable and each has its own branch: a pond whose outlet has been cut below the waterline has joined the sea and is handed to it (5 of them); a pond whose lowest rim point is a kept lake's own water has been swallowed by that lake, which happens when a cut made for some third basin joined the two (9); and everything a pond ever covered is settled when it finishes, dry ground at its own elevation and water at the surface, because otherwise the footprint the level left behind re-seeds itself as thousands of separate ponds. A DISH CAN DIG A NEW BASIN, SO THE SWEEP REPEATS. Most of a 320 m disc is ground no pond ever covered, whose `esc` was read once and never re-read, so taking a saddle out of a ridge can leave the ground just outside it lower than the ground beyond -- a dip that was not there when the basins were enumerated, and 48 of them over a metre deep after the first sweep. So the field is re-flooded and swept again until an enumeration comes up empty, up to `sweeps` (5), which on this island is the third. Only the first sweep may keep a basin: the lakes are the island's own hollows, never a hole this pass dug. EVERY SWEEP RE-READS THE KEPT LAKES OFF THE FLOOD IT JUST RAN, and nothing protects a lake from a dish cut for one of its neighbours: erosion is allowed collateral effects, so a lake is what the ground leaves it and never what was written down before the cut. Two lakes a cut joined come back as one body at one level, an arm a cut took off a lake becomes ordinary ground the same sweep drains, one a dish dug past `keepDepth` is drained again, and one a dish emptied is dropped and counted (`lost`, 1 of them on this island). What the drain does still refuse is to cut a kept lake's own BED -- the dish skips those cells -- and that refusal cannot dam anything, because a submerged bed is never the route by which water escapes.
2. **Cliffs** (`cliffs.js`, `CLIFFS`). OFF by default (`STEPS.cliffs` in `generate.js`), and switched back on from /terrain-v3's sidebar or `tune.steps.cliffs`, because flown rather than read off the map the ladder reads as striation: a rise of constant height repeated along a hillside, and its benches -- 18.9% of canyon land under 10 degrees against the forest's 11.8 -- come back as rounded domes exactly where the peaks should be sharpest. What follows is what it does when it is on. The drained field has no cliffs in it and thresholding cannot find any: over the 304449 land texels the steepest texel-to-texel drop is 25 deg at the median and 44.5 at p90, and 0.293% fall more than two texel-distances. So they are made. A ladder of rungs `step` metres apart cuts the slope into bands, and inside a band a texel's height is remapped through a two-piece ramp: a FACE over the lowest `riser` of the band taking `snap` of its fall, and a SHELF over the rest carrying the remaining `1 - snap` gently up to the rung above. The face's grade is the old grade times `snap / riser`, the shelf's the old grade times `(1 - snap) / (1 - riser)`, and both ends of the ramp are fixed points, so the ladder never drifts. Three rules shape it. THE SHELF SITS ABOVE ITS FACE, because a shelf below one can only be cut down to, and that cut is a trench -- the ground under the face was never touched, so the shelf sits in a hollow, and where the weight varies from texel to texel the hollow becomes a run of dishes. Lifting instead makes `f >= frac` everywhere and the pass never lowers a texel (it throws if it computes a move that would); what lies under a face is the untouched slope the jitter made, a real downhill grade, shy of flat, no trench. THE LADDER IS SPARSE: a rung carries a face only when its hash stands above both its neighbours', about one rung in three and never two in a row, so there is always a whole band of untouched slope between one face and the next and a hillside is not a staircase. Integer hash and not noise, because what is wanted between neighbouring rungs is independence, not continuity. THE HEIGHT WANDERS: `snap` is drawn per rung from a span the class sets, so one band puts half its fall in the face and leaves the shelf a walkable grade while the next puts all of it there and goes flat, and `roll` (0.25 of a step over `rollScale`, 240 m) slides the rungs up and down so a shelf tilts and a face grows and shrinks along its run. Reading `riser`: a face is `riser * step / tan(slope)` metres of ground, so under 8 m it lands inside one texel and the whole of `snap * step` falls between two of them -- the most the grid can hold, and a wall. Canyon (`step` 34, `riser` 0.11, `snap` 0.80-1) and desert (28, 0.13, 0.70-1) sit under the line and get walls with near-level tops; arctic (26, 0.30, 0.50-0.90), forest and jungle (21, 0.34, 0.45-0.85) and plains (17, 0.42, 0.50-0.85) sit over it and get a firm bank over a shelf that still falls away under the walker, which is the "45 degrees is fine here" half of the ask. THE BAND: a per-texel coin flip on steep ground gives salt and pepper, so the gate is one octave of simplex at `wavelength` (320 m) thresholded at the QUANTILE of that noise over each class's own steep ground -- `share` (canyon 0.85, desert 0.58, arctic 0.38, forest 0.30, jungle 0.24, plains 0.16, swamp 0) means exactly the fraction of that class's steep ground the band lets through, of which the sparse ladder moves about a third -- and `bandFeather` past the threshold tapers a band's ends off. THE PHASE: a second, slower noise at `phaseScale` (1100 m) offsets the ladder by up to a step, or every shelf on the island would bench at the same heights and read as a contour map. The slope gate (`minSlope` 26 deg, `slopeFeather` 6) is there to spare the flats, not to scale the effect: a wide feather leaves the 30-degree ground, which is most of the steep ground there is, tabled at a fifth strength and no cliff anywhere. Nothing under `minHeight` (6 m) tables, so the coast keeps its beaches. It runs before everything that reads the field, so the lakes, the route and the rivers are all solved on the shape that will be drawn.
3. **Lakes** (`hydrology.js`, `LAKES`). The basins the drain kept, and only those, since every other one was emptied: the field is re-flooded on the drained ground and each kept basin is grown from its own deepest cell, which is where the water still stands. A body left under `minDepth` (1.5 m) after all -- a dish cut for some other basin took its rim on the way past -- is dropped and counted. Each is kept whole: every cell the flood wets round its pool, up to and including the spill, at the spill's level, so the river out of it leaves from the water. A lake has no dam: its shore is wherever the ground meets its level.
4. **Route.** Priority flood, D8 steepest descent with every lake cell sent along the flood's tree to the spill (steepest descent off the flat lets the cells beside the spill step over the rim on their own, and each is then a source, so one lake would let out three rivers a few cells apart), accumulation in cells. WHAT STILL PONDS OUTSIDE THE LAKES IS MEASURED AND LEFT WHERE IT IS: the shallow remainder the drain stopped at, held to `pond` by the sweeps, 0.29 km2 of it at 0.23 m mean, and a hollow with no water drawn in it is what a dry hollow looks like. The second class the instrument counts -- ground the drain marked as a kept lake's water that the finished field ponds at some OTHER level, a lobe a later dish cut off from its lake -- is zero by construction now that every sweep re-reads its lakes off the flood the last cut left, and the gate asserts exactly zero rather than a bound.
5. **Rivers.** A cell outside the sea and the lakes with `RIVERS.minCatchment` (5 ha) draining through it is a river cell. TWO RIVERS THAT COME NEAR EACH OTHER ARE ONE RIVER, and this is read off the route before anything is traced: D8 hands every cell a single receiver, so a tributary can run a hundred metres alongside its own trunk either side of a divide no higher than the water and never meet it, and since v2 cuts a bed under each polyline what gets drawn is two trenches with a thin wall between them -- reported at -530,830 on seed 1. So every river cell drains into the nearest cell of MORE catchment within `joinReach` (20 m), the line below that junction then carries nothing of its own, falls under `minCatchment` and stops being a river, and the two become one where they first came near. Three things disqualify a neighbour: ground between them standing over `joinWall` (3 m) above the higher of the two, which is a divide between two valleys and not a wall between two channels; water or sea on the line between them, which would run a river through a lake; and a cell the water already reaches by a way no more than `joinBend` (2.5) times the straight gap, which is this same channel round a bend rather than another river -- without that ratio every straight reach short-circuits itself and is cut to every tenth cell, and with it a tributary that has run beside its trunk still joins, because its water's way round to the trunk cell beside it is the whole of that run. No edge it draws can close a loop: catchment never falls downstream, an edge is only ever drawn towards strictly more of it, and the receiver must come earlier in the flood's own order. The network is then walked from every mouth (a river cell whose receiver is not one) up its largest donor to a source, the other donors becoming tributaries whose last point is the trunk cell they join. A SOURCE FED BY A LAKE BEGINS AT THAT LAKE'S OUTLET AND EXACTLY AT ITS SURFACE. The outlet is the wet cell the flood's tree sends to dry ground, so the ground there stands at the very level the lake is drawn at and v2 pins the first node to a level the node is already on. A source set any further into the water is a node metres under the surface, and since v2 cuts a bed under every leg it is handed, what it cuts is a channel across the lake floor. The ring below keeps the outlet texel as a vertex, so the first node is never left outside the water it starts in: 19 of the 19 lake-fed sources on seed 20260824 start 0.00 m under their lake. Cell centres are Douglas-Peucker simplified to `tolerance` (5 m) with the junction cells pinned, because a junction the simplifier drops leaves a tributary's mouth beside the trunk rather than on it and v2 cannot pin it, and with the cells either side of a fall of more than `pinDrop` (3 m) pinned too: the simplification is in PLAN and reads no elevation at all, so on a straight reach it would drop a cliff's lip and foot and leave v2 a chord its router is free to lay up to `tolerance` metres to the side, off the notch the water cut and onto the face beside it. The water's half-width is metres from catchment, never cells from a brush: `halfAtMin` (2.4 m) times the square root of catchment over `minCatchment`, tapered from the source by `tip` (0.35), widened by `flatWiden` (0.9) where the floor is flat and wandered by `wobble` (0.1) over `wobbleScale` (40 m) so no reach holds one width for long and its two banks are never parallel, floored at `minHalf` (0.5 m) and capped at `maxHalf` (20). A CONFLUENCE NEEDS NO TERM OF ITS OWN: two equal branches meeting is twice the catchment, so the square-root law leaves the trunk 41% wider than either arm, which is what the real one does. THE MOUTH IS THE WIDEST PART OF A RIVER and the taper is most of the reason: `tip` alone brings a mouth to x1.6 that river's own mean width, where the catchment law gives x1.16 and no exponent honest enough to be a width law can lift it. BOTH ENDS OPEN OUT WHERE THEY MEET STANDING WATER -- `flare` (0.2) at a mouth entering a lake or the sea, `spout` (0.5) at a source leaving one, each a share of the local width eased out over `flareLen` (30 m) of arc. The two shares differ because the taper runs with the mouth and against the source: `tip` makes a source the narrowest point of its river, while water leaving a lake is as wide as the lake lets it be. At 4097 on seed 20260824 a mouth comes out x1.87 its river's own mean and a source x1.19 the reach thirty metres below it. A RIVER ALSO BENDS INSIDE ITS OWN VALLEY FLOOR, because the route is a D8 walk down eight headings that the simplification straightens most of what is left of: each cell is drawn as much as `meander` (2.5) channel widths off it, perpendicular to the local heading, over two octaves of noise at `meanderScale` (130 m) and a third of that, eased to nothing over `meanderEnds` (40 m) at either end so a source stays at its outlet and a mouth on the water or the trunk it joins. THE AMPLITUDE IS BOUNDED BY RELIEF AND NEEDS NO FLATNESS TERM: an offset may stand `meanderClimb` (1.5 m) off the ground of the channel it came from, either way, which is full amplitude on a floodplain and a metre or two against a gorge wall. Then the PAIR is checked, on the simplified line and not on the cells: each kept node is pulled back toward its own channel until it stands no higher than the node before it plus whatever the channel itself rose, since two neighbours pushed opposite ways stand metres apart in height with each inside its own allowance, and v2 beds whatever line it is handed. Cell-wise that same rule tightens as the grid gets finer -- at 2 m spacing a channel falls centimetres a cell while a bend swings tens of metres sideways -- and would sand the meanders off the fine field while leaving them on the coarse one. At 4097 the line runs x1.19 its own chord and stands 3.3 m off the descent at the mean, and no leg of it climbs more than 0.71 m of the metre the drain leaves standing. A tributary under `minLength` (60 m) that nothing feeds is not drawn.
6. **The doc.** Each lake body is ONE v2 lake record with `carve 0` carrying its own shore: the contour round the texels its water covers, traced by marching squares over the flooded mask, simplified to `LAKES.tolerance` (1.5 m) and cut into segments of at most `LAKES.spacing` (10 m). The outer ring comes first and its islands follow, wound the other way -- the water on the left, which is how v2's own tracer winds what it contours -- so `shoreline.js` hands the ring straight to the ear-clipper and reads no height for a lake at all. A SHORE IS NOT A SHAPE FITTED TO THE WATER, IT IS THE WATERLINE ITSELF. The drain picks its lakes on depth alone, so some of them are long or forked, and one ellipse round a forked pool covers the ridges between its arms: v2 then draws water in every hollow under the level inside it, and a 100 m2 sliver of hillside 70 m under the level is a wall of water on a slope that costs almost nothing by area -- which is why a fit had to be judged on how deep it spilled, split up to sixteen ways per body and shrunk against what the shrinking left dry, for 76 records and a 1.38 m worst spill on seed 20260824. A baked ring has nothing to spill. Its only error is the half-texel the contour stands off the centres it was traced from plus the `tolerance` the simplification may move it by, and that lies on the shore and nowhere else: at 4097 on seed 20260824, 20 lakes over 0.437 km2 with 2594 shore vertices and 7 islands, longest segment 10.00 m, disagreeing with the flooded texels themselves over 0.0050 km2 -- 1.1% of the water, all of it in that band. Every boundary vertex of a spill survives the simplification, or a river's own first node is left outside the water it starts in. Nothing else about the record is read for the footprint: a lake with a ring has no shape, no feather and no radii, and `footprint` is inside the rings or outside them. The sea stays lake l1. Each river is a v2 river polyline `[x, z, w]`, source to mouth, which v2's `paths.js` re-routes over its coarse heightmap and pins into the lake or river it ends in.

### Sheer faces -- the scarp operator (built)

`src/v2/height/scarp.js`, a `RELIEF_KNOBS` entry (`scarp`, off value 0, and 0 is bit-identical like every other knob's), composed into `V2Height`'s reconstruction through the one `attachCrease` choke point beside `_linear` and `_crease`; `/terrain-v3` runs it at 1 and C toggles it. It runs at READ time on every sample rather than in the generator, and that is the whole reason it can do what the tabling cannot: `cliffs.js` writes texels, and a step of any size between two texels comes back through Catmull-Rom smeared over about 0.8 of a texel, so a written face tops out near 78 deg over a run of 6.4 m however deep the drop is -- a wall to climb along, not one to stand under -- while an operator inside the reconstruction may put the break anywhere between the texels.

It is a HEIGHT REMAP and not a displacement in the ground plane. Elevation is cut into bands of `SCARP_RISE` (34 m), offset by a `SCARP_DRIFT` (700 m) noise `phase` so that two faces on one hill do not break at the same elevation and read as a contour line drawn across both; inside a band the height goes through `h' = RISE * (k + S(t))` with `t = frac((h - phase) / RISE)`, `S` two halves of a power curve of exponent `SCARP_POWER` (14) meeting at (0.5, 0.5). `S(0) = 0` and `S(1) = 1` exactly, and the construction rests on those two identities: they are what make `k + S(t)` continuous across a band boundary. The local slope multiplier is exactly `(1 - s) + s * S'(t)`, so 14 puts the middle 80% of a band's rise in about 2 m of ground -- four cells of the mesher's finest rung, and further than that is a face the mesh cannot carry at any distance. `SCARP_MAX` (0.92) caps the blend because at 1 the tread is dead level and a dead-level bench reads as a machined terrace. The remap is non-separable and knows nothing of the lattice, so a face running diagonally does not zigzag.

Four gates keep it off. THE TEXEL-SCALE SLOPE past `SCARP_SLOPE` (50 deg, fading in over `SCARP_FEATHER` 12), read off a uniform cubic B-spline over the same 4x4 block rather than off the interpolating curve, because the question is how far the landform drops across a texel and not how steep the curve between two of them happens to be -- the same basis and the same argument as `crease.js`. Its support is four texels, so it reports a cliff's slope from up to 16 m away and the operator's reach is wider than the face by that much, which is what the sill in `SCARP_SLOPE` pays for. THE SHEERNESS FIELD: one octave at `SCARP_BROAD` (1100 m) choosing which faces are candidates at all, times one at `SCARP_PATCH` (180 m) making sheerness come and go ALONG a face, which is the "it should transition from sheer to slanted and back, not a whole face vertical" half of the ask. THE BAND IS TALLER THAN THE CLIFFS: 34 m is above the tallest step `cliffs.js` tables (26.2 m measured), so a tabled face meets one riser and not a flight. AND THE BANDS ARE CHOSEN ONE AT A TIME, which the rise alone cannot give, since a face 100 m tall crosses three bands however tall they are and three risers up one face is a staircase: `_pick(k) * (1 - _pick(k - 1))`, each band asking a field of its own -- continuous in x and z, decorrelated from its neighbours by an offset far larger than the feature size -- whether it stands up here, and standing down by as much as the band below it is standing. That is the owner's rule that a cliff is never immediately above or below another.

Per-band selection is safe because of a property of the remap: at a band boundary the input height IS an exact multiple of the rise past the phase, so the remapped height equals it and the displacement `s * (stepped - smooth)` is EXACTLY ZERO there, whatever `s` is. The blend may therefore step from band to band without tearing the surface. What it may not do is step WITHIN a band, which is why each band's own field has to vary continuously. The exact promise, which is what the gate asserts: a riser at full strength forces the bands above and below it to zero, and in between the two trade, so adjacent bands can never both be more than half up.

A RISER IS A NARROW BAND BY CONSTRUCTION -- treads wide, risers thin -- so a scatter of random points lands on a tread nearly every time and is blind to what the operator claims. Measured that way on the shipped v2 import the population gets LESS steep with scarp on (p50 57.8 -> 55.3 deg), which is true and beside the point. The instrument is a fall-line walk. On the v3 island over 74563 dry points, scarp 0 -> 1: the 0.5 m slope median is unchanged at 25.1 deg and p90 hardly moves (45.5 -> 45.3), while the max goes 80.6 -> 87.3 and the share at or past 85 deg goes 0.0000% -> 0.0201%; walking 40 m down the fall line from 1130 steep points, the steepest 0.5 m cell on the way has median 53.3 -> 53.4 -- most faces stay slanted, which is the point -- p90 67.4 -> 70.1, max 80.6 -> 88.6, past 80 deg 0.2% -> 4.1% and past 85 deg 0.0% -> 1.9%.

What `scripts/check-v2-field.mjs` holds, on the shipped import, as the `scarp` knob's five probe checks: gentle ground's RECONSTRUCTION is bit-identical (0 of 1685 sites under 30 deg move), so it is a cliff operator and not a terrace; the COMPOSED field there moves by centimetres and not by nothing (worst 0.082 m), which is a weaker statement and cannot be made as an identity, because the detail stack is not point-local and a riser bleeds onto gentle ground a few metres away; it does reach the steep ground (40.7% of 150 sites past 50 deg); it draws a landform face the plain bicubic cannot (fall line from those sites: p90 73.7 -> 82.9 deg, max 86.6 -> 89.0, past 84 deg on 3 -> 14); and no riser stands directly above another (0 of 80000 adjacent band pairs both past half, the worst pair summing to 1.000, tight to the algebra). Two traps are written into that probe. The stratum is the COARSE field's own slope, not the composed slope over a stride, which is not a proxy for it. And an absolute-angle assertion against `heightAt` is the wrong bar altogether: on the 3x-stretched shipped import the plain bicubic reconstruction already reaches 84.6 deg over a 0.5 m cell and the composed field about 88 with every knob off.

Not done here: the operator knows nothing of the biomes -- its sheerness field is noise, so canyon rock is no likelier to stand sheer than forest, where `cliffs.js` one step upstream is entirely class-driven -- and a sheer face still has no scree at its foot.

### The elevation pyramid -- what stays in memory (built)

`src/v3/tiles.js` (the geometry), `src/v3/pager.js` (the policy), a `tiles` store in `src/v3/store.js`, and on the v2 side `Heightmap.attachTiles`, `TerrainV2.putTile/dropTile` and the worker's `tile`/`untile` messages. The whole of it is the answer to one number: widening the image to 2 m made the field 4097² Float32, 64 MB, and the class grid 16 MB, and `terrain-v2.js` hands every mesh worker its own copy of both -- so three holders, this page for collision and the two meshers, were carrying 240 MB of height map. A Quest browser tab does not have it to spend. Paged, the same three hold 6.1 MB each, 18.3 MB in all, and the gate counts it rather than asserting it.

THERE IS ONE FIELD, STORED TWICE, AT TWO PITCHES. That sentence is the architecture, and it was chosen over the alternative that compresses better (the seed plus the drain's delta, 0.31 MB at 1025², measured above) because a field that has to be reconstructed before it can be read is harder to hold in the head than a field that is simply there at two resolutions. The base is the finished 2 m field decimated by 4 to 1025² at 8 m, 4.0 MB, and it is resident always. The 2 m texels it came from go to IndexedDB as 1024 tiles of 256 m, and `TilePager` keeps the handful within reach of the eye. Both halves are the DRAINED field, after the hydrology and the tabling: decimate before the drain and a rim dish would be cut inside the paged disc and absent outside it, and the ground would heave as tiles paged in.

REGISTRATION IS THE INVARIANT AND IT IS EXACT. `Heightmap` puts texel 0 on the -X/-Z world corner, so coarse texel `c` has to land on fine texel `4c` or the two grids describe ground offset from each other by a fraction of a texel, which reads as the whole island sliding as a tile arrives. A box average over each 4x4 block does exactly that, shifting by 1.5 fine texels; a symmetric filter centred on the kept texel does not. So `decimate` runs a separable binomial 5-tap `[1 4 6 4 1] / 16` with the same edge clamp `Heightmap._tap` uses, `(n - 1)` divides by 4 so both world edges survive, and the gate proves the registration on a plane, where a correct filter reproduces the input exactly and a shifted one cannot. What the filter removes is the 8 m and 16 m jitter rungs and the terrain's own curvature at those lags: 0.26 m rms against the fine field at the texels they share, 31.4 m at the single worst texel on seed 20260824.

THE DISC IS A SUPERSET OF WHAT SELECTION CAN ASK FOR, which is what keeps the pager from having to negotiate with the quadtree at all. `selectNodes` refines while `size / CHUNK_RES > range * tan(triDeg)`, so `reach = BASE_CELL / tan(triDeg)` is the range past which no node ever splits below a base texel -- 153 m at the desktop knob of 3.0, 114 m at the 4.0 an XR route takes. The radius is `reach` plus the diagonal of the coarsest node that can refine to a sub-base cell, `BASE_CELL * CHUNK_RES` = 128 m on a side, 181 m across: 334 m at 3.0, 295 m at 4.0, 563 m at `MIN_TRI_DEG`. THE PARENT'S DIAGONAL, not the child's, and check-v3's disc section is what caught that -- `range` is measured to a node's NEAREST corner, so the test that admitted a node was read at its parent, and every texel under that parent including the far child's far corner is inside `reach + diag(parent)`. Bounding the child understates the disc by half a parent diagonal, which shows up as a chunk 300 m out reading the base on one side of a tile line and the fine texels on the other. The gate runs the real `selectNodes` over a spread of cameras at four settings of the knob, with node bounds left null because `nodeRange` without them ignores the camera's height and is therefore the most refinement any camera can provoke, and asserts no sub-base vertex falls outside the radius. The radius is derived from `LOD.triDeg` on every update and never written down, because the knob is a live slider.

What the disc costs: 14 tiles at 3.0 and 12 at 4.0, under 1 MB, worst of the 1089 eye offsets the gate sweeps inside a tile; 28 at `MIN_TRI_DEG`. The memory arithmetic is done against the enclosing square instead -- 16, and 36 at `MIN_TRI_DEG`, 2.4 MB -- because a budget should not be checked against a number that moves with where in a tile the eye stands. It is deliberately wider than the reach and the 181 m of margin is the prefetch -- a tile is admitted most of a tile's width before anything over it can refine past the base, so in steady walking a tile is resident before a chunk wants it and nothing meshes coarse and then re-meshes. At boot and after a teleport the IndexedDB read is simply slower than the first selection, which is why `putTile` invalidates the tile's rect in both meshers: the disc is a superset in SPACE, not in time. `dropTile` deliberately does not invalidate, because a chunk outside the disc cannot be drawing finer than the base anyway.

THE CLASS GRID IS COARSENED TO MATCH, 16 MB to 1.0 MB, by NEAREST at the same registration -- a class is a label and the mean of `arctic` and `desert` is not a biome. This is not a compromise: `GroundTint` reads the grid bilinearly over the palette, so a border is a ramp one texel wide, and at 2 m that ramp was sharper than the design ever asked for. The classes are not paged at all; there is no 2 m class grid at runtime.

THE THREE HOLDERS ARE STILL THREE. `Heightmap.attachTiles` is the one choke point every reader of the field already goes through, so collision, the mesher, the scatter and the water see the same surface, which is what `relief.js` exists to protect -- and `view()` carries the store forward, because with a `detail` term `V2Height._attachReconstruction` samples through a view and a view without the tiles would put the collision field on the 8 m base while the mesher drew 2 m. What is NOT done is making the three share one buffer: a `SharedArrayBuffer` would take the 18.3 MB to about 6.1 MB, which was worth 134 MB before the paging and is worth 12 MB after it, and it needs COOP/COEP on the whole site (every cross-origin subresource then needs CORP). That is a page-wide decision for a 12 MB return and it is deliberately not taken here. `relief.erode` and a tiled heightmap now THROW together rather than quietly disagreeing: the relaxation makes a different surface from the one the tiles were cut from.

The cache slot is the 4.0 MB base record plus 69 MB of tiles in their own IndexedDB store, one record each, because the point is to read one tile without deserialising the other 1023. The tiles are written BEFORE the island record, so a tab killed mid-bake leaves tiles with no record -- a plain cache miss -- rather than a record whose detail is gone, and `load()` verifies the tile count on every hit for the same reason: a storage eviction that took one store and not the other would otherwise stand up a world that is silently 8 m everywhere. `/terrain-v3-map` is the one caller that still wants the whole 2 m field, since it paints every texel of it, and it asks for it with `fine: true`: the worker transfers the fine arrays alongside the base, and `store.js` strips them before the record is cached, so the field that must not be resident on the 3D route is not in the cache for it to find.

The `memory` table in /terrain-v3's sidebar reads it live: the base field and the class grid with their pitches, the resident tiles with how many the disc wants and how wide it is, those three times the three holders, the tile count on disk, and `performance.memory.usedJSHeapSize`, which COUNTS THIS THREAD ONLY -- not the mesh workers, not the GPU -- so it is a floor and a trend, and reads `--` on Firefox and Safari rather than a zero that would look like good news. A tile the store expected and IndexedDB did not have shows as a `MISSING` row and logs, because the symptom otherwise is one 256 m square of quietly coarse ground.

### Step E -- lock (not built)

The generated field is baked to `public/world-v3/` the way `make-heightmap.mjs` bakes the shipped one, the doc beside it, and the boot path becomes a load. The generator stays as the gate's reference.

### Phase 2 -- populate (not built)

Per-biome prop rules through the seam `BiomeField` already leaves in `src/v2/layers/biome.js`.

## Shared files this touches
