# 31. Terrain v3 -- the generated island

The route is `/terrain-v3`, the map instrument `/terrain-v3-map`, the code `src/v3/`, the gate `scripts/check-v3.mjs`. This section is the plan and, as each step lands, the record of what was built; a step that is not yet built is marked so.

## What v3 is

v3 is a new AUTHOR OF THE COARSE FIELD for the v2 engine, not a new renderer. v2 (§18) draws whatever 1025² Float32 heightmap at 8 m a texel it is handed through `Heightmap.fromRaw` (the leafkin village room is the precedent), puts its own procedural detail from 8 m down to 10 cm on top of it, and reads lakes, rivers and roads out of a layers document. v3 generates that heightmap and that document from a seed, in the browser, at boot. Nothing in `src/v2/terrain/` is forked.

The island: roughly circular, ocean all round it running out to the box edge, a coast with bays and headlands of many sizes, hills rising inward to a jagged snowy massif at the centre, one or two broad arms off the massif, and inland valleys wide enough to hold lakes -- lakes between mountains and low-lying ones, not only lakes that open on the sea.

## The two representations (§18's rule, applied)

Stored is small and parametric; runtime is baked and O(1) to read.

| Thing | Stored | Runtime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The ground | seed + algorithm version | 1025² Float32 metres, cached in IndexedDB |
| Biomes | boundary polygons (`biomes[]` in the v3 record; the v2 doc's `serialize()` drops keys it does not know, so they move into the doc when the doc learns them) | class grid rasterised from the polygons in the worker, shipped to the mesh workers beside the heightmap |
| Rivers | doc `rivers[]` polylines, found from the eroded grid | v2's A*-routed splines (`route.js`), as today |
| Lakes | doc `lakes[]`, the sea one uncarved rectangle at y = 0 | v2 `WaterSurfaces`, as today |
| Props | nothing | scattered live from the grid and the biome class, as today |

The biome grid is not a texture: it is a `Uint8Array` the mesher reads per vertex (`GroundTint` in `src/v2/layers/ground.js`, bilinear over a per-class palette), and `shade()` in `chunk-mesh-v2.js` takes that colour as its grass base in place of the grass-to-scrub altitude lerp. Bit-identical to today when the grid is absent.

## Where the work runs

Everything runs at boot in a Web Worker, every layer of it -- macro shape, octaves, biomes, hydrology, rivers. It is cached in IndexedDB (`aurora-v3`, key `seed:algorithmVersion`) as the finished field plus the doc, so the second boot is a read; `?regen` throws the cache away and `?seed=N` picks the island. Nothing is pre-baked into `public/` until the algorithm is LOCKED (step E): a layer that is baked is a layer that cannot be revised from the page, and revising is the whole of the next few weeks. The same generator module runs in node for the gate and the PNG script -- `src/v3/` is three-free and DOM-free, the constraint that makes that possible.

The cost is one worker run per client per algorithm version. Desktop node measures shape, octaves and biomes at 1025² in about a second and the rain at 10.5 s; the eroded whole is budgeted at 15-40 s on a Quest 2, once, behind a progress line, and a headset run at three times the desktop's 13 s would blow it. If that budget is blown, the escape is to bake at lock time, not to move layers out of the boot path early.

## Scale

`WORLD_SIZE` stays 8192 m so nothing in v2 has to be told. 1025 texels at 8 m. The island's mean coast radius is 2.5 km, so the sea runs 1.2-1.7 km out from the coast to the box edge and keeps falling all the way. The generator owns the octaves from 512 m down to 8 m, seven of them; the 4 m rung and below is what v2's `detail.js` already supplies at runtime, calibrated against whatever coarse field it is given.

The known trap in that calibration: `calibrateRough` fits its curvature law over sites drawn from the whole grid, and 72% of this grid is sea floor. The sea floor carries the jitter at 30% of the massif's so the fit has nothing flat to be dragged down by; it lands at rough 0.0306, exponent 1.07, a finite fit the gate asserts, and `detail.js` is untouched.

## The steps

Each step is its own look on the map page and in 3D before the next goes on, because the lesson of §14 is that shape complaints must become numbers before they become code, and the number has to be read off the layer that is wrong, not off the sum.

### Step 0 -- skeleton and instruments (built)

`src/v3/island.js` is the field, `src/v3/generate.js` the pipeline (`generate({ seed, n, log, tune })` -> `{ v, seed, n, cell, height, meta, doc, biomes, ground, stats, tune, ms }`), `src/v3/paint.js` the hillshade and layer painters, `src/v3/worker.js` the worker, `src/v3/store.js` the IndexedDB cache. `/terrain-v3-map` runs the generator in the worker every time (and so is what fills the cache) and draws it as relief, elevation, slope, closed bowls, biomes and water with a hover readout and the stats; its sidebar carries a field per warp octave and per jitter octave (`tune`, metres; the URL carries them as `?warp=` and `?jitter=` and a `defaults` button clears them), and the island made from edited numbers is cached under the seed's own key, so `/terrain-v3` flies whatever the map page last made. `scripts/island-png.mjs` writes any layer to a PNG; `scripts/check-v3.mjs` asserts determinism, finiteness, land fraction, a summit near the centre, a sea floor that falls, an irregular coast, a doc that validates, the rg16 round trip, and that `V2Height` will stand on the field. `/terrain-v3` boots the v2 engine on the result behind the cache with the game's own `Player`: walking by default, space to fly and hold to climb, speed rising with height, double-tap to land; N (or the `+6h` button) skips the world clock six hours, since the room runs the day-night cycle. Its near plane steps with height above ground (0.1 m on foot, up to 8 m at 200 m up) so the shoreline stays crisp from altitude instead of z-fighting the sea; `window.__v3` exposes the rig, camera, look, player, height and clock for the console and the headless probe.

### Step A -- the cone (built)

`MACRO` in `island.js`. A circular cone standing in a sea that falls away, and nothing smaller than the coast's own features, so the map view is judging only whether the island's silhouette and its skirt are right.

1. **The warp.** Each texel's position is displaced by three octaves of simplex (1600 m / 150 m, 520 m / 45 m, 170 m / 15 m amplitude), and every later term reads the warped position. This is what puts bays and headlands of three sizes on a coast that would otherwise be a circle. Amplitude stays under a tenth of the wavelength: past that the warp folds the plane over itself, and every fold is a crease of terrain running inland.
2. **The coast radius.** `R(theta) = R0 * (1 + a1 n1(theta) + a2 n2(theta))`, noise sampled on a circle so it is periodic by construction: a low-order term at +-32% for the big peninsulas and the deep bays, a higher-order term at +-9% for the middling ones. A peninsula is an arm: because height is a function of `r / R(theta)`, ground that reaches further out reaches further out AT HEIGHT, and the ridge runs down it on its own. The same rule would run every middling bay inward as a valley converging on the summit, so the higher-order term is skin-deep: full from 0.9 R0 outward, gone inside 0.35 R0.
3. **The profile.** `d = r / R(theta)`, 0 at the centre and 1 at the coast. Land is `H (1 - d)^1.2` with H = 450 m: nearly a straight cone, so the slope the cone contributes is much the same at the coast as under the massif and the hills the jitter puts on it read alike everywhere (at an exponent of 1.7 the interior was a steep rough massif over a flat skirt, which is not the island wanted). The centre is not a point: the top 8% of the cone is capped by a tangent parabola, flat at the top, so the pedestal tops out at 414 m as a massif and not a spike. Below 0.12 H the profile is bent toward a quadratic apron that keeps 15% of the cone's slope at the waterline, so the last few hundred metres before the shore are lowland but never a plane the sea can fight. Sea is a shelf 6 m under reached by a 24 m drop, 120 m wide, then a slope of 1:9 that keeps falling to the box edge: -229 m on average along the edge, deeper in the corners, against an encoding floor of -600 m (rg16 quantum 2.4 cm).

Cliff coasts were tried as sectors of the circle where the cone is held up at 110 m to the waterline and dropped to the shelf across 32 m, and rejected: a wall that long and that even along the coast's own curve is not a cliff. If the island is to have cliffs they will have to come from something the ground does -- a fault, or what step D's carving leaves standing -- not from the coast's parameterisation.

Instruments: land fraction, summit height and offset from the centre, coast irregularity (the shoreline's length against the circumference of the circle with the island's area), the relief-by-scale table (rms of what a box blur at 32, 128, 512 and 2048 m removes over land), and the closed bowls (what a priority flood would pond over a metre deep, grouped, over 2 ha).

### Step B -- the jitter (built)

`JITTER` in `island.js`. Seven octaves of value noise, one per scale from 512 m down to the 8 m texel, halving each time. An octave is a square lattice of nodes at that spacing; every node is moved up or down by a random amount up to the octave's entry in `amps` (128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2 m: a quarter of the spacing each, and the numbers the map page's jitter fields edit); the ground between nodes is a smoothstep between the four corners; and the octaves are added, so each refines the one before it. A node's move is a hash of its lattice coordinates and the seed, so the same seed always gives the same island and any texel can be asked for on its own. Two details keep the lattice from showing: every octave's lattice is turned by the golden angle from the last so no two share axes, and the interpolation is a smoothstep rather than a straight lerp, because a lerp is continuous but its slope is not, and the lattice lines read as creases across every hillside (`interp: 'linear'` is kept so this can be seen).

Why 512 m and not the whole box: the recipe cannot start at 4096 m, since +-1024 m of jitter at that scale is the island, not a refinement of it, and 25% at every scale from there down sums to a slope of about 30 degrees everywhere. The cone owns the shape above 512 m; the jitter owns everything under it.

The jitter is the same everywhere inland, so a region by the coast is as hilly as one under the massif. It eases only in the last 20% of the radius (`shore.band`), down to 30% of itself at the waterline and over the sea (`shore.floor`), because full jitter there is +-128 m on a 54 m apron and shreds the coast into islets; 30% still cuts the apron into coves and skerries, which is most of the coastline's character. Scaling the jitter by height instead (full on the summit, 30% at the shore) was tried and rejected: with the steeper profile it made the interior rough and the lowlands smooth, an island steep in the middle and flat toward its shores.

Seed 20260824 measures: land 29.0% (19.5 km2), summit 502 m 419 m off the centre, coast 83 km at 4.17x its circle, sea floor -84 m a kilometre out and -229 m at the box edge, 13 bowls over 2 ha ponding 1.20 km2 (largest 0.46 km2, 121 m deep), relief 4.2 / 14.6 / 45.4 / 118.5 m at 32 / 128 / 512 / 2048 m. The bowls are measured on the raw field, before step D: they are what full jitter to the coast leaves in ground with no drainage yet, and step D chooses its lakes among them and drains the rest. `scripts/island-png.mjs --jitter interp=linear` renders an experiment without touching the cache.

### Step C -- climate and biomes (built)

`src/v3/biomes.js`, run in the worker after the octaves, about 0.2 s. Temperature is a base less a latitude gradient across the box (colder north) less a lapse of 1 per 1300 m, plus a little noise. Moisture is a westerly marched texel by texel across each row: it evaporates over the sea, rains out in proportion to the ground rising under it, dries a little every texel, and the rain that falls is box-blurred 300 m; to that is added the remaining humidity and a coast term (the sea mask blurred 500 m). So the west is wet and forested, the lee of the massif is plains, and the coast is damper than the interior. The seven classes are thresholds on the pair set at the LAND'S OWN QUANTILES, so each holds a seventh of the land whatever the seed: the coldest seventh is arctic; the next two sevenths temperate, split at their median moisture into plains and forest; the warmest four sevenths hot, split at their moisture quartiles into desert, canyon, jungle and swamp. The snow line in the doc is the height a seventh of the land lies above, so v2's snow and the arctic class roughly agree. The lookup is noise-warped (70 m at 260 m) so no border is smooth, and patches under 6 ha are given to their neighbours.

The classes are then TRACED: marching squares per class, each loop simplified to 10 m by Douglas-Peucker, and the polygons (world metres, to the decimetre) are what the v3 record stores. The grid is rebuilt from them by scanline fill in class order with the slivers the simplification leaves taken by the nearest painted texel; the gate asserts the rebuilt grid agrees with the traced one on more than 98% of texels (99.5% on seed 20260824) and that the cached grid IS the rasterisation of the cached polygons. Each class gets a flat distinct ground colour through `GroundTint` and `shade()` until the props exist to tell them apart; the map page's biomes layer paints the same palette over the hillshade.

Seed 20260824 after the warp and the despeckle: every class between 13.6% and 14.9% of the land, the snow line at 203 m; forest and swamp on the wet west, desert and canyon in the massif's lee to the east, jungle across the hot south, arctic on the peaks and the cold north. `CLIMATE` shapes WHERE each class falls; the shares it cannot change.

### Step D -- hydrology (built)

`src/v3/erosion.js` and `src/v3/hydrology.js`, run in the worker after the biomes on the raw field, about 12 s of the pipeline's 13 s. The water is not routed and then carved along its routes: it is thrown at the land as droplets and the land is what they leave. Phase A's toolkit (`src/sim/hydrology.js`: priority flood, D8 routing, accumulation; `selectLakes` from `phase-a.js`) reads the result. The rule the gate asserts is unchanged: every drop of rain on land has a path that never climbs to the sea, a lake or another river, and every lake is a bowl.

1. **Rain** (`erosion.js`, `EROSION`). `droplets` (300000, about one per land cell) units of water land on random land cells. Each walks cell to steepest cell until it reaches the sea or `maxSteps` run out, reading the slope under its own feet and nothing else, so where many agree a valley forms and its tributaries branch off it on their own. A droplet carries sediment up to a capacity that grows with the drop it just took (never read under `minSlope`), its speed and the water left in it; under capacity it cuts `erode` (0.05) of the difference, times the biome's yield (`byBiome`: canyon 2.5, desert 1.6, jungle 1.3, forest and plains 1, arctic 0.6, swamp 0.3), never more than the drop itself so it can make no pit and never more than `maxCut` (1 m) in a step; over capacity it lays `deposit` (0.1) of the surplus where it stands. THE GROOVE: the cut is spread over a brush of `radius` (5) cells with the weight falling off from the centre, so a groove is a V cut into the slopes beside it, 88 m wide at the ground, and not a slot one texel wide (radius 3 left the trunks as slots). THE BEND: eight headings on a grid make ruled lines, so the heading just taken is scored at `straight` (0.5) of its slope against the other seven and a droplet holds it only where it is more than twice the steepest turn -- a fall line stays a fall line, an even slope is walked as a shallow zigzag, and the grooves that gather out of them bend. THE BOWL: a droplet that finds no downhill has run into a closed bowl, and what it walks on there is the water that would stand in it: a priority flood, re-run every `batch` (30000) droplets, gives every ponded cell the level of the bowl's spill and the way to it, so a droplet crossing a bowl walks the flat to the rim and grooves the far side, its sediment settling on the floor and never over the water. The bowl drains as its outlet cuts and fills as its floor rises, which is what a lake does with time; the outlets are where the deepest cuts are (94 m). This is the "search upward until a downhill appears" done once per batch for every bowl at once rather than once per droplet.
2. **Cliffs** (`cliffs.js`, `CLIFFS`). The rain leaves no cliffs and thresholding cannot find any: over the 304592 land texels the steepest texel-to-texel drop is 24 deg at the median and 45 at p90, and 0.216% fall more than two texel-distances. So they are made. A ladder of rungs `step` metres apart cuts the slope into bands, and inside a band a texel's height is remapped through a two-piece ramp: a FACE over the lowest `riser` of the band taking `snap` of its fall, and a SHELF over the rest carrying the remaining `1 - snap` gently up to the rung above. The face's grade is the old grade times `snap / riser`, the shelf's the old grade times `(1 - snap) / (1 - riser)`, and both ends of the ramp are fixed points, so the ladder never drifts. Three rules shape it. THE SHELF SITS ABOVE ITS FACE, because a shelf below one can only be cut down to, and that cut is a trench -- the ground under the face was never touched, so the shelf sits in a hollow, and where the weight varies from texel to texel the hollow becomes a run of dishes. Lifting instead makes `f >= frac` everywhere and the pass never lowers a texel (it throws if it computes a move that would); what lies under a face is the untouched slope the droplets left, a real downhill grade, shy of flat, no trench. THE LADDER IS SPARSE: a rung carries a face only when its hash stands above both its neighbours', about one rung in three and never two in a row, so there is always a whole band of untouched slope between one face and the next and a hillside is not a staircase. Integer hash and not noise, because what is wanted between neighbouring rungs is independence, not continuity. THE HEIGHT WANDERS: `snap` is drawn per rung from a span the class sets, so one band puts half its fall in the face and leaves the shelf a walkable grade while the next puts all of it there and goes flat, and `roll` (0.25 of a step over `rollScale`, 240 m) slides the rungs up and down so a shelf tilts and a face grows and shrinks along its run. Reading `riser`: a face is `riser * step / tan(slope)` metres of ground, so under 8 m it lands inside one texel and the whole of `snap * step` falls between two of them -- the most the grid can hold, and a wall. Canyon (`step` 34, `riser` 0.11, `snap` 0.80-1) and desert (28, 0.13, 0.70-1) sit under the line and get walls with near-level tops; arctic (26, 0.30, 0.50-0.90), forest and jungle (21, 0.34, 0.45-0.85) and plains (17, 0.42, 0.50-0.85) sit over it and get a firm bank over a shelf that still falls away under the walker, which is the "45 degrees is fine here" half of the ask. THE BAND: a per-texel coin flip on steep ground gives salt and pepper, so the gate is one octave of simplex at `wavelength` (320 m) thresholded at the QUANTILE of that noise over each class's own steep ground -- `share` (canyon 0.85, desert 0.58, arctic 0.38, forest 0.30, jungle 0.24, plains 0.16, swamp 0) means exactly the fraction of that class's steep ground the band lets through, of which the sparse ladder moves about a third -- and `bandFeather` past the threshold tapers a band's ends off. THE PHASE: a second, slower noise at `phaseScale` (1100 m) offsets the ladder by up to a step, or every shelf on the island would bench at the same heights and read as a contour map. The slope gate (`minSlope` 26 deg, `slopeFeather` 6) is there to spare the flats, not to scale the effect: a wide feather leaves the 30-degree ground, which is most of the steep ground there is, tabled at a fifth strength and no cliff anywhere. Nothing under `minHeight` (6 m) tables, so the coast keeps its beaches. It runs after the rain, because the droplets' 5-cell brush would grind a scarp back into a slope, and before everything below it, so the lakes, the silt, the route and the rivers are all solved on the shape that will be drawn.
3. **Lakes** (`hydrology.js`, `LAKES`). The flood's bowls on the rained-on land (the sea's own bowls are excluded by reading the flood as the ground itself under the waterline) are scored by the widest disc of water over `minDepth` (1.5 m) in them, and the widest `keep` (12) at least `minWidth` (80 m) across are the lakes, each kept whole: every cell the flood wets round its pool, up to and including the spill, at the spill's level, so the river out of it leaves from the water. A lake has no dam and no carve: its shore is wherever the ground meets its level.
4. **Silt.** Every other bowl the rain left is raised to its spill. That is the one artificial move in the step and it shows: a silted bowl is a flat terrace at its rim's height (0.61 km2 on seed 20260824, 2.9 m mean, 36 m at the deepest), where a real one would be a marsh or a lake.
5. **Route.** Priority flood, D8 steepest descent with every lake cell sent along the flood's tree to the spill (steepest descent off the flat lets the cells beside the spill step over the rim on their own, and each is then a source, so one lake would let out three rivers a few cells apart), accumulation in cells. A cell outside the lakes that still ponds throws.
6. **Rivers.** A cell outside the sea and the lakes with `RIVERS.minCatchment` (5 ha) draining through it is a river cell. The network is walked from every mouth (a river cell whose receiver is not one) up its largest donor to a source, the other donors becoming tributaries whose last point is the trunk cell they join; a source whose receiver chain starts in a lake starts inside it. Cell centres are Douglas-Peucker simplified to `tolerance` (5 m) with the junction cells pinned, because a junction the simplifier drops leaves a tributary's mouth beside the trunk rather than on it and v2 cannot pin it, and with the cells either side of a fall of more than `pinDrop` (3 m) pinned too: the simplification is in PLAN and reads no elevation at all, so on a straight reach it would drop a cliff's lip and foot and leave v2 a chord its router is free to lay up to `tolerance` metres to the side, off the notch the water cut and onto the face beside it. Width is `widthAtMin` (1.2 m) times the square root of catchment over `minCatchment`, capped at `maxWidth` (14 m); a tributary under `minLength` (60 m) that nothing feeds is not drawn.
7. **The doc.** Each lake is a v2 lake record with `carve 0`: an ellipse fitted to its deep pool's second moments, grown until every pool cell is inside and scaled by `margin` (1.06), at the body's level, so v2 draws water wherever the ground inside the ellipse lies under the level. The pool and not the whole body, because a bowl's shallow arms stretch the ellipse over the ground beside it: fitted to the whole body the summit lake drew 2.9 ha of water in the hollows next to it, fitted to the pool 0.6 ha, and six of its shallow cells stay dry. The sea stays lake l1. Each river is a v2 river polyline `[x, z, w]`, source to mouth, which v2's `paths.js` re-routes over its coarse heightmap and pins into the lake or river it ends in.

Seed 20260824: 300000 droplets in 11.3 s, 136 cells each, 93% reaching the sea (5% stranded in a pit a deposit made since the last flood, 2% out of steps); the rain cut 4.2 m mean over 8.7 of the 19.5 km2 of land and laid 4.4 m over 1.1 km2, 2.35 m of cut per droplet spread over the brush (0.033 m on each of the 71 steps that cut); the tabling then lifted 1.10 km2 in 34 ms, 5.0 m mean and 26.2 m at the most, leaving 1.87% of the canyon's land over 70 deg against the forest's 0.08% and 6361 texels of face over 12 m in 1211 runs, the longest 258 texels; 11 lakes kept of 420 bowls, 0.62 km2 of water at levels from 9 to 430 m, the ellipses leaking 0.024 km2 and leaving 0.0003 km2 dry; 91 rivers totalling 32.9 km (1.7 km per km2, the longest 1.8 km), 44 into the sea, 13 into a lake, 10 out of one, none climbing, their beds 0.83 m under the ground they cross at the median and 1.60 at p90.

What the bend is worth, measured on the drainage the rain leaves (heading runs along every river cell's walk down): without it a run averages 2.45 cells and 17.2% of runs hold a heading 4 cells or more, 4.4% eight or more; at `straight` 0.5 that is 1.93 cells, 10.4% and 2.2%. It costs nothing -- the same 93% of droplets reach the sea, within a second of the same time. 0.25 is marginally straighter still (1.85 / 9.0 / 2.1) and 0 -- a heading the droplet may not repeat at all -- is worse on every count, including 87% to the sea, because a droplet forbidden its fall line walks off the valley it was in. What the bend does NOT do is meander: the rivers' sinuosity is 1.233 against 1.237 without it. It takes the ruled lines out at the texel, and the reaches that still read as straight are the ones under standing water, where a droplet follows the flood's way to the spill and not the slope: a third to a half of the river cells in a heading held 8 cells or more are on a pond or a silted flat.

What it replaced: least-cost breach channels cut from every bowl's floor through its rim, then twenty passes of implicit stream-power incision along the D8 routes. That was 3.5 s, but it cut only the channel cell, so every river was a trench one texel wide and as deep as the incision made it, and the routes were fixed before any carving, so nothing the water did could move them. The droplets are three times slower and a channel is wherever they agree to run.

b65b766 is the first island worth keeping: flown on `/terrain-v3`, its terrain was the first the owner was reasonably happy with, "at least as a starting point". The rain that made it landed in 8e5053a; the bend came after it. That is the baseline anything later is judged against -- a later change that makes the island less convincing than b65b766's is a regression, whatever its numbers say.

Rivers from the air. Flown over at a few hundred metres the network broke into dashes, and both faults were v2's. `WaterSurfaces.updateLod` was called from `src/v2/main.js` alone, so on this page every river vertex carried rung 0: the ribbon kept its fine index and the level it was solved at against the full-detail ground, while the terrain under it drew 64 and 128 m cells whose vertices stand on the banks, and the ground ate the water. It is called now from the v3 frame right after `terrain.update`, which both lifts each vertex by the rung drawn under it and swaps the ribbon to its coarse index -- over the reach probed, 98.2% of vertices took a rung and the drawn index fell from 179160 to 26346, the terrain's own cadence. What was left after that was a metre, and it is the ribbon's own doing: past `ribbon.js`'s `LOD_FINE` the strip keeps one sample in `LOD_STEP`, and the chord between two of them cuts the corner off a bend by enough to sink it into ground drawn at full detail. `RAISE_EYE_LIFT` is that metre -- a floor under the per-rung lift, ramped in from `RAISE_EYE` 30 m of plan distance and full by 60, inside the same sight-line gate as the rest, so from the bank a river still lies in its bed. It holds for the shipped rivers too. The v3 page's stub water is a `MeshBasicMaterial` and ignored all three attributes; it runs the lift block in `onBeforeCompile` now, the shipped one's without the waves, the flow or the lap.

Instruments: the map page's water layer (each lake's ground under its level inside its ellipse, each river stamped at its width, brightening as it widens) and `scripts/island-png.mjs --layer water`; the step D panel (droplets, seconds, cells each, fates; the cut's mean, area, deepest and deepest step; the deposit; the cliffs' area, time, mean and largest move, with each biome's share of its own steep ground beside it; the silt; river count, km, longest, mouths by kind) and a row per lake with its leak and its dry ground; the log line the worker writes. There are no cliff sliders: `tune` is two lists of amplitudes and the knobs worth turning here are a `{step, riser, share, strength}` per biome. The gate asserts over 80% of the droplets reach the sea, none leave the box and under 5% run out of steps; the cut's mean between 1 and 20 m over at least a fifth of the land and no step over `maxCut`; no tabling lift past the height a shelf can stand above the foot of its own band, its area between 0.5 km2 and a quarter of the land, each biome within 0.02 of its `share` of its own steep ground and swamp untouched, and on a clean 32-degree ramp of canyon with the band gate held open, not one texel of 9216 finishing below where it started and the nearest two faces 13 texels apart where a band is 6.8 -- the two promises the ramp's shape makes, no trench and no staircase; six times as much wall per 45-degree ramp in the canyon as in the forest, eight times as much canyon as forest over 70 deg, canyon holding four fifths of forest's share of the 40-50 deg band and a fifth more than its share of the flats, and the faces over 12 m lying in runs (over 2000 of them, the longest over 60 cells, more than 3 per connected run) rather than in specks; the silt under a tenth of the land; lake count between 3 and `keep`, every lake at least `minDepth` deep with its ellipse under a kilometre across, leaking under 5% of its area and leaving under 2% dry and nothing deeper than 4 m; at least 20 rivers and 10 km at 1 to 6 km per km2, at least 5 into the sea and no more out of lakes than there are lakes; and per river: no node higher than the one before it on the v3 field, every width within `[widthAtMin, maxWidth]`, and a mouth in the sea, inside a lake's footprint or within a cell of another river's node.

Not done, in the order it should be revisited: the silted bowls (a marsh class, or a shallow lake record, where a bowl is now a terrace); thermal erosion (the field has no talus and no angle of repose, and the cliffs now standing in it want a skirt of scree at their feet); meanders (the bend takes the ruled lines out at the texel but leaves sinuosity where it was, and a reach crossing a flat is still as straight as the flood's way to the spill); the lakes are ellipses in the doc because that is the record v2 has, so a pool whose shape is far from one leaks or is clipped (both are measured; a polygon lake record would end them); a steep tributary's water sheet steps down the grade from sample to sample, seen close up at a confluence, which is v2's rendering of any steep river and not the doc's; the 12 s are on the boot path until step E bakes the field, and a shipped island can simply be the cached result of one seed. And the cliffs stop at the ceiling the field itself sets: a step of any size between two texels is read back through Catmull-Rom, which smears it over 0.8 of a texel, so a face tops out near 78 deg over a run of 6.4 m however deep the drop is -- a wall to climb along, not one to stand under. A sheer face needs the field to carry the break rather than the height: an escarpment traced as a polyline, the ground on the low side dropped hard within a texel of it and the mesher told to leave that edge alone. That is what is left here.
