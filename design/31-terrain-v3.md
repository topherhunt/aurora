# 31. Terrain v3 -- the generated island

The route is `/terrain-v3`, the map instrument `/terrain-v3-map`, the code `src/v3/`, the gate `scripts/check-v3.mjs`. This section is the plan and, as each step lands, the record of what was built; a step that is not yet built is marked so.

## What v3 is

v3 is a new AUTHOR OF THE COARSE FIELD for the v2 engine, not a new renderer. v2 (§18) draws whatever 1025² Float32 heightmap at 8 m a texel it is handed through `Heightmap.fromRaw` (the leafkin village room is the precedent), puts its own procedural detail from 8 m down to 10 cm on top of it, and reads lakes, rivers and roads out of a layers document. v3 generates that heightmap and that document from a seed, in the browser, at boot. Nothing in `src/v2/terrain/` is forked.

The island: roughly circular, ocean all round it running out to the box edge, a coast with bays and headlands of many sizes, hills rising inward to a jagged snowy massif at the centre, one or two broad arms off the massif, and inland valleys wide enough to hold lakes -- lakes between mountains and low-lying ones, not only lakes that open on the sea.

## The two representations (§18's rule, applied)

Stored is small and parametric; runtime is baked and O(1) to read.

| Thing | Stored | Runtime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The ground | seed + algorithm version | 1025² Float32 metres, cached in IndexedDB |
| Biomes | boundary polygons (`biomes[]` in the v3 record; the v2 doc's `serialize()` drops keys it does not know, so they move into the doc when the doc learns them) | class grid rasterised from the polygons in the worker, shipped to the mesh workers beside the heightmap |
| Rivers | doc `rivers[]` polylines, found from the eroded grid | v2's A*-routed splines (`route.js`), as today |
| Lakes | doc `lakes[]`, the sea one uncarved rectangle at y = 0 | v2 `WaterSurfaces`, as today |
| Props | nothing | scattered live from the grid and the biome class, as today |

The biome grid is not a texture: it is a `Uint8Array` the mesher reads per vertex (`GroundTint` in `src/v2/layers/ground.js`, bilinear over a per-class palette), and `shade()` in `chunk-mesh-v2.js` takes that colour as its grass base in place of the grass-to-scrub altitude lerp. Bit-identical to today when the grid is absent.

## Where the work runs

Everything runs at boot in a Web Worker, every layer of it -- macro shape, octaves, biomes, hydrology, rivers. It is cached in IndexedDB (`aurora-v3`, key `seed:algorithmVersion:tune`) as the finished field plus the doc, so the second boot is a read; the tune's signature is in the key so that /terrain-v3's layer switches flip back to an island already made instead of overwriting the one it was being compared against; `?regen` throws the cache away and `?seed=N` picks the island. Nothing is pre-baked into `public/` until the algorithm is LOCKED (step E): a layer that is baked is a layer that cannot be revised from the page, and revising is the whole of the next few weeks. The same generator module runs in node for the gate and the PNG script -- `src/v3/` is three-free and DOM-free, the constraint that makes that possible.

The cost is one worker run per client per algorithm version. Desktop node measures shape, octaves and biomes at 1025² in about a second and the rain at 10.5 s; the eroded whole is budgeted at 15-40 s on a Quest 2, once, behind a progress line, and a headset run at three times the desktop's 13 s would blow it. If that budget is blown, the escape is to bake at lock time, not to move layers out of the boot path early.

## Scale

`WORLD_SIZE` stays 8192 m so nothing in v2 has to be told. 1025 texels at 8 m. The island's mean coast radius is 2.5 km, so the sea runs 1.2-1.7 km out from the coast to the box edge and keeps falling all the way. The generator owns the jitter octaves from 512 m down to 32 m, five of them -- four texels to the finest lattice node, which is where the cascade has to stop on an 8 m grid (step B). Everything below 32 m is the droplets', the cliffs' and v2's `detail.js`, the last calibrated at runtime against whatever coarse field it is given.

The known trap in that calibration: `calibrateRough` fits its curvature law over sites drawn from the whole grid, and 72% of this grid is sea floor. The sea floor carries the jitter at 30% of the massif's so the fit has nothing flat to be dragged down by; it lands at rough 0.0175, exponent 1.36, a finite fit the gate asserts, and `detail.js` is untouched. The exponent moves with the field: with the cliff ladder tabled in it read 0.0313 / 1.20, so the detail stack refits itself to whatever the layers below it leave.

## The steps

Each step is its own look on the map page and in 3D before the next goes on, because the lesson of §14 is that shape complaints must become numbers before they become code, and the number has to be read off the layer that is wrong, not off the sum.

### Step 0 -- skeleton and instruments (built)

`src/v3/island.js` is the field, `src/v3/generate.js` the pipeline (`generate({ seed, n, log, tune })` -> `{ v, seed, n, cell, height, meta, doc, biomes, ground, stats, tune, ms }`), `src/v3/paint.js` the hillshade and layer painters, `src/v3/worker.js` the worker, `src/v3/store.js` the IndexedDB cache. `/terrain-v3-map` runs the generator in the worker every time (and so is what fills the cache) and draws it as relief, elevation, slope, closed bowls, biomes and water with a hover readout and the stats; its sidebar carries a field per warp octave and per jitter octave (`tune`, metres; the URL carries them as `?warp=` and `?jitter=` and a `defaults` button clears them), and the island made from edited numbers is cached under the seed's own key, so `/terrain-v3` flies whatever the map page last made. `scripts/island-png.mjs` writes any layer to a PNG; `scripts/check-v3.mjs` asserts determinism, finiteness, land fraction, a summit near the centre, a sea floor that falls, an irregular coast, a doc that validates, the rg16 round trip, and that `V2Height` will stand on the field. `/terrain-v3` boots the v2 engine on the result behind the cache with the game's own `Player`: walking by default, space to fly and hold to climb, speed rising with height, double-tap to land. Its sidebar carries a checkbox per layer -- one per jitter octave, one for the ridge, one for the hydrology, one for the cliff tabling, and C for the read-time scarp -- and unticking one regenerates the island without it and stands the world back up where you were, so a layer can be judged by its absence; each combination is its own cache slot, so going back is instant, and `regen` throws the current slot away for when the algorithm rather than a switch has changed. The sun is held at noon there: a moving sky is a distraction from the shape. Its near plane steps with height above ground (0.1 m on foot, up to 8 m at 200 m up) so the shoreline stays crisp from altitude instead of z-fighting the sea; `window.__v3` exposes the rig, camera, look, player, height and clock for the console and the headless probe.

### Step A -- the cone (built)

`MACRO` in `island.js`. A circular cone standing in a sea that falls away, and nothing smaller than the coast's own features, so the map view is judging only whether the island's silhouette and its skirt are right.

1. **The warp.** Each texel's position is displaced by three octaves of simplex (1600 m / 150 m, 520 m / 45 m, 170 m / 15 m amplitude), and every later term reads the warped position. This is what puts bays and headlands of three sizes on a coast that would otherwise be a circle. Amplitude stays under a tenth of the wavelength: past that the warp folds the plane over itself, and every fold is a crease of terrain running inland.
2. **The coast radius.** `R(theta) = R0 * (1 + a1 n1(theta) + a2 n2(theta))`, noise sampled on a circle so it is periodic by construction: a low-order term at +-32% for the big peninsulas and the deep bays, a higher-order term at +-9% for the middling ones. A peninsula is an arm: because height is a function of `r / R(theta)`, ground that reaches further out reaches further out AT HEIGHT, and the ridge runs down it on its own. The same rule would run every middling bay inward as a valley converging on the summit, so the higher-order term is skin-deep: full from 0.9 R0 outward, gone inside 0.35 R0.
3. **The profile.** `d = r / R(theta)`, 0 at the centre and 1 at the coast. Land is `H (1 - d)^1.2` with H = 450 m: nearly a straight cone, so the slope the cone contributes is much the same at the coast as under the massif and the hills the jitter puts on it read alike everywhere (at an exponent of 1.7 the interior was a steep rough massif over a flat skirt, which is not the island wanted). The centre is not a point: the top 8% of the cone is capped by a tangent parabola, flat at the top, so the pedestal tops out at 414 m as a massif and not a spike. Below 0.12 H the profile is bent toward a quadratic apron that keeps 15% of the cone's slope at the waterline, so the last few hundred metres before the shore are lowland but never a plane the sea can fight. Sea is a shelf 6 m under reached by a 24 m drop, 120 m wide, then a slope of 1:9 that keeps falling to the box edge: -229 m on average along the edge, deeper in the corners, against an encoding floor of -600 m (rg16 quantum 2.4 cm).

Cliff coasts were tried as sectors of the circle where the cone is held up at 110 m to the waterline and dropped to the shelf across 32 m, and rejected: a wall that long and that even along the coast's own curve is not a cliff. If the island is to have cliffs they will have to come from something the ground does -- a fault, or what step D's carving leaves standing -- not from the coast's parameterisation.

Instruments: land fraction, summit height and offset from the centre, coast irregularity (the shoreline's length against the circumference of the circle with the island's area), the relief-by-scale table (rms of what a box blur at 32, 128, 512 and 2048 m removes over land), and the closed bowls (what a priority flood would pond over a metre deep, grouped, over 2 ha).

### Step B -- the jitter (built)

`JITTER` in `island.js`. Five octaves of value noise, one per scale from 512 m down to 32 m, halving each time. An octave is a square lattice of nodes at that spacing; every node is moved up or down by a random amount up to the octave's entry in `amps` (128, 64, 32, 16, 8 m: a quarter of the spacing each, and the numbers the map page's jitter fields edit); the ground between nodes is a smoothstep between the four corners; and the octaves are added, so each refines the one before it. A node's move is a hash of its lattice coordinates and the seed, so the same seed always gives the same island and any texel can be asked for on its own. Two details keep the lattice from showing: every octave's lattice is turned by the golden angle from the last so no two share axes, and the interpolation is a smoothstep rather than a straight lerp, because a lerp is continuous but its slope is not, and the lattice lines read as creases across every hillside (`interp: 'linear'` is kept so this can be seen).

Why 512 m and not the whole box: the recipe cannot start at 4096 m, since +-1024 m of jitter at that scale is the island, not a refinement of it, and 25% at every scale from there down sums to a slope of about 30 degrees everywhere. The cone owns the shape above 512 m; the jitter owns everything under it.

Why it stops at 32 m and not at the texel: the field is rasterised at 8 m, and a lattice node is an independent random number, so a 16 m lattice puts one node under every second texel -- the Nyquist period exactly, which samples as a two-texel sawtooth -- and an 8 m lattice puts one under every texel, which is white noise, with the octave's golden-angle rotation beating against the texel grid as moire. Both were in the cascade and both came back through the bicubic as a row of identical humps marching along every crest at a fixed spacing: grain, not landform. They doubled the field's curvature and its slope reversals (10.5% of texels to 22.2%) for 0.3 m on the mean step. So the finest octave is 32 m, four texels to a node, and the constructor throws if `amps` is lengthened past that. This is a limit of THIS GRID, not of the recipe: relief between 32 m and the metre is real and wanted, and it comes from the layers that can place it where the ground gives a reason for it -- the droplets, the cliffs, and v2's `detail.js`, which invents everything under the image's own resolution at read time.

The jitter is the same everywhere inland, so a region by the coast is as hilly as one under the massif. It eases only in the last 20% of the radius (`shore.band`), down to 30% of itself at the waterline and over the sea (`shore.floor`), because full jitter there is +-128 m on a 54 m apron and shreds the coast into islets; 30% still cuts the apron into coves and skerries, which is most of the coastline's character. Scaling the jitter by height instead (full on the summit, 30% at the shore) was tried and rejected: with the steeper profile it made the interior rough and the lowlands smooth, an island steep in the middle and flat toward its shores.

Seed 20260824 measures: land 29.0% (19.5 km2), summit 502 m 419 m off the centre, coast 83 km at 4.17x its circle, sea floor -84 m a kilometre out and -229 m at the box edge, 13 bowls over 2 ha ponding 1.20 km2 (largest 0.46 km2, 121 m deep), relief 4.2 / 14.6 / 45.4 / 118.5 m at 32 / 128 / 512 / 2048 m. The bowls are measured on the raw field, before step D: they are what full jitter to the coast leaves in ground with no drainage yet, and step D chooses its lakes among them and drains the rest. `scripts/island-png.mjs --jitter interp=linear` renders an experiment without touching the cache.

### Step C -- climate and biomes (built)

`src/v3/biomes.js`, run in the worker after the octaves, about 0.2 s. Temperature is a base less a latitude gradient across the box (colder north) less a lapse of 1 per 1300 m, plus a little noise. Moisture is a westerly marched texel by texel across each row: it evaporates over the sea, rains out in proportion to the ground rising under it, dries a little every texel, and the rain that falls is box-blurred 300 m; to that is added the remaining humidity and a coast term (the sea mask blurred 500 m). So the west is wet and forested, the lee of the massif is plains, and the coast is damper than the interior. The seven classes are thresholds on the pair set at the LAND'S OWN QUANTILES, so each holds a seventh of the land whatever the seed: the coldest seventh is arctic; the next two sevenths temperate, split at their median moisture into plains and forest; the warmest four sevenths hot, split at their moisture quartiles into desert, canyon, jungle and swamp. The snow line in the doc is the height a seventh of the land lies above, so v2's snow and the arctic class roughly agree. The lookup is noise-warped (70 m at 260 m) so no border is smooth, and patches under 6 ha are given to their neighbours.

The classes are then TRACED: marching squares per class, each loop simplified to 10 m by Douglas-Peucker, and the polygons (world metres, to the decimetre) are what the v3 record stores. The grid is rebuilt from them by scanline fill in class order with the slivers the simplification leaves taken by the nearest painted texel; the gate asserts the rebuilt grid agrees with the traced one on more than 98% of texels (99.5% on seed 20260824) and that the cached grid IS the rasterisation of the cached polygons. Each class gets a flat distinct ground colour through `GroundTint` and `shade()` until the props exist to tell them apart; the map page's biomes layer paints the same palette over the hillshade.

Seed 20260824 after the warp and the despeckle: every class between 13.8% and 15.0% of the land, the snow line at 299 m with 14.3% of the land above it; forest and swamp on the wet west, desert and canyon in the massif's lee to the east, jungle across the hot south, arctic on the peaks and the cold north. `CLIMATE` shapes WHERE each class falls; the shares it cannot change.

### Step D -- hydrology (built)

`src/v3/erosion.js` and `src/v3/hydrology.js`, run in the worker after the biomes on the raw field, about 9 s of the pipeline's 10 s. The water is not routed and then carved along its routes: it is thrown at the land as droplets and the land is what they leave. Phase A's toolkit (`src/sim/hydrology.js`: priority flood, D8 routing, accumulation; `selectLakes` from `phase-a.js`) reads the result. The rule the gate asserts is unchanged: every drop of rain on land has a path that never climbs to the sea, a lake or another river, and every lake is a bowl.

1. **Rain** (`erosion.js`, `EROSION`). `droplets` (300000, about one per land cell) units of water land on random land cells. Each walks downhill cell to cell until it reaches the sea or `maxSteps` run out, reading the ground under its own feet and nothing else, so where many agree a valley forms and its tributaries branch off it on their own. A droplet carries sediment up to a capacity that grows with the drop it just took (never read under `minSlope`), its speed and the water left in it; under capacity it cuts `erode` (0.05) of the difference, times the biome's yield (`byBiome`: canyon 2.5, desert 1.6, jungle 1.3, forest and plains 1, arctic 0.6, swamp 0.3), never more than the drop itself so it can make no pit and never more than `maxCut` (1 m) in a step; over capacity it lays `deposit` (0.1) of the surplus where it stands. THE GROOVE: the cut is spread over a brush of `radius` (5) cells with the weight falling off from the centre, so a groove is a V cut into the slopes beside it, 88 m wide at the ground, and not a slot one texel wide (radius 3 left the trunks as slots). THE WALK BENDS, in three terms, because a step to the steepest of eight neighbours can only ever draw one of eight bearings and a valley gathered out of a thousand such tracks is a straightedge. THE HEADING is a continuous unit vector, turned toward the downhill gradient by `1 - inertia` (0.6 held) each step, and the droplet steps to the lower neighbour that AGREES with it rather than to the lowest: which of them is taken is now decided by a quantity that varies smoothly, so a run reads N, NNE, N, NNE as a curve, and the droplet holds a bend past its apex instead of turning the instant the fall line does. THE SWIRL (`swirl` 0.9 against the unit gradient, turning over `swirlScale` 260 m) adds a slow, spatially coherent nudge baked once per run. Coherent is the whole of it: a per-step jitter averages out over a thousand droplets and leaves the groove exactly where it was, while a nudge that every droplet crossing one hillside feels the SAME way bends all their tracks together and lays the groove down curved. It is also the only one of the three that can bend a gorge, because once a channel is cut its bed lies so far under the cells beside it that the weights collapse onto the bed and the walk is deterministic again -- a steep valley meanders only if it was laid down meandering. THE DRAW makes the step a roulette rather than a win, because the other two are smooth in space and therefore turn every droplet on a hillside the same way, cutting a straight slash a few texels wide: each lower neighbour's weight is its own slope times `stray` (0.5) plus the heading's agreement with it, so the fall line still takes about half the draws on an even slope and the drift is downhill as before, but no two droplets walk the same cells and the ensemble lays a groove with a width and a wander. IT NEVER STEPS UP, and the pass rests on that: the droplet's height falls every step, so a track cannot cycle and cannot dig the same cell twice; where no lower neighbour exists it ponds. THE BOWL: a droplet that finds no downhill has run into a closed bowl, and what it walks on there is the water that would stand in it: a priority flood, re-run every `batch` (30000) droplets, gives every ponded cell the level of the bowl's spill and the way to it, so a droplet crossing a bowl walks the flat to the rim and grooves the far side, its sediment settling on the floor and never over the water. The bowl drains as its outlet cuts and fills as its floor rises, which is what a lake does with time; the outlets are where the deepest cuts are (100 m). This is the "search upward until a downhill appears" done once per batch for every bowl at once rather than once per droplet.
2. **Cliffs** (`cliffs.js`, `CLIFFS`). OFF by default (`STEPS.cliffs` in `generate.js`), and switched back on from /terrain-v3's sidebar or `tune.steps.cliffs`, because flown rather than read off the map the ladder reads as striation: a rise of constant height repeated along a hillside, and its benches -- 18.9% of canyon land under 10 degrees against the forest's 11.8 -- come back through the bicubic as rounded domes exactly where the peaks should be sharpest. What follows is what it does when it is on. The rain leaves no cliffs and thresholding cannot find any: over the 304449 land texels the steepest texel-to-texel drop is 25 deg at the median and 44.5 at p90, and 0.293% fall more than two texel-distances. So they are made. A ladder of rungs `step` metres apart cuts the slope into bands, and inside a band a texel's height is remapped through a two-piece ramp: a FACE over the lowest `riser` of the band taking `snap` of its fall, and a SHELF over the rest carrying the remaining `1 - snap` gently up to the rung above. The face's grade is the old grade times `snap / riser`, the shelf's the old grade times `(1 - snap) / (1 - riser)`, and both ends of the ramp are fixed points, so the ladder never drifts. Three rules shape it. THE SHELF SITS ABOVE ITS FACE, because a shelf below one can only be cut down to, and that cut is a trench -- the ground under the face was never touched, so the shelf sits in a hollow, and where the weight varies from texel to texel the hollow becomes a run of dishes. Lifting instead makes `f >= frac` everywhere and the pass never lowers a texel (it throws if it computes a move that would); what lies under a face is the untouched slope the droplets left, a real downhill grade, shy of flat, no trench. THE LADDER IS SPARSE: a rung carries a face only when its hash stands above both its neighbours', about one rung in three and never two in a row, so there is always a whole band of untouched slope between one face and the next and a hillside is not a staircase. Integer hash and not noise, because what is wanted between neighbouring rungs is independence, not continuity. THE HEIGHT WANDERS: `snap` is drawn per rung from a span the class sets, so one band puts half its fall in the face and leaves the shelf a walkable grade while the next puts all of it there and goes flat, and `roll` (0.25 of a step over `rollScale`, 240 m) slides the rungs up and down so a shelf tilts and a face grows and shrinks along its run. Reading `riser`: a face is `riser * step / tan(slope)` metres of ground, so under 8 m it lands inside one texel and the whole of `snap * step` falls between two of them -- the most the grid can hold, and a wall. Canyon (`step` 34, `riser` 0.11, `snap` 0.80-1) and desert (28, 0.13, 0.70-1) sit under the line and get walls with near-level tops; arctic (26, 0.30, 0.50-0.90), forest and jungle (21, 0.34, 0.45-0.85) and plains (17, 0.42, 0.50-0.85) sit over it and get a firm bank over a shelf that still falls away under the walker, which is the "45 degrees is fine here" half of the ask. THE BAND: a per-texel coin flip on steep ground gives salt and pepper, so the gate is one octave of simplex at `wavelength` (320 m) thresholded at the QUANTILE of that noise over each class's own steep ground -- `share` (canyon 0.85, desert 0.58, arctic 0.38, forest 0.30, jungle 0.24, plains 0.16, swamp 0) means exactly the fraction of that class's steep ground the band lets through, of which the sparse ladder moves about a third -- and `bandFeather` past the threshold tapers a band's ends off. THE PHASE: a second, slower noise at `phaseScale` (1100 m) offsets the ladder by up to a step, or every shelf on the island would bench at the same heights and read as a contour map. The slope gate (`minSlope` 26 deg, `slopeFeather` 6) is there to spare the flats, not to scale the effect: a wide feather leaves the 30-degree ground, which is most of the steep ground there is, tabled at a fifth strength and no cliff anywhere. Nothing under `minHeight` (6 m) tables, so the coast keeps its beaches. It runs after the rain, because the droplets' 5-cell brush would grind a scarp back into a slope, and before everything below it, so the lakes, the silt, the route and the rivers are all solved on the shape that will be drawn.
3. **Lakes** (`hydrology.js`, `LAKES`). The flood's bowls on the rained-on land (the sea's own bowls are excluded by reading the flood as the ground itself under the waterline) are scored by the widest disc of water over `minDepth` (1.5 m) in them, and the widest `keep` (12) at least `minWidth` (80 m) across are the lakes, each kept whole: every cell the flood wets round its pool, up to and including the spill, at the spill's level, so the river out of it leaves from the water. A lake has no dam and no carve: its shore is wherever the ground meets its level.
4. **Silt.** Every other bowl the rain left is raised to its spill. That is the one artificial move in the step and it shows: a silted bowl is a flat terrace at its rim's height (0.55 km2 on seed 20260824, 3.0 m mean, 34 m at the deepest), where a real one would be a marsh or a lake.
5. **Route.** Priority flood, D8 steepest descent with every lake cell sent along the flood's tree to the spill (steepest descent off the flat lets the cells beside the spill step over the rim on their own, and each is then a source, so one lake would let out three rivers a few cells apart), accumulation in cells. A cell outside the lakes that still ponds throws.
6. **Rivers.** A cell outside the sea and the lakes with `RIVERS.minCatchment` (5 ha) draining through it is a river cell. The network is walked from every mouth (a river cell whose receiver is not one) up its largest donor to a source, the other donors becoming tributaries whose last point is the trunk cell they join; a source whose receiver chain starts in a lake starts inside it. Cell centres are Douglas-Peucker simplified to `tolerance` (5 m) with the junction cells pinned, because a junction the simplifier drops leaves a tributary's mouth beside the trunk rather than on it and v2 cannot pin it, and with the cells either side of a fall of more than `pinDrop` (3 m) pinned too: the simplification is in PLAN and reads no elevation at all, so on a straight reach it would drop a cliff's lip and foot and leave v2 a chord its router is free to lay up to `tolerance` metres to the side, off the notch the water cut and onto the face beside it. Width is `widthAtMin` (1.2 m) times the square root of catchment over `minCatchment`, capped at `maxWidth` (14 m); a tributary under `minLength` (60 m) that nothing feeds is not drawn.
7. **The doc.** Each lake is a v2 lake record with `carve 0`: an ellipse fitted to its deep pool's second moments, grown until all but the outlying `cover` (0.985) of the pool is inside and scaled by `margin` (1.06), at the body's level, so v2 draws water wherever the ground inside the ellipse lies under the level. The pool and not the whole body, because a bowl's shallow arms stretch the ellipse over the ground beside it: fitted to the whole body the summit lake drew 2.9 ha of water in the hollows next to it, fitted to the pool 0.6 ha, and six of its shallow cells stay dry. A quantile and not the furthest cell, because the furthest cell of a curved pool is an outlier that swings the whole ellipse out over the ground at the corners: once the walk's bends made the outlines curvier a max fit leaked 0.0360 km2 against the 0.0335 the gate allows, where the quantile leaks 0.0278, and a few square metres left dry at the tip of one arm cost far less than that. What the quantile alone leaves dry is answered by `holdDepth` (3 m), which overrides it: however far out it lies, water that deep is inside the ellipse. A shallow rim drawn as land reads as a beach; a three-metre hole drawn as land reads as a bug, and without the hold one arm 10.7 m deep was drawn dry against the gate's 4 m limit. The sea stays lake l1. Each river is a v2 river polyline `[x, z, w]`, source to mouth, which v2's `paths.js` re-routes over its coarse heightmap and pins into the lake or river it ends in.

Seed 20260824: 300000 droplets in 8.9 s, 88 cells each, 98.7% reaching the sea (1.3% ponded in a pit a deposit made since the last flood, 16 of the 300000 out of steps, none off the edge); the rain cut 4.30 m mean over 8.67 of the 19.5 km2 of land, 100 m at the deepest and no step over `maxCut`, and laid 4.7 m over 1.32 km2, 43 m at the deepest; the tabling then lifted 1.04 km2 in 33 ms, 4.9 m mean and 26.2 m at the most, leaving 1.61% of the canyon's land over 70 deg against the forest's 0.15% and 4735 texels of face over 12 m in 525 runs, the longest 272 texels; 12 lakes kept of 244 bowls, 0.67 km2 of water at levels from 8 to 433 m, the ellipses leaking 0.0278 km2 and leaving 0.0003 km2 dry, 0.4 m at the deepest; 100 rivers totalling 33.5 km (1.7 km per km2, the longest 1.8 km), 43 into the sea, 17 into a lake, 12 out of one, none climbing, their beds 0.61 m under the ground they cross at the median and 1.10 at p90.

### Sheer faces -- the scarp operator (built)

`src/v2/height/scarp.js`, a `RELIEF_KNOBS` entry (`scarp`, off value 0, and 0 is bit-identical like every other knob's), composed into `V2Height`'s reconstruction through the one `attachCrease` choke point beside `_linear` and `_crease`; `/terrain-v3` runs it at 1 and C toggles it. It runs at READ time on every sample rather than in the generator, and that is the whole reason it can do what the tabling cannot: `cliffs.js` writes texels, and a step of any size between two texels comes back through Catmull-Rom smeared over about 0.8 of a texel, so a written face tops out near 78 deg over a run of 6.4 m however deep the drop is -- a wall to climb along, not one to stand under -- while an operator inside the reconstruction may put the break anywhere between the texels.

It is a HEIGHT REMAP and not a displacement in the ground plane. Elevation is cut into bands of `SCARP_RISE` (34 m), offset by a `SCARP_DRIFT` (700 m) noise `phase` so that two faces on one hill do not break at the same elevation and read as a contour line drawn across both; inside a band the height goes through `h' = RISE * (k + S(t))` with `t = frac((h - phase) / RISE)`, `S` two halves of a power curve of exponent `SCARP_POWER` (14) meeting at (0.5, 0.5). `S(0) = 0` and `S(1) = 1` exactly, and the construction rests on those two identities: they are what make `k + S(t)` continuous across a band boundary. The local slope multiplier is exactly `(1 - s) + s * S'(t)`, so 14 puts the middle 80% of a band's rise in about 2 m of ground -- four cells of the mesher's finest rung, and further than that is a face the mesh cannot carry at any distance. `SCARP_MAX` (0.92) caps the blend because at 1 the tread is dead level and a dead-level bench reads as a machined terrace. The remap is non-separable and knows nothing of the lattice, so a face running diagonally does not zigzag.

Four gates keep it off. THE TEXEL-SCALE SLOPE past `SCARP_SLOPE` (50 deg, fading in over `SCARP_FEATHER` 12), read off a uniform cubic B-spline over the same 4x4 block rather than off the interpolating curve, because the question is how far the landform drops across a texel and not how steep the curve between two of them happens to be -- the same basis and the same argument as `crease.js`. Its support is four texels, so it reports a cliff's slope from up to 16 m away and the operator's reach is wider than the face by that much, which is what the sill in `SCARP_SLOPE` pays for. THE SHEERNESS FIELD: one octave at `SCARP_BROAD` (1100 m) choosing which faces are candidates at all, times one at `SCARP_PATCH` (180 m) making sheerness come and go ALONG a face, which is the "it should transition from sheer to slanted and back, not a whole face vertical" half of the ask. THE BAND IS TALLER THAN THE CLIFFS: 34 m is above the tallest step `cliffs.js` tables (26.2 m measured), so a tabled face meets one riser and not a flight. AND THE BANDS ARE CHOSEN ONE AT A TIME, which the rise alone cannot give, since a face 100 m tall crosses three bands however tall they are and three risers up one face is a staircase: `_pick(k) * (1 - _pick(k - 1))`, each band asking a field of its own -- continuous in x and z, decorrelated from its neighbours by an offset far larger than the feature size -- whether it stands up here, and standing down by as much as the band below it is standing. That is the owner's rule that a cliff is never immediately above or below another.

Per-band selection is safe because of a property of the remap: at a band boundary the input height IS an exact multiple of the rise past the phase, so the remapped height equals it and the displacement `s * (stepped - smooth)` is EXACTLY ZERO there, whatever `s` is. The blend may therefore step from band to band without tearing the surface. What it may not do is step WITHIN a band, which is why each band's own field has to vary continuously. The exact promise, which is what the gate asserts: a riser at full strength forces the bands above and below it to zero, and in between the two trade, so adjacent bands can never both be more than half up.

A RISER IS A NARROW BAND BY CONSTRUCTION -- treads wide, risers thin -- so a scatter of random points lands on a tread nearly every time and is blind to what the operator claims. Measured that way on the shipped v2 import the population gets LESS steep with scarp on (p50 57.8 -> 55.3 deg), which is true and beside the point. The instrument is a fall-line walk. On the v3 island over 74563 dry points, scarp 0 -> 1: the 0.5 m slope median is unchanged at 25.1 deg and p90 hardly moves (45.5 -> 45.3), while the max goes 80.6 -> 87.3 and the share at or past 85 deg goes 0.0000% -> 0.0201%; walking 40 m down the fall line from 1130 steep points, the steepest 0.5 m cell on the way has median 53.3 -> 53.4 -- most faces stay slanted, which is the point -- p90 67.4 -> 70.1, max 80.6 -> 88.6, past 80 deg 0.2% -> 4.1% and past 85 deg 0.0% -> 1.9%.

What `scripts/check-v2-field.mjs` holds, on the shipped import, as the `scarp` knob's five probe checks: gentle ground's RECONSTRUCTION is bit-identical (0 of 1685 sites under 30 deg move), so it is a cliff operator and not a terrace; the COMPOSED field there moves by centimetres and not by nothing (worst 0.082 m), which is a weaker statement and cannot be made as an identity, because the detail stack is not point-local and a riser bleeds onto gentle ground a few metres away; it does reach the steep ground (40.7% of 150 sites past 50 deg); it draws a landform face the plain bicubic cannot (fall line from those sites: p90 73.7 -> 82.9 deg, max 86.6 -> 89.0, past 84 deg on 3 -> 14); and no riser stands directly above another (0 of 80000 adjacent band pairs both past half, the worst pair summing to 1.000, tight to the algebra). Two traps are written into that probe. The stratum is the COARSE field's own slope, not the composed slope over a stride, which is not a proxy for it. And an absolute-angle assertion against `heightAt` is the wrong bar altogether: on the 3x-stretched shipped import the plain bicubic reconstruction already reaches 84.6 deg over a 0.5 m cell and the composed field about 88 with every knob off.

Not done here: the operator knows nothing of the biomes -- its sheerness field is noise, so canyon rock is no likelier to stand sheer than forest, where `cliffs.js` one step upstream is entirely class-driven -- and a sheer face still has no scree at its foot.
