# §30 -- Leafkin and the leafkin villages

Every large forest has a hidden leafkin village: grubby elf-men a metre tall who grunt, squeak and pant to themselves while they bumble through the wood collecting mushrooms. See one and it squeals, drops what it carries and runs for home. Home is a dark hole low in the side of a giant boulder, easy to miss, and walking into that hole puts her inside the boulder: a glade of fern-lined winding paths, tall trees, a stream running to a small lake, and a clearing of shabby stump-huts around one great one. The leafkin are not friendly unless she brings them something they want. Later: leafkin at home in the village, and trade. Built in three pieces, each a thing on its own: the entrances, the leafkin, the village -- the last being the first **room** that is not the overworld.

## 1. The entrances

### Siting: the `hollow` rock bed

The entrance boulder needs a near-vertical face at ground level, deep in a wood, and must look like every other big rock there. §25's giants cannot promise a flat flank at a known height, so the entrances are a bed of their own: **one per 300 m tile that holds a wood**, the tile's one candidate put by `deep` at the point of the tile furthest from open ground and the tile refused unless that point is 50 m into the forest (the forest law's, which runs 55 m up into peak ground), `maxSlopeDeg: 20`, not submerged, `lie: true` (the shape's middle axis up: the longest level like a lying giant's, and the bank boulder's flat bed face -- measured 2 x 1.44 x 1.16 -- standing as a wall), the burial pinned at `sinkRange [0.45, 0.46]` (just under half, never squashed, so the face is the hull's own), `anchor` and `blocks`, resident to 1250 m. A pure function of the tile seed like every bed. `scripts/probe-villages.mjs` counts the shipped map's (287 boulders, 278 of them with a face the mouth probe accepts; the blind ones sit on 14-27° ground) and lists them nearest a point; the `critter LOD tint` row paints them purple (`Rocks.setHollowTint`).

### The mouth

`Entrances` owns no scatter: every frame it asks the rocks for the resident hollows within `RADIUS_M` (400) and seats a mouth on each new one, so a site is where its boulder is and goes when the boulder goes. The face is found by rays against the boulder's own hull from a bearing rolled off the site's key (`PROBE`: 1 m out, eye 0.75 m, 1.5 m of wall within 20° of vertical, up to 16 bearings -- the flat flank is a narrow target on a lying boulder, and the smallest, 8 m, shows 3.2 m of stone), so two clients agree on the mouth to the bit. The mouth is the shipped `cave-mouth` arch scaled to `MOUTH_HEIGHT_M` 1.5, sunk 0.1 m into the face along the normal, drawn on the props' ladder with the shipped T3 in the card's place (an arch is on a wall; a card spun to her would stand out of it), and a **black polygon** -- unlit, `fog: false`, `HOLE.outline` in the arch's frame -- a hair proud of the stone across the arch's passage rather than a cavity: the depth test keeps it in front of the face and the arch's ring around it, and the parallax across the arch's protruding half metre makes it read as a hole. The arch is a solid mound with a recess, not a through hole, and its ring thins at the sides and runs out above ~1 m, so the polygon is cut **inside the ring's solid** at one plane, 0.14 m into the arch's frame (`MOUTH_SINK_M` + `HOLE.proud` 0.04): 0.1-1.1 m over the arch's base, 1.22 m across, the left shoulder notched under a seam that opens 0.1 past the plane. Wherever it reached past the ring it was a black shape on the boulder, and wherever it fell short a rim of stone showed inside the ring, so `check-entrances` samples the pick itself (parity of ray crossings through the shipped T0) and demands stone at every vertex and every centimetre of edge across the planes 0.12-0.16, and that the outline grown 1.5 cm still fits. The face is probed at the outline's vertices and its box centre, since a bulging face is proudest at the centre; how far the stone stands proud of the face point's plane (`bulge`, at most `HOLE.maxProud` 0.2, and at most `maxBulge` 0.4 deeper than the deepest probe) brings the arch, the hole and the mouth point forward together, so the hole keeps its one plane in the ring on every face. **Flanking stones** (`FLANK`): 1-2 a side, rolled off the key, 4.8-8.8 m across, the boulder's own placed colour (`Rocks.hollowTintAt`, its roll rather than `tintAt`'s middle), the rocks' own boulder on the rocks' own material (`Rocks.boulder()`, the tiers cloned because PropMeshes hangs its fade attribute on what it is given), each on the rung its own size and distance earn under `ROCK_LOD_AT` like any rock (the arch's rungs are scaled to a 1.5 m arch and put a 6 m stone on its 20-face tier at 10 m), each set along the face from the ring outward where a ray along the normal meets the boulder, leaning `lean` 0.4 of its radius into it and bedded `sink` 0.4 of its height, and each a column to the walker (`walk.addStone(entrances)`, culled by the site's `flankReach`), so the mouth is tucked away and found only by one who knows what to look for.

`sites(into)` yields `{ key, x, y, z, nx, nz, r, ax, ay, az, holeX, holeZ, state, flank, flankReach }`: the **mouth point**, `MOUTH_STEP_M` 0.7 plus the bulge in from the face along the outward normal, for the leafkin; the arch's centre; and the **hole point** on the face, for the portal. A room passes `fixed: [site]` instead and gets that one mouth, at that point, on nothing.

### The portal test

The slope limiter is not replaced: `player.js` refuses the step into the face and slides, as it must at every other boulder. `portalTest` runs after the step: for a site within `PORTAL.reach` 20 m (`PORTAL` lives in `entrances.js`), she enters when her feet are within `walk` 0.5 m of the hole point and the step ran into the face (`-(step · n) / |step| >= into` 0.5), or a teleport landed within `blink` 0.8 m; the door she stands in takes her once a visit (`portalIn`). The limiter stops her feet 0.05-0.54 m from the hole on the shipped map, so `walk` is as near as she can get; `check-entrances` and `check-village` walk a `Player` at the face and demand it. Into a village she arrives at its own exit; out of one she arrives at the mouth she came in by (`cameInBy`). Refused in the editor.

## 2. The leafkin

### Pipeline

`rig-map.mjs` derives `rig-map.json` from the Tripo biped rig (the `--forward` flag names the facing axis where the rig's own guess is wrong), the skin ladder cuts the tiers, the clip solver bakes the human library plus two new specs, and `ship-biped.mjs` with `'leafkin'` in `BIPEDS` writes `public/creatures/leafkin.glb`. The two clips are human-library specs, so every biped gets them: **`run-carry`** (the run gait with the arms held still, raised and bent so the hands meet at the chest; its speed is the run's) and **`gather`** (a pose clip, `loops: false`, ~0.9 s: crouch, spine forward, one arm to the ground; the key at t 0.5 is the reach). **The human run is a run**: `run.json` at duration 0.5, stride 1.0, duty 0.32 (6.25 wheelbases a second, 1.84x the old 0.7/0.95/0.4, which was slower than her walk), and the leafkin's `clipTweaks` run and run-carry at stride 0.7, duration 0.32, duty 0.36 -- short quick steps, a skittering little thing at 3.25 m/s against her 1.45 m/s walk. Every biped ships the same clips, so all eleven were re-shipped with the library, and `avatars.json` with them.

### Runtime: `leafkin.js`

Snowmen's puppet, tiers, FootIK and fade, with the scatter replaced by **one leafkin per resident entrance site** (`MAX` 16: the 400 m entrance radius on the 300 m tiling), its PRNG seeded from the site key and the chapter (`sim/score.js` `chapterOf`, 600 s from the site's own offset). It comes out of its mouth on the chapter's first tick, makes for home `HOMING_S` 90 before the turn, and is stepped on the score's fixed ticks while her feet are within its roam and cull, so two instances stepped on different frame times agree to the bit; met mid-chapter it is replayed from the chapter's start `CATCH_UP_TICKS` a frame, undrawn and silent until caught up (a cold join over the real ground: 20 frames, ~17 ms). Every step asks its ground (`leafkin-ground.js` `LeafkinGround`), never the drawn scene: a `CELL` 0.5 m cell is OPEN, BLOCKED (water, slope past 50°, a trunk, dead wood) or STONE, and its caps are `capsNear`, all pure of the viewer -- the field, the water and the tree, rock, dead-wood and mushroom scatters read at full density (`pureTrunksInto`, `pureRocksInto`, `pureCapsInto`) -- memoized per cell and per tile under caps. The stone is a superset: every blocking rock's hull circle at its unfitted scale (the gate finds no point a placed rock covers left open), so a leafkin gives boulders a wide berth, which suits it, since caps do not grow on them; STONE alone is walkable inside `FINAL_M` of its mouth, where the hollow's boulder is. Measured on the overworld: a fresh cell ~6 µs, a 60 m square 47 ms cold and ~1 ms memoized. `SIZE_M` 1 ±15%; drawn only within `critterTier`'s cull (~36 m for a metre of body). It never walks: every gait is the run.

| state | what | leaves when |
|---|---|---|
| **roam** | a target inside the site's `ROAM_M` 150 disc every `RETARGET_S` 5-15 s, run (run-carry with a bundle) on arcs (`_arc`): each `ARC_M` 0.8-3 m of run holds one curvature of `ARC_CURVE` 0.15-0.5 rad/m, bent toward the target more often the farther off its bearing the heading points, so the path never runs a metre straight and still arrives; every step's cell asked of the ground, a refused one turning it away by `DETOUR`, `REFUSALS` 10 in a row picking a new target | a cap within `SEEK_M` 3 -> gather; a fright -> startle |

A leafkin is not constructed in a room (`ROOMS.leafkin.leafkin` false); the village has its villagers instead.

### The villagers: `villagers.js`

The leafkin at home: one a house and `EXTRA` 1 more (the spare shares house `k % houses`), the same puppet, ladder, materials and voice as the gatherer, stepped under the `leafkin` visibility row and on the score's fixed ticks. **They walk the roads and nothing else.** The build's roads are dry, clear of trunks and houses and hold a footpath's grade by construction, so a villager never probes the ground: `roadGraph` flattens every road but the pads at `SAMPLE_M` 1.5 into chained nodes, joins each road's ends to the nearest node of another road (or its own ≥ 3 nodes off) within `JOIN_M` 3, and hangs every house door (`doors()`, on the ground a step out) on its nearest node with its `sill` atop the two steps (`DOOR.sill` 0.235 of the pick out, just short of the door's face), so a trip home climbs the steps from the door node, hides at the sill and comes out there; every trip is a `dijkstra` path over that graph, followed at `NODE_M` 0.35 with the walk gait (`_follow`, the heading turned at `TURN_RATE`), its feet on `walk.heightAt` read from where they are, so a house's awning over the door and the roof over that are overhead and not a step it is lifted onto. The trunk is never a wander target, so nobody walks out to the exit. **Nobody walks through anybody, and nobody queues**: two centres keep `SPACE_M` 0.4 apart (scaled by their mean size; they brush), every route runs `KEEP_M` 0.22 right of a road's centreline (`_keepRight`), so two meeting pass without a dodge, a walker veers up to `DODGE` 60° off its bearing to the point it makes for from anyone within that of the line `LOOK_M` 2.5 ahead (to its right when dead ahead; never off its heading, which the veer turns and which then fed back into the veer), a step into someone's space slides round their rim at full length to one side it keeps until a step goes clear (`side`, or it circles; never backward), a point taken by someone counts as reached when pressed against them (`_reached`), and no step goes past `LANE_M` 0.5 of its leg or the roads, a slide past `PART_M` 0.75 (the lane alone pinned walkers at its edge behind anyone standing); `STALL_S` 3 without headway on its point gives the errand up (a fleer plans again), and nobody comes out of a door someone stands in. `check-villagers` holds every walker to `JAM_S` 1 without headway with someone within a metre, and stages a head-on pair and a walker between two standing across a road. They are stepped tick by tick together, so each sees the others where they stand now. Four **gazing spots** are spread round the loop, each `GAZE_OFF_M` 1 m off the road toward the lake where that is dry (the one step off the cobbles a villager takes).

An **errand** (`ERRANDS`) is home 0.2 (the trip ends inside), a gaze 0.25, a sit 0.25 on a random free seat (a wander when none is free) or a wander 0.3 to a random node off the trunk (ends standing). The **seats** are the hearth's stools (`Hearth.stools` in the world, facing the fire) and the scattered ones (`Stools.seats()`), each hung on its nearest graph node and claimed from the moment a villager sets out for it; a seat is `{ x, z, top, r, lookX, lookZ }` and a gazing spot is never put within `r + SIT.round` of one. **Each villager goes at its own pace** (`PACE` 0.75-1.3, rolled with its size off `hash32(seed, id)`), on the gait's ground speed and the puppet's `timeScale` alike so the feet cover what the speed does, and a `RUNNERS` 0.25 share (never fewer than one a village: the fastest, if the roll gave none) run their errands rather than walk. **Every client sees the same day**: each villager reseeds its PRNG at every `CHAPTER_S` 600 s chapter from `hash32(seed, id, chapter)`, a boot mid-chapter replays the chapter from its start in one frame with an unbounded budget (`stepTo`, `Infinity`) and its voices are dropped when more than `SILENT_TICKS` were stepped, so a village entered at any moment is already about its day and silent about the replay. The startles are the one thing local: her feet are hers, and a flee on one client is a walk on another until the next chapter turn puts them back together. `bodies` and `voices` feed the ambience like the gatherer's (`FOOTFALLS.human` on the walk and the run, every call a `SOUNDS` name); `PUPPETS` 8 puppets are shared among however many are out, the rest stepped but not drawn (`stats.starved`).

Gate: `check-villagers.mjs` builds one real village with its hearth and stools and runs the villagers on the gatherer's stand-in asset: the graph covers every road but the pads and every door in one piece with every node on a centreline and dry; a 600 s day with her far off in which every villager goes in and out of its own door at the top of its steps, no two come within their space of each other, nobody steps off the cobbles but at a spot or round a stool or stands over `WALK.reach` above the ground, more than one sits through all four phases on more than one stool, every sitter holds its own seat and no seat two, its feet off the disc, its seated underside on the stool's top through the hold and on the ground as it turns, never outside the two, the stool cut close enough that its feet keep the ground within 0.06 m, faced its stool's way, on the right clip cut at `SIT_CUT` for the phase, walkers walk, or run at their own pace where they are runners, and standers idle, every pace its own and at least one runner but never all, a gazer faces the lake, every talk is a facing pair within reach who both chatter and gesture, every call is a sound the ear has and none comes from indoors; two instances on different frame times agree to the bit and a boot 500 s into a chapter finds them out and silent; and her feet beside a walker set every one running, whimpering and panting by turns and nothing else heard of it, none calming under 30 m and all past it, each getting away from her or indoors.

### Carrying and dropping: `hands.js`

`carry(owner, span)` returns a carrier -- `add(record, src)`, `place(x, y, z, yaw, chest)` per frame, `count()`, `scatter()` -- drawn by the hands' item pool. Everything it takes is resized to `span` (the record itself, so the thing stays that size scattered and in her hand; the leafkin's is `CARRY_SPAN` 0.14 of its body, half its torso's width) and held against the chest at `CARRY_SLOTS`, each jittered and given its own roll about the body's forward axis and tilt about its side, so an armful is a jumble and not one shape drawn five times; `scatter()` moves them into the loose list in state `fall` with random headings, so they fall, thud and roll with the drop physics and are pickable after. Carriers are taken lazily and held to `CARRIERS` 16, one per resident leafkin, since a leafkin gathers whether or not it is drawn; `POOL_CAP` grows by `CARRY_MAX * CARRIERS`.

### Voice: `ambience.js`

A herd entry `{ layer: leafkin, clips: 'human' }` for the feet (`run-carry` on the run's beats); the one-shots (a call and a pant by turns, `CHATTER_S` 2-3.5 s apart roaming and `WHIMPER_S` 2.7-4.1 s apart in flight, the chatter or the whimper first; a squeal at a cap in sight, no oftener than `SQUEAL_S` 5 s; the scream) drained from the layer's `voices(into)` with a position, so the sound engine and the state machine never share a clock; the gatherer heard within the `voice` rule's 60 m, fading over its last 10 m (across its wood, before it is seen), and a glade's villagers within `villagerVoice`'s 10 m over the last 5 m (across the pond, not across the wood); `Ambience` takes each voiced layer with its rule. The pant is a one-shot at the voice's level, not a loop: a loop at breathing level was never heard over the chatter. The six `.m4a` were re-encoded to `.mp3`; `npc-leafkin-panting.mp3` was a genuine MP3 and plays as is.

### Netplay

Lockstep, with no owner and no heartbeat: every client steps the same leafkin from the same seed over the same ground, and the room hears only her doings, as `lk` anchors through `CreatureNet` (`_notes/creature-sync.md`): a `fright` (her feet within `STARTLE_M` of it) and a `pick` (a cap her hand took within its reach, heard through `mushrooms.onTake`), each keyed `lk:<site>:<tick>:<hash>`; a pick is raised for the next tick, a fright `LEAD_TICKS` 4 (200 ms, about a relay round trip) on and screamed at once on her client, so a peer usually hears it before stepping that tick. The villagers' startles work the same way (`vg` anchors, whimpered at once, the villager's own first whimper hushed so it is not heard twice). Each client logs a site's anchors in tick order and deduped, its own included (`hearsOwn`); one heard for a tick already stepped rolls the leafkin back to the last snap before it (every `SNAP_TICKS` 20, `SNAPS` 30 deep) and steps it again, and one older than the snaps replays the chapter. A rollback is never drawn as a jump (`render/net-ease.js`): the body is drawn where the undone timeline had it and the error eases off at 1/e per `SMOOTH_S` 0.35 s, no faster than `CLOSE_MPS` 4. A drawn move past `JUMP_M` 1 in one frame is counted (`jumps`, with `maxRewind` in the stats) and logged as `[net] <Layer> pop` at most once a second, for the headset's shipped log. All of this rests on the room's clock agreeing across devices: `Netplay` pings the relay (`PING_MS` 10 s, quicker at first) and `WorldClock` reads `Date.now()` plus the quickest trip's offset, so a device clock seconds out does not turn into seconds of rollback. A late joiner gets the relay's anchors and replays to the same leafkin. The bundle it scatters falls through the hands' pool on each client that draws it, and the caps it takes are gone on each by the same step. Gated by `check-leafkin` and `check-villagers`: two clients over a lagged relay agree after a fright, a pick and a fright heard 40 s late, one heard inside the lead never rolls back and one 1.5 s late is drawn without a jump, and two clients grown about different spots on the real overworld step one leafkin with 0 of 3600 frames apart.

Known gaps: the embedded hollow bed's loose circles make roughly a quarter of some discs STONE; rock-anchored caps (~5%) are not among the pure caps; a cap the chapter regrows but `taken` still holds gone locally can be gathered as air; `hollowOver` and the mouth's flanks hang on the hollow bed being resident; the hands' net relays a leafkin's takes as her own; and `Math.sin`/`cos` are assumed to agree across engines.

## 3. The village: the first room

### What a room is

A room is what `ROOMS[id]` names: the overworld (`dir: 'world'`, its files, sky, hollows, leafkin) and the village (`village: true`, built in memory at boot). `bootWorld` reads the save and builds the room it names; `bootRoom(room, site)` is the swap: the view faded to black over `FADE_MS` 500 (a sphere on the camera whose opacity the tick steps, so it holds in XR; a hidden tab, which ticks no frame, snaps it after two fades' time), every layer disposed, the room's stack rebuilt (`buildRoom`), her feet `ARRIVE_M` 2 out from the site along its normal, facing along it (`faceAlong`), and the view faded back in over another 500 ms once she can move. The clock, the backpack and the net come with her; what her hands hold is packed and restored (`restoreHeld`), except a creature, whose layer is gone -- it goes in the backpack if there is room, else it is lost. The banks (atlas, GLBs, clips) stay loaded, so the swap is the terrain build and the placement. The save carries `room` and `door`, the mouth she came in by, since that is the village to build; a village save without a door boots the overworld with a warning. In a village the animals are the small ones: the wildlife holds fox and hare only (`Wildlife`'s `species` option), no roosts and so no dragons, and the fish, frogs, crabs and insects as the overworld has them. ### The valley: `rooms/village.js`

**Every village is its own.** The seed is `keyHash` of the mouth's site key, the entrance's place in the overworld, so a village is the same on every visit and every client and no two are alike. `rollVillage(seed, house)` rolls the shell's yaw, the jitter's seed, whether the basin stands off the axis and where (`BASIN`), how many rivers (`RIVER.count` 1-2), their bearings (the first sometimes beside the exit, `RIVER.nearOdds`) and each one's swing, wavelength and phase, how far the loop stands off the water (`LOOP.over` 2-4 m), which side the clearing is and how far round (`CLEARING.bearing` 50-120° off the exit's), the ring houses' count and heights and which is the great house, and the outlying houses' count and heights (`OUTLYING.count` 2-3). `buildVillage({ spec, shell, house })` answers `{ heightmap, doc, spawn, exit, clearing, lake, props, gardens, decor, wood, lamps, stools, ground, spec, fishSeed }`, `spec` being the roll actually built: a roll whose lake is a puddle, whose houses do not fit round the ring clear of the wall and `EXIT.houses` 20 m from the exit, whose lake reaches within `LAKE.margin` of the wall, whose rivers none reaches the lake, falls `RIVER.rise` or winds too little or folds, whose rivers leave no room for the loop, whose trunk finds no way down clear of them and the clearing or comes down to the arrival's own feet (`TRUNK.room`), whose loop or trunk steepens past `ROAD_GRADE` between its held chords or holds one bearing for `STRAIGHT_M`, whose trunk crosses the loop, whose roads cannot hold their grade or whose outlying house finds no site is rolled again from the next `attempt` under the same shell (`ROLL_TRIES` 128; **no seed re-rolls now**: the six gate seeds and forty more all build their first attempt in 28-260 ms cold, where a 55-scale shell round six houses took a mean of 7 re-rolls and up to 34. The room outgrew the rejections rather than being tuned out of them -- the shell is a fifth wider and the ring one house shorter, so the houses fit round it with room to spare. The count is chaotic if it ever returns: any constant moved re-rolls everything after it). An attempt builds one `V2Height` over the map, the lake alone in it, for the ground the exit stands on and the rivers are walked on, and swaps the finished doc into the same field (`setLayers`): the field's reconstruction was half of every attempt, and it does not depend on the layers. `fishSeed` is the first seed from the room's up whose first bass site on one of the four fish tiles about the lake stands in water deep enough with clearance (`fishSeedFor`, fish.js's own roll replayed), since a pond a few hundred square metres wide holds such a site for one roll in ten and a room seeded blind was as often fishless as not; main.js seeds the room's `Fish` on it, so every village has a school. ### The shell, the light, the exit

The **shell** (`shell.js`) is **the rock bank's boulder she walked into**, on the bank's own stone layer, stood as the hollow bed stands it (a Z quarter turn), at `SHELL.scale` 66 (96 m on its long axis, the wall 29-45 m from the axis at the rim) with `sink` 0.4 of its height under the floor, turned inside out (index winding reversed and normals negated: a negative scale alone does not expose the inside, three flips the front face for it), front-face-only, the stone the boulder's own tile at the stood scale (a placed boulder's tile scales with the rock), and tinted as a placed boulder is (`Rocks.tintAt`, the bank shared: `rocks.bank === shell's`). `wallAt(y, bearing)` and `roofAt(x, y, z)` are rays against it, and the gate demands 3 m of roof over every walkable point. **The shell is stone to her** (`walk.addStone(shell)`): a column table over the hull's plan at `CELL`, each column the heights the vertical line through it crosses the surface, so below the first crossing, above the last and every second gap between is stone (`columnAt`, `blockTopAt`, `crossingsAt`; a wall bulging into the room is a ledge on that line), read bilinearly where the four cells about a point agree on their count and from the nearest cell where they do not. The wall refuses her step like any boulder, and her flight is held under the roof: `WalkSurface.ceilingAt` is the underside of the lowest stone over a point, `Player._fly` keeps her head the same clearance under it that her feet keep over the ground, and a column with no room for both (the foot of the wall) refuses the step unless she was already in one, so a step out of stone is never refused the way a step in is. Within `EXIT.door` 1.5 m of the exit's face point the stone gives way top to bottom (`setDoor`) for the arch and its hole.

It is a map baked once over the room's plan at `texel` 0.5 m, the sum of each lamp's falloff to `reach` 9 m at `gain` 0.6, three channels carrying three flicker groups so `uLampGlow` can flicker them out of step (`FLICKER`, two sines about a mean, real time), the alpha the flames' height so a roof ten metres over a lamp stays dark and the hut wall beside it does not; `lighting.js` reads it as a third compile-time axis (`setLamps`/`clearLamps`, the vertex path reading it per vertex and carrying the light across), so the overworld pays nothing for it, and the light multiplies the surface's own colour, which is what makes it read as a flame's and not as the night lift. The flames carry their group in an instance attribute and burn on the same `uGlow`, so each flickers with its own pool of light; `Lamps.breath` is the three groups' mean, for the windows. **The windows** ride the same map: every hut's `WINDOWS` (room-props.js, points in the pick's frame with the pane's facing, read off the mesh by a click in the `/gen-prop` viewer) is a cone in the bake, `window.reach` 6 m at `window.gain` 0.5, `pow(cos, cone 2)` about its facing and nothing behind the wall, spread evenly over the three channels so it breathes at the flickers' mean and lifts the ground from the pane's height. `check-shaders` compiles the lamp axis and the glow; `check-village` gates the placement and the map (bright at every foot, lit within the reach and dark past every emitter's, a step out of every window and a cone from one, a flame in every dish, hidden by day and burning by night on the map's glow).

**Glowing panes: `gen-props.js` `addGlow`.** A hut's honeycomb window is lit from inside by the mesh's own material, not by a light: `addGlow(material, points)` splices a Lambert program so that inside a disc about each point (`{ x, y, z, r }` in the geometry's own frame, before the instance matrix, full at 0.6 r and gone at r) the surface is drawn **unlit** by `uGlowOn` (0..1): its lit colour gives way to its own albedo times `uGlow`, so the atlas's amber cells come up amber whatever the night and the dark bars painted between them stay dark (an additive glow lifted toward white lit the bars with the cells and read as an orange wash over the disc); at `uGlowOn` 0 the disc shades like the wall about it. The huts' `setGlow` writes the lamps' breath into `uGlowOn`, so the panes are plain wall by day and come up with the lamps at dusk, flicker included, at `GLOW.night` 1.6 times the tint; the count of points is baked into the program key. The points are in the **shipped** pick's frame -- its raw vertices centred over their feet, since `ship.mjs` drops Tripo's node yaw (the leafkin house's is 20°) and `loadCritterGlb` centres what ships -- so one list authored on the pick lights every decimated tier. They are placed in the `/gen-prop` bench's glow points table, which maps a click back through the working mesh's node into that frame and previews the list through the same `addGlow`, the panes lit only with the dusk box ticked.

The **exit** is the same arch and quad, one fixed site from the build's `exit` (`placeExit`): the bearing whose wall stands most nearly plumb across the arch's height (`EXIT.band` 1.5 m, within `plumb` 0.5 m; every `sweep` 4° round, then every degree about the best, the band read at its ends and middle and the rest only where that spread still beats the best, since thirteen rays a bearing is most of a build), its face point on the wall at the arch's mid height over the ground there as the trunk's shoulder will leave it (the bare ground blended toward the arrival's, `ARRIVE_M` 2 m in, by the road feather), so the arch's back half stands in the stone and the mouth is never loose on the ground; its normal into the room; the same portal test sends her home. **No stone covers the black**: the wall is read at the hole's outline (`HOLE.outline`, `EXIT.foot` 0.4 m under and over the ground for whatever the arch reads there), how far the innermost stands into the room past the face's plane is the exit's `bulge`, and the arch and its hole come forward by it (`Entrances._place`; the bearings taken bulge under 5 cm, and the gate reads the outline every 5 cm against the real ground). `Shell.wallAt` is a hand-written ray against the hull's triangles banded by height (`BAND` 1 m), front faces only like the raycaster it replaced and sixteen times faster, since the exit's rays are most of a build.

### The stools: `render/stools.js`

The hearth's stools (`HEARTH.stools`, `render/hearth.js`) are cut to a metre-tall leafkin's seat rather than to a height, and the build scatters more of the same for the villagers to sit on away from the fire (`placeStools`, `STOOLS`): one by every outlying house's door, at its trunk's edge `beside` 1.2 m off the branch's centreline on whichever side stands clear (stepped up to 1.5 m further out where the first place fails), facing down the branch; and `shore` 3 spread along the loop where the water lies within `wet` 4 m past it, `off` 1.2 m off the loop toward the lake (or half a metre or a metre further), facing it. A place is dry, `edge` 0.4 m past every road's edge, `wall` 0.4 m off a house's trunk, `lamp` 0.8 m off a lamp, `apart` 5 m from every other stool, `LAMPS.wall` inside the stone and **level**: within `level` 0.1 m of flat `flat` 0.3 m about and `flat` toward its look, where a sitter's feet stand; a place that fails is skipped, not moved. The stools are set on the shipped ground, so the build swaps the roads and pads into its field before placing them (the field before them is rougher along every verge). `Stools` cuts each as a nonagon of pine bark off the hearth's numbers into one merged mesh on the prop material (six prisms is under a card of triangles, drawn whole at every distance), and every stool, the hearth's ring and these alike, is cut by one rule (`cutStool`): its base the lower of its own ground and its sitter's, 2 cm under, and its top `SEAT_M` 0.19 m -- a metre-tall leafkin's `seatY` -- over the mean ground 0.15-0.3 m toward what it faces, where the feet stand. So it stands taller where the ground falls away past it and shorter where it rises, within `height` 0.14-0.34 m; past that band the cut stops and the feet take the rest. Each stool is stone to the walker, off limits to the wood and a seat to the villagers. `check-village` gates the placement (one by every outlying door facing its way, two or more on the shore facing the lake, every clearance, the level rule) and the layer (one stool a site, each a seat, cut to the hearth's numbers, stone to the walker and not past its rim, the wood off it).

### Her size

**In a glade she is half her size** (`HER_SCALE` 0.5 in `rooms/village.js`, `ROOMS.leafkin.scale`; the overworld's is 1), near the leafkin's own metre, so the ferns stand over her head, the frogs are the size of cats and the huts are houses. It is one knob read as `herScale()` and set under the swap's black alone: `buildRoom` builds the room's `WalkSurface`, `Player` and `Hands` with the room's `scale`, and nothing changes size mid-view. **The rig is the whole of it**: `Player` sets `rig.scale` to it, and WebXR composes the head and the grips through the rig's matrix, so her eye height, her roomscale steps, her hand meshes and everything parented under the camera (the blackout, the desk hand) are hers without a line of code; the A-Frame raycaster normalises its direction after the rig's transform, so a hit's distance stays in the world's metres. What is measured in metres in code is multiplied by the scale so it stays hers: her pace, stride, fly floor and ceiling clearance, the flight altitude bands and the travel cruise (`Player.scale`); her capsule, step reach, the rock she may stand on, the trunk pad and the slope's sampling (`WalkSurface`'s `reach`, `height`, `radius`, `rockMin`, `trunkPad`, `slopeEps`, each `WALK`'s times the scale, and the villagers and peers walk on the same surface, so in a glade a villager's step reach is halved too, which a metre-tall villager does not notice); her hand's reach, `GRAB_MAX`, `STOW_MAX` and the stow zone (`Hands.scale`); the teleport's lob, gravity, range, ring and bead, so the arc is the same shape at half the throw; the quest panel's stand-off, eye offset, leave distance and size, and its pointer's line and dot (the line hangs under the scaled rig, so its length is divided); the desk hand's limit and stand-off; the mirror's; and the footstep rule, fed `player.speed / player.scale` so a stride still sounds a stride. What is not scaled is meant: a held thing stays the world's size (a 1 m rock in her hand is 2 m to her), the creatures startle at their own distances, `ARRIVE_M` and the portal are the room's. **Over the net** her pose carries `scale` when it is not 1; the relay validates it in [0.05, 20], keeps it on the client and forwards it, and `PeerAvatars.dress` builds the peer's body at `heightM * scale` and re-dresses when it changes, so a peer in the glade is drawn half size to anyone beside her, and her mirror double is dressed the same way. A relay older than the field drops it and peers draw her full size. `check-walk`, `check-hands` and `check-village` gate her half size: a step under `WALK.reach` is a wall at half, a bridge at half the height is cleared, the eye and the pace halve, the hand's reach and zone halve, and the village's walk, hull and flight checks run at `HER_SCALE`.

### The score: `audio/music.js` (unwired, no songs)

A glade is meant to have music and the overworld none, but **no song auditioned so far is the glade's**, so `SONGS` is empty, nothing in `main.js` constructs a `Music`, `public/music/` is gone and the gate is off `npm run check`. The module keeps the player for when the right songs arrive. **The playlist is a timetable and she keeps to it**: a `timeline` over the room's world clock (`clock.seconds`, the same on every client in a netplay room) seeded by the village's seed, slot k a `PAUSE_S` 1-5 min silence rolled from `hash32(seed, k)` and then a song rolled from it that is never the one before, each slot starting where the last ends on the pinned lengths in `SONGS` (afinfo's, not the decoder's, so every browser cuts the same slots), so the second every song starts on is fixed by the seed and every client hears it start together. A song is only heard from its beginning: entering mid-song or mid-pause plays nothing until the next slot's second (a start reached within `LATE_S` 2 s still plays from the top; one the frame loop slept through is passed over for the one after), and a song that has started plays to the element's `ended` unless she leaves or the switch (`setEnabled`, for a menu row beside Sound) goes off, either of which ramps the level `LEVEL` 0.125 to 0 over `FADE_OUT_S` 3 and pauses the element when the ramp lands; entering again mid-fade cuts it dead, and back on she waits for the next start like anyone walking in. The songs stream through `HTMLMediaElement`s (a decoded two-minute song is 45 MB of PCM) into one gain on the master bus: the Sound row's mute takes them, the water does not, and the next song is told to fetch during its pause. A song that fails to load turns the music off until the next room and says so once. Wiring it back is one `Music` on the `SoundEngine` at boot, `enter(seed)` in `buildRoom`, `leave()` in `disposeRoom` and `update(clock.seconds)` in the frame loop.

Gate: `check-music.mjs` (by hand) runs the timeline on its numbers and the player against a fake engine and fake elements on a fixture pool, frame by frame on a world clock, for every rule above, and checks whatever `SONGS` lists exists under `public/`.

## Decisions taken

- The room is built in memory in the overworld's format at the overworld's 8 m texel; nothing ships for it.
- The bowl is a valley of its own inside the bank's boulder, not the boulder's underside; the shell is that boulder's mesh and stone.
- The light inside is the overworld's, on the same clock; only the sky, stars and aurora are hidden. The lamps add a baked map, never point lights.
- The shell is stone to her: no walking or flying through the wall or the roof.
- She is half her size in a glade, the one knob `HER_SCALE` on the room; a leafkin is a metre tall, so 0.6 would match it, and 0.5 was chosen for the fairy-tale of it.
- The villagers keep to the roads and are local under a startle; the day itself is shared through the seeded chapters.
- A village pond hears the quiet lapping only; a size threshold for the wave is wishlisted, not built.
- A sitter is placed by its butt, not its feet: the stool is cut to a metre-tall leafkin's seat and the body hung from it, so a taller or shorter sitter's feet ride a few centimetres off the ground. Foot IK for a seated leafkin is wishlisted, not built.
- `hollow` is a rock bed.
- The `house-leafkin` mesh ships; no stand-in huts.
- The mouth is 1.5 m and she does not duck.
- The startle range is measured feet to feet: the leafkin's ground point against her plan position, never her head.
- The leafkin is per client until creature sync lands (the aurora-game-22 build); the seed keeps the same leafkin at the same site on every client.
- `npc-leafkin-panting.mp3` is a valid MP3 and is used as is.
