# 39 -- Caves

Status: built, untested in a headset. Builds on the torch (§37 `uTorch`), the mushroom family (§24), the trust relay pattern (`server/src/main.js` `applyTrust`) and the hands' sources (`hands.js`, `taken.js`).

Cave mouths stand at the feet of cliffs. Walking a couple of metres into one fades her to black and moves her into the mouth's cave **system**: a pitch-dark labyrinth of tunnels, caverns, drops and flooded passages, in regions that each look their own, generated from the system's seed so every peer walks the same cave. Mouths within ~250 m may open onto the same system.

## 1. Pieces

| piece | job |
|---|---|
| `caves/sites.js` | sites the mouths on the overworld (`siteMouths`) and groups them into systems (`groupSystems`) |
| `layers/clefts.js` | the notch cut into the cliff foot at each mouth, and the keep-off for trees and rocks |
| `layers/trails.js` | the footpath from each mouth to the roads |
| `render/cave-mouths.js` | the leafkin arch at each mouth (an `Entrances` in fixed mode), and the walk-in test |
| `caves/graph.js` | the topology: nodes, passages, drops, sumps, rivers, pools, glows, regions |
| `caves/field.js` | the signed distance field the graph describes |
| `caves/props.js`, `caves/regions.js` | what stands in the cave, and each region's look |
| `caves/build.js` | `planCave`: graph, field, props, lights and the doors, from `{ seed, entries }` |
| `caves/mesh.js`, `caves/worker.js` | surface nets per 16 m chunk, in a worker, nearest chunks first |
| `caves/walk.js` | `CaveWalk`: the walk surface read off the field |
| `render/cave-room.js` | the system drawn: chunks, instanced props, water, chalk |
| `caves/chalk.js`, `render/chalk.js` | chalk strokes: codec, store, pens, ribbons; the lumps as a hands source |
| `check-caves.mjs` | determinism, the no-trap proof on the built surface, sump flooding, triangle budget, chalk |

All of `caves/` is pure and node-runnable. main.js wires it (`--- caves` section): `siteCaves` groups the world plan's mouths at overworld build, `caveTest` runs the portal each step, `enterCave`/`leaveCave` swap the walk under the fade (`enterCave` plans the system and starts the worker there, nothing ahead of her: a plan on the main thread near a mouth froze flight 0.25-1.4 s per mouth passed), `stepCave` runs the frame underground.

## 2. Mouths in the overworld

**Siting.** Candidates are scanned on the raw 8 m texels for level ground with a face rising at least `RISE_MIN` (11 m) two texels behind it, then refined against the walker's exact height so the hole's floor is the ground under her feet. Water, towns, the mirrored strip past `TOWN.realZ` and ground no trail reaches refuse a site. Siting runs in `planWorld` (layers/world-plan.js) after the roads and before the trails, whose records would move it, so the mouths ride the baked plan. Mouths keep `MOUTH_SPACING` (160 m) apart, at most `MOUTH_CAP` (160; the shipped world fills it, nearest neighbours a median 232 m apart). Pairs within `LINK_M` (250 m) join one system with odds `LINK_ODDS` (0.6), by union-find; LINK_M keeps the chains short, so a shipped system is at most 8 mouths and 418 m from its centre, inside chalk's ±655 m (check-caves holds it under 600 m; nothing in `groupSystems` caps it, so the number moves whenever the mouths renumber). A system is `{ id, seed, mouths, cellars, cx, cz }`; the seed is a uint32 and keys everything about it, chalk included.

**Cellars.** `siteCellars` gives `CELLAR_ODDS` (0.15) of town houses (never huts), `CELLAR_SPACING` (40 m) apart, a stair down into the cave in a screened corner of the house's ground floor (design/38 Cellar). A house is skipped where its inside, rolled with `probe` as far as the stair, has no corner for it two rooms in from the hall; about a third of the candidates on the shipped map, mostly two-room cottages, leaving 40 cellars. `groupSystems` runs after the cliff union-find, so cliff systems keep their ids, seeds and centres. Each cellar then joins the nearest system whose centre is within `CELLAR_LINK_M` (250 m, inside chalk's ±655 m packing) on a `LINK_ODDS` coin, or founds a system of its own with no mouths. `caveEntries` orders a system's doors mouths first, then cellars, so door `i >= mouths.length` is a cellar. A cellar's passage ends at a wooden door (boards in the interiors' grain, iron straps and ring) with a torch in its wall 3 to 5 m from it, so the door shows in its light, and maybe a second 4 to 9 m on. The torch is the overworld's stick set `TORCH_SINK` deep in the rock along its lean, a spot whose lean runs along the wall refused. Stick and door wear the baked light of the rock they meet (`rockAt`). The door is a wall of posts (`block`) she stops 0.3 m short of; within `HOUSE_DOOR.walk` of its face, as at the house's own door, she goes up into the house at the foot of the stair. Chalk drawn in a cliff system before it gained a cellar no longer lines up, because the graph changed.

**The look.** The ground is a height field and cannot overhang, so a mouth is two parts, both sized off `MOUTH` (sites.js):

1. **An arch** (`CaveMouths`): the leafkin villages' stone entrance (`render/entrances.js`, the `cave-mouth` bank) at `MOUTH.scale` (2.2), 3.3 m tall and 4 m across, its black hole about 2.7 m wide. It runs as an `Entrances` in fixed mode, one site per mouth, so it has the same LOD rungs and ground shadow as a village's. Like a village's, it does not collide.
2. **A notch** (`CleftSet`): in the mouth's frame (s metres in past the foot, t across), a level floor at the mouth's y, cut or filled, `floorW` (3 m) either side and `floorOut` (4.5 m) out from a straight back wall `MOUTH.wall` (1 m) in. The wall rises 5 m half a metre behind that line, so the terrain's 0.5 m cells never climb in front of the hole, and feathers into the cliff behind. The arch stands against it. Clefts are made at boot like roads and never saved. Trees and rocks keep off the floor and a 6 m approach out in front of it (`occupiesAt`), so the arch shows from a way off.

**The trail** (`routeTrails`): one Dijkstra over the roads' 8 m grid, on the live surface, outward from every laid road cell at once, so each mouth follows it back to its cheapest road or onto a trail already laid (a quarter of them). It runs straight from half a metre short of the wall to 10 m out, level across the floor, then smoothed, resampled at 6 m and Gaussian-heighted like a road. It is a generated road record (`trail<k>`) half a road wide with a 3 m feather, so it paints dirt and keeps trees and rocks off like any road, but it is not a way: no signpost, no traveller, and no road cell within 24 m of a signpost seeds it, so no sign seems to point down it. A footpath steps up to 0.5 grade where a road stops at 0.28; the shipped trails run a median 263 m, at most 1.4 km, and pitch no steeper than 0.84 (check-caves).

**The portal.** With her feet within `MOUTH.reach` (0.8 m) of the hole's plane and `MOUTH.holeW` (1.2 m) of its axis, `caveTest` fades her under and boots her at the mouth's door inside, `ARRIVE_IN` (3.5 m) down the passage, facing in. Behind each mouth node the passage runs on as a bumpy 20 m flare (`mouthFlare`, field.js; a cellar's is 2.4 m), capped by a plane that draws the overworld's own sky gradient (`skyRadiance` on Sky's shared uniforms, so it follows the hour). Boulders ring the mouth and ferns and grass grow on its floor (`mouthDressing`, props.js). Its `DAYLIGHT` is baked as `day` light scaled by `uDay`, so the way out reads from down the passage. Walking within `EXIT_R` of the mouth node, or anywhere past it into the flare, takes her back out onto the notch's floor before the arch (`CaveMouths.apron`), facing away from the cliff.

**Where the cave is.** Every system is built at `CAVE_OY` (-2000 m) under the overworld at its plan's own x, z, so a peer in the same cave stands where she does with nothing new on the wire. Underground, the overworld is not stepped (`stepOverworld`), and every overworld scene child except the rig, hands, peers, flames and the room is hidden each frame; `darkCave` zeroes sun, sky and night lift and turns the fog black.

## 3. The graph

Nodes are `mouth`, `junction`, `chamber` and `cavern`. Each mouth gets a throat junction 18 m in; more junctions scatter at `SPACING` and some become chambers or caverns. The first cavern with room, kept 35 m clear of every mouth, is a **great cavern**: 30 to 200 m long, its roof 40-70 m up, ringed by lobes and nooks, with up to 16 braids off it (most passages elsewhere take 4) and never fewer than two open passages, a rockfall included. Edges are `walk`, `drop` or `sump`; a walk may also be a squeeze or a river.

**The no-trap rule is structural.** Every node hangs off a spanning tree of two-way edges (walks and sumps), so any node reaches a mouth by walking and swimming alone. Drops (a lip from `DROP_MIN`, 2.2 m, past her reach, to `DROP_MAX`, 9 m) are only ever added on top of the tree as shortcuts down, often opening high in the lower chamber's wall. Grades stay under `GRADE_MAX` (0.42), a fifth of tree walks descending into the cave. Passage width and height vary per edge (from 1.8 m wide and 2.3 m tall, some vaulting several metres higher), stacked passages keep `SILL` (2.6 m) of rock between them, and two links out of one node keep 40 degrees apart (`alongside`), so a climbing passage never leaves a ledge in its sibling's tube. A rockfall chokes `CHOKE_ODDS` (0.4) of the plain braids (never a tree edge, a river, a mouth's passage, or one that would leave a great cavern under two open) partway along, at a spot no other passage's run comes near: the edge's `choke` is the metres along it, and props.js fills the tube there with boulders and a wall-to-wall row of obstacles, so the braid becomes two dead ends. `check-caves.mjs` proves the rule on the graph with chokes removed, then walks every unchoked two-way passage both ways on the built surface and checks each rockfall stops her in both directions, swimming included.

**Down is in.** Depth grows from the mouths at y = 0, so "climb when lost" is a rule she can learn, and the drops make it one with exceptions.

## 4. The field and the mesh

`CaveField.at(x, y, z)` is positive in rock, negative in air, roughly metres, in cave-local coordinates. Passages are vaults over a flat floor swept along each edge's samples, each edge in its own cross-section (`passageForm`, seeded by the edge): the plain tall half-ellipse, wide and low, a round tube bellied over its floor, or crumpled, any of them maybe leaning with its vault swaying side to side, all faded to plain over `FORM_FADE` (6 m) at each end and never under `FORM_H_MIN`; mouth passages, swims and drops stay plain. Nodes are ellipsoid domes cut by a floor. **Shape octaves** misshape every room and passage alike: noise features `SHAPE_BASE` (0.625 m) doubling per octave, each up to `SHAPE_AMP` of its size deep, a piece taking every octave whose feature size fits `SHAPE_FIT` (3.6) times in its largest dimension, so raising `SHAPE_AMP` (0.9) deepens the folds without adding octaves. At that depth the field's gradient can flatten in a fold, so `onRock` (chalk.js) refuses a Newton step over a metre rather than land tens of metres off. A room's octaves are biased inward (`SHAPE_BIAS`) and push its wall in at full depth but carve it out at most `SHAPE_CARVE` (2 m), faded in over `SHAPE_RISE` above the floor, so the graph's sumps and passages stay clear of its rim and passages still meet it level; the macro irregularity is mostly rock bulging in. A passage's octaves only carve. Past a metre off a room's wall its shell is compressed so the field's slope stays under what a sphere tracer trusts. A great cavern's hollow the wall swallowed holds no pool. Everything joins by a smooth min. Noise (`noise3.js`, value noise for speed) roughens walls in warped lumps and ridged creases, and floors in swells and ledges; the wall noise is skipped where it cannot change the sign (`ROUGH`, `AIRY`), and `bump` is read once per sample, as it is most of a prim's cost. The mesher and `CaveWalk` build identical fields from the same graph, so they agree to the millimetre.

**Surface nets** on a voxel grid in 16 m chunks (`LODS`, `CHUNK`). A chunk samples one voxel past each face but emits quads only for edges it owns, so seams close without cracks. Per vertex the mesher bakes region colour, a cheap AO, and the glow of every light within reach (§8). Passages end in rounded caps whose floor climbs a metre a metre under a vault left on the unclimbed floor, so a climb never shelves a terrace and a cap never leaves a film of air at its neighbour's roof; a passage's floor coves up `COVE` (0.9 m) into its walls, so climbing out of another's tube is a ramp. Chamber and cavern floors carry `relief` (graph.js `relieve`): a tilt and mounds on a 1 m grid, slope-limited, flattened where passages arrive; a great cavern's is rougher (its own noise stream, not the graph's), runs past the floor's edge so hills climb into the wall instead of dropping to floor level at the rim, and cuts a river channel and pools in its hollows (only hollows whose low point is on the floor).

**LOD and regions.** Every chunk is meshed once at 0.5 m and every tier vertex-clustered from that, its seam rows clustered at 1 m along the face whatever the tier, so a chunk's seam vertices are identical at every tier and neighbours at different tiers meet without a crack (the gate checks it). The hem is thus twice as fine as the 2 m tier and four times the 4 m; the seam fans are about half the coarse tiers' triangles. A seam vertex whose triangles a coarse tier would all collapse keeps them at full detail there, so none goes missing in one tier only. One mesh per region: 0.5 m within 10 m of her (gap to the chunk's box), 1 m clusters to 25 m, 2 m to 50 m, 4 m to `CULL_M` (85 m), nothing past it (`drawnLod`, shared by `CaveRoom` and the gate). Only her region and those it touches are drawn. Clear sightlines past 30 m are rare and the black fog hides most of what lies past 25 m, so the thin walls the coarse tiers drop do not show. The gate holds the triangles drawn from any node, counting every direction, under 120k; its systems peak around 110k, a great cavern's open floor among the heaviest.

**The worker** plans the same cave from `{ seed, entries }` and posts chunks nearest her arrival first, then 'near' once everything within `NEAR_M` is out; `enterCave` waits on 'near'. The main thread also runs `planCave` itself, for the walk and props.

## 5. Water

Water stands only in a **sink**: a chamber or ordinary cavern every neighbour of which stands `SINK_RISE` (0.3 m) over it, with no sump and no drop leaving it, so nothing leads down out of it. A sink chamber has a dish at even odds; a sink holds its dish filled to under the rim, or is flooded `FLOOD` (0.4-1.6 m) over its floor. About two in three ordinary caverns are pool caverns, 2-4x wider (as room from mouths and other caverns allows, its roof up by the root of that), with a dish 2-4x deeper if a sink. A pool at least `DROWN_DISH` (6 m) deep may open a **drowned passage** onto a higher braid: it leaves the bowl `DROWN_ROOF` under the surface, runs under the dry rim to `DROWN_OUT` of the radius, swims up at `DROWN_RISE` and walks out; she dives to find it. Rivers cut `RIVER_DEPTH` under a long, gentle walk's floor. Up to three tree walks become **sumps**: the passage dips under its water level to the roof and climbs out at `SUMP_GRADE`. The pools' fish are the rivers' own (`render/fish.js`), which main.js points at the cave's beds and water while she is in one. A torch dipped in any water, cave or overworld, goes out with `extinguish`.

**Shores.** `settleWaters` (caves/water.js) floods each pool and sump from its deepest point over the real floor under its planned level, on a 0.5 m grid refined to 0.25 m where the shoreline crosses, so the plane runs into the bank instead of stopping at an ellipse. Two columns join only with air `LIP` over the higher floor, so water does not leak into a passage under rock. The flood is a priority flood within the body's home (node and lobe ellipses × `HOME`, its own passages and nooks padded `PAD`; a sump's line padded `SUMP_HOME`): the first floor under the planned level it reaches outside home is where the water would pour away, and the level settles `GAP` under that sill, so no plane ends in the air. A room's water left shallower than `SHALLOW` at its deepest is dropped; a sump's throws. A pool a sump's water covers takes the sump's level and draws no plane of its own. The plane's cells are merged into greedy rectangles (up to `MERGE_CELLS` across) and its shoreline reads every `SHORE_STRIDE`-th (4) shore sample, since its edge lies under the bank, so a great cavern's pool is about 2k triangles, not hundreds of thousands. `CaveWalk` and the renderer both read the shore grid. A pool is drawn only with its regions and within `CULL_M` of her by its box, as the rock is.

**Reflection.** From above, the water shows a 64 px cube (`MIRROR_SIZE`) the room captures one face a frame, a hand over the water vertex nearest her eye within `MIRROR_REACH` (30 m). The surface is the overworld lake's (`water.js`): Fresnel over a torch-lit body, mostly opaque and clearer looking straight down (`clarity`), rippled by its detail layers alone (`RIPPLE_GLSL`, no swell), with the analytic torch glint on top. The water is hidden from the capture; the torches are not, so the wall she lights shows in it. Underneath, the murk. Under a roof lower than the water, `CaveWalk.waterAt` answers the roof less `ROOF_GAP`, so a swimmer is held under the rock and follows it down and back up instead of floating through it.

**Sumps and the torch.** Under water her view is the blurry murk (`CAVE_MURK`), like a night swim outside, and `underWater` reads the cave's water, so a sump douses the torch and blocks the flint as the overworld's water does. She relights it after surfacing. Both ends of a sump glow with odds 0.85 (others by their region's `glow`), so exits are usually but not always near glowing mushrooms.

## 6. Regions and props

A region is a connected group of nodes with one of seven `PALETTES` (grey rocky, dusty brown, blue-grey mushroom, pale limestone, old ruin, rust red, green damp): rock, vein and floor albedo in the terrain's range, and densities of dripstone, mushrooms, glow, ruins and rubble. Each region's biggest chamber holds its landmark (pillar, arch, giant mushroom, column, colonnade). A pillar or column is a giant stalagmite of the ordinary model, 42-55% of the way to the roof (slim for a column), under a giant stalactite of comparable length.

`placeProps` lays dripstone in bunches of up to eight, sized over a tenfold range, a stalagmite under a stalactite at odds rising with its size and an eighth of them standing alone (one lumpy lopsided 400-triangle model, `stalagmite.js`, its one seed `DRIP_SEED`, pointed up or down, wearing the stone tile turned a quarter from the walls' so its grain runs along it, drawn past `DRIP_NEAR` (20 m) as a baked card spinning about its own axis; each wears the colour and baked glow of the rock it grows from; `/gen-stalagmite` shows model and card), mushrooms (glowing caps twice as common as plain) (the forest's species from `mushroom-bank.js` grown large, wearing the props atlas with caps from `MUSHROOM_CAP_CAVE`, each region's glowing its one colour scaled by the cap texture's luminance so the grain still shows, and small glowing clumps standing out of the walls), blocky ruin columns, and chalk lumps. In a chamber with `FILL_GLOWS` (5) or more glowing caps, a fern grows by about a third of them. A great cavern adds groves of giant glowing mushrooms (1.5 to 12 m, up to `GROVE_LIGHTS` of them lit) off its low ground and pools in its hollows. Boulders lie everywhere: strewn stones (0.3 to 7 m across in a great cavern), rubble heaps of 6-19 stones, boulders protruding from the floor or bulging out of a wall, small stones along passage walls, and pebbles strewn over every floor in scatters (`PEBBLE_M2`, `PEBBLE_STEP`). A stone from `BOULDER_FROM` (1 m) across is the world's boulder on the rock LOD ladder (`ROCK_LOD_AT`), a smaller one litter.js's pebble on litter's reach and thinning; both are re-picked each `DRIP_STEP` she moves and share the colour and glow of the rock surface under them per `STONE_LIT_M` cell (sampled from air, climbed out of the rock first). Props stand on the relieved floor (`reliefAt`). A floor mushroom, or a wall clump on rock that faces up, is planted (`plant`): tilted outward from a slope fitted to its foot's rim, then sunk until its whole base disc (`MUSH_FOOT` of its height across) is in rock. Nothing that grows is placed under a pool's or river's surface. **Anything she cannot step past** (a stalagmite over `MITE_BLOCKS`, 1 m, a column, a giant stem, a boulder over 0.5 m, a wide heap, a protrusion) is an obstacle in `CaveWalk` and is kept clear of every passage line into its node, so dressing cannot close a route the graph promised.

## 7. Walking

`CaveWalk` gives Player the WalkSurface contract read off the field. A cave stacks floors over floors, so every question is asked from a height: her foot height, or `hintY`, which main sets to her feet each frame for the rest. A rock column from over her reach down past her feet is a wall. The gate times a walk step under 50 us. **Flying** in a cave (`_flyPorous`, player.js) treats roofs as porous: she rises through rock and sinks back out of it, but floors and walls still stop her while she is in air, and landing from inside rock first steps her down into air. Her flight speed in a cave is the steady `flyLowSpeed`: a speed that followed the floors under her jumped with every one. A landing with no floor within `landDepth` (100 m) under her is refused with a pop, and she keeps flying. While she flies, `CaveRoom.update` draws every region at no further LOD than the last, without fog, and lights every surface at least `FLY_LIT` (0.1), so the whole layout reads at any distance.

## 8. Drawing, light and chalk

**Light.** Pitch dark: the torches (`uTorch`) are the only light that moves. Every glowing mushroom (a giant's light reaching `9 + 1.5h` m, a floor cap's `3 + 5h`, a wall clump's 5), a fill light in each chamber of `FILL_GLOWS` or more glowing caps (reaching its walls, up to `FILL_REACH` 20 m, brighter the more caps), and the mouths' daylight are baked into a per-vertex glow the cave shader adds unlit (the daylight as a separate `day` scalar times `uDay`), so stretches can be walked by glow alone; a mouth's daylight runs from the sky plane to `DAY_M` (11 m) inside the mouth. A sky plane is drawn only with its region and within `CULL_M` of her, as the rock around it is. Props take the glow and colour of the rock under them (`rockAt`, mesh.js), sampled once when the room is built. Torch shadows are not built; the baked AO carries the depth. The rock wears the boulders' stone tile every `STONE_M` (2.2 m), tinted by its region.

**Chalk lumps** are a hands source (`ChalkStones`, kind `chalk`) over the room's `chalkRows`. Taking one marks it in `taken` (session only).

**Drawing.** In VR a held lump draws where its tip is within `CHALK_TOUCH_M` of rock (`onRock`, two Newton steps on the field, no raycast). On the desktop, a left drag with chalk held draws where the cursor's ray meets rock (`rayRock`, sphere march), and drag-look is suppressed while a stroke is live. A `ChalkPen` per hand resamples every `STEP_M` (3 cm) and starts a new stroke on a jump over `JUMP_M` or at `STROKE_MAX` points.

**Storage.** A stroke is `[system seed, id, player, points]`; points are int16 triples at `QUANT_M` (2 cm) about the system's centre (cx, cz) and cave-local y, base64, 6 bytes a point. Out of range throws rather than wraps. Wall normals and grain are recomputed at draw time, so only points are stored.

**Ribbons.** `ribbons()` lays each stroke as a strip lifted `LIFT_M` off the rock along the field's gradient; the mark shader discards by a grain hash for the dry, broken look, with a polygon offset and no blending. The room rebuilds the marks mesh when the system's rev changes, and the live strokes when they grow.

**Sharing** copies the trust relay. The client keeps every stroke it learns (`Chalk`), saves only hers, and resends them on load and on every welcome, `BATCH` (8) a flush. The relay keeps up to `CHALK_CAP` (4096) per room with a rev, tells each client what it has not seen at most 32 a snapshot, and never echoes. A stroke lasts as long as its author keeps their save.

## 9. Open

- `planCave` runs twice per entry, on the main thread under the fade and again on the worker, so the black lasts both plus the near chunks' meshing; `settleWaters` is 120-200 ms of each, and the props' `rockAt` another ~70 ms on a big system.
- A dripstone's foot on the floor takes the floor palette, which can read browner than a purple or blue wall around it.
- The marks mesh rebuilds whole on every stroke commit; many strokes may hitch.
- Chalk lumps come back on reload (`taken` is session only); things dropped in a cave are not kept once she leaves.
- The arch does not collide; she can walk through its stones. Grass and ferns still grow in the notch.
- Every chunk's finer LODs are held in GPU memory once met.
- Torch shadows.
- The chalk relay needs the server redeployed.
- Not yet felt in a headset: how dark, how lost, VR chalk drawing.
- Spiders and horrors are out of scope; caverns leave room for lairs.
