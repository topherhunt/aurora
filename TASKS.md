- [ ] Wild creatures roaming around, walking or running or flying (songbirds, eagles, deer, mythic creatures). They pause and turn to look at you when you get close

## Props -- follow-ups

The Blender pipeline is built and gated (`npm run props`, `scripts/check-props.mjs`, DESIGN.md §9). 160 assets, 122 texture layers of a guaranteed 256, 78.4k triangles across every LOD, 4.68 MB of GLB. Nothing in the runtime loads any of it yet -- that is the next piece of work, and it is the larger half.

- [ ] **Load `public/props` at runtime.** `src/props/scatter.js` still builds every prop procedurally from `shapes.js`. It needs to fetch the GLBs, pack the LODs into a `BatchedMesh` per material, and switch tiers with `setGeometryIdAt` at the per-class distances the manifest already carries (`lod0_m`, `billboard_m`, `cull_m`).
- [ ] **Build the `uArrAsset` DataArrayTexture** from the 122 layer PNGs (21 albedo, 101 impostor sheets) and give each asset its layer index. The layers are all 128^2 RGBA and the gate enforces it, so the array upload should be mechanical.
- [ ] **Biome-sensitive placement rules.** Phase A already reports bare/pine/mixed/heath/lush per cell; the scatter tables that map biome to species mix and density do not exist yet. §5 says density is the lever the look hangs on, affordable to ~0.2 stems/m^2.
- [ ] **Compression.** No meshopt, no KTX2. 4.68 MB is fine to ship today, but the KTX2 array-texture round-trip is unverified and worth checking before the layer count grows.
- [ ] **Headset gate (§17).** §5's ladder predicts ~4,500 mostly-6-tri instances in view. §0 measured 8,000 instances but at ~190 triangles each -- the opposite regime, geometry-bound rather than instance-bound. The instance count is the untested number, not the triangle count.
- [ ] **Replacement sources for the excluded assets** -- see the shopping list in `tools/props/make-manifest.mjs`. Photoreal card foliage cannot be decimated (every leaf is its own quad, so the mesh is ~100% boundary edges and collapse does nothing at all); it has to be re-authored as cross-cards with a baked canopy, or replaced with game-ready low-poly. Run `tools/props/probe-source.py` on anything new **before** adding it -- boundary fraction plus measured floor called every accept and reject in the second batch without building anything.
- [ ] **The albedo bake comes unstuck from the mesh when the mesh decimates hard.** `consolidate_texture` unwraps and bakes the 128² atlas on the *full-res* object, and the LOD chain is decimated from that object afterwards. Nothing re-checks that the surviving triangles still point at anything, so where decimation succeeds the UVs stretch across the atlas gutters and every fragment that lands in one is discarded at alphaTest 0.5. Measured by sampling each LOD0 triangle at its UV centroid: **11 of the 21 textured assets lose more than a quarter of their triangles, and 5 lose 100%** -- `forest_floor_cluster`, `log_mossy_scan`, `log_birch_scan`, `grass_tall_scan_b`, `grass_tall_scan_c` render as nothing at all, and `grass_tall_scan_a` renders 5%. It tracks decimation depth: the cabins keep 8-18% of their source triangles and are fine, the photoscans keep 0.01-0.6% and are not. This is the same root cause as the seam-cost note below, but the severe half of it, and it is why the props page shows a texture audit per tier.
- [ ] **Every textured asset's impostor sheet is black.** `render_billboard` (build.py:881) renders `lod_objs[0]` *after* `finalize_material` (build.py:838) has swapped the albedo for the 1×1 black stub, and a textured asset's vertex colours are deliberately set to white so the AO multiply is the only thing in them -- so the render has nothing to sample but the stub. Exactly the 9 textured assets that have a billboard are affected (3 cabins, watchtower, windmill, `tree_oak_hero`, `tree_cracked_dead`, `tree_dead_standing`, `tree_deciduous_hi`); all 92 vertex-colour impostors are fine, because their colour is in COLOR_0. Fix is ordering: render the billboard before `finalize_material`, or point the stub swap at a copy.
- [ ] **`windmill`'s albedo bakes pure black.** Separate from the two above: its atlas is 65% opaque but at mean RGB 0, and 88% of its LOD0 triangles sample a black texel. The UVs are fine, the bake is not. Check the source material -- this is the signature of a base colour Cycles cannot read.
- [ ] **Smart UV Project's seams cost the textured props triangles.** The collapse decimator protects UV island boundaries like mesh boundaries, so the same grass tuft reaches 15 triangles before `consolidate_texture` and 83 after (§9). Affects all 21 textured assets. Either decimate before unwrapping (a bake per LOD instead of one shared layer -- an architecture change) or raise the 66° angle limit for fewer islands and measure the atlas cost across the library.
- [ ] **`tree_oak_hero` must be placed sparsely.** It is the one asset whose decimation floors at 1,703 triangles, given the `structure` ladder for that reason. At §5's 0.08 stems/m² it would eat the entire prop budget by itself; it wants to be a landmark, a handful per region.

## Day/night, sky and aurora -- follow-ups

The cycle, the horizon-map shadows, the starfield, the moon and the aurora are built and gated by `scripts/check-daynight.mjs` (see DESIGN.md §8 and §13). What is left:

- [ ] **Nothing in `npm run check` compiles a shader, and one release of the aurora shipped GLSL that did not link.** The redeclaration scan in `check-daynight.mjs` closes the specific hole that caused it, but only that one: a type mismatch, a missing varying, an undeclared identifier or a wrong argument count would all still pass every check in the project and still draw nothing. The real fix is a headless GL context (`gl` or a Playwright page) that links each program once and fails on the info log. Worth doing before the next shader-heavy round.
- [ ] **Headset gate.** Per §17 rule 3, everything here is new, transparent and full-screen -- the aurora, the starfield and the sky dome have never been fill-rate tested on device. Read frametime and worst-frame at 01:00 in-world with the aurora at storm strength and three forms overlaid on top of the always-on quiet-arc floor (press `P` to pin `breakup`, `corona` or `vapour spiral`, which are the three heaviest), which is the worst case the system can produce. Eleven slots, 35,640 triangles, and the two floor slots are never degenerate so they always cost fill. Working against it, the round-four presence envelope multiplies two masks instead of summing them, so a typical frame is substantially darker than round three's and average overdraw is down even though peak is unchanged.
- [ ] **Night brightness is calibrated to a model, not to a headset.** `scripts/check-daynight.mjs` reproduces three's Lambert + hemisphere maths on the CPU and reports sRGB luma; the numbers it targets are a moonlit-photograph look chosen on a desktop monitor. At 01:00, near field: trunk 8, gully rock 16, shaded grass 24, lit grass 67, snow 129, and 4.4:1 slope contrast under a full moon. Far field (past 50 m): lit grass 34, shaded grass 0, snow 70. The knobs are `skyGlowAmt`, `skyFloor`, `hemiIntensity` in the night rows of `KEYS`, plus `MOONLIGHT.intensity` and the two new `farDirect`/`farAmbient` columns; the gate re-measures whatever they are set to.
- [ ] **The near-field lighting envelope is a moving brightness gradient centred on the player, and nothing in nature does that.** §8 round five: ambient is faded out between 25 m and 50 m, so shaded ground goes luma 24 to 0 across a 25 m annulus that travels with her. On a monitor that reads as a soft pool of light; in stereo, where the ground plane is a strong depth cue, it may read as a spotlight following her. The radii are `WL_NEAR_M`/`WL_FAR_M` in `lighting.js` and the strengths are `farDirect`/`farAmbient` in `clock.js`. If it is wrong, the likely fixes in order are: widen the transition (25/50 -> 20/80), or give `farAmbient` a small non-zero floor at the cost of some far-field slope contrast.
- [ ] **Night fog went from 0.0022 to 0.00032 and now needs a look on device.** Round six: the heavy night fog was the reported "pitch-black past 500 m", and it is gone -- 10% at a kilometre instead of 99%. What that exposes is everything the fog used to hide: the far terrain LOD seams, the props' billboard tier, the chunk edges, and whatever the horizon map does at grazing angles are all visible at 1-3 km at night now. Judge those before adding any density back, because the fog was covering for them and it should not be asked to again.
- [ ] **Night distance fog is a guess at where "beyond your vicinity" starts.** `fogDensity` at full dark went 0.0004 -> 0.0022 against a near-black fog colour, which puts 300 m at 35% and 600 m at 82%. Nothing has judged that in stereo, where depth cues are real and a fog that reads as atmospheric on a monitor can read as a wall. The specific thing to check on device is whether a *lit* village across a valley still reads as being across a valley -- `flameMat` is exempted from fog for exactly this reason, but the buildings around it are not.
- [ ] **Village windows and fires do not yet respond to the clock**, and night fog makes that more visible rather than less: the one thing guaranteed to stay bright at 800 m is a fire, so a village with no lit windows now reads as a cluster of hearths floating in the dark. (Same item as the one further down; noted here because round four changed its priority.)
- [ ] **The aurora does not light the world's geometry, only its ambient.** `state()` tints `hemiSky` green and lifts `skyGlowAmt` with aurora strength, so a storm does brighten the ground -- but uniformly, with no direction. A curtain overhead and a curtain on the northern horizon light the scene identically. A cheap fix would be a second weak directional light aimed at the brightest live band.
- [ ] The drifting cloud layer from §13 (two scrolling alpha-blended layers on the dome). Not built.
- [ ] Per-chunk fine horizon maps (§8). Deliberately skipped -- the 1024^2 global tier plus the AO bake carries it. Revisit only if crevice-scale shadowing looks flat on device.
- [ ] Aurora reflections in water. `src/water.js` samples the sky colour, not the aurora, so a lake under a full storm stays dark.
- [ ] Village window and fire light does not respond to the clock. `flameMat` is deliberately unshadowed so it stays bright at night, but nothing lights up *at dusk* -- windows should come on as the sun goes down.
- [ ] The three vortex forms (`vapour spiral`, `rising column`, `flaming aurora`) now carry `twist` as well as `shear` -- the footprint azimuth actually rotates with altitude, so they have a near limb and a far limb. That is the piece stereo should sell and the piece a monitor cannot. Judge them on device before adding a fourth; the twist magnitudes (0.14 to 0.62 deg/km) were picked to look right in mono and are the most likely thing to be wrong.
- [ ] **Bands now reach 350 km altitude to stay large-looking, and the colour ramp was not designed for that.** Round six raised `alt1` across the catalogue (top out at 350 km on `rayed band`) so a low band could still subtend 20 degrees. But `col` goes fully to OI 630.0 magenta above 235 km, so every tall band now has a red-to-magenta top, gated only by its own `crown`. That is right for a rayed band and probably too much for the quiet arc, which is why its `crown` was cut to 0.3. Judge on device whether an ordinary night reads green with a dusky top or pink.
- [ ] **`curl` was tuned against a reversal count, not against a look.** §13 round five: the gate walks each footprint and counts bearing reversals, and the per-form `curl` values were picked to land each form in a target band (quiet arc 5, drapery 18, breakup 28, STEVE 0.3). That measurement is honest about *whether* a band folds back; it says nothing about whether the folds are the right size or read as curtain rather than as ribbon-tangle. `breakup` at 28 reversals per frame is the one most likely to be too much. The knob is `curl` per band and the gate re-measures it.
- [ ] **The aurora shell means the aurora is now closer than the far plane but further than everything else, and that has never been fill-rate tested.** Bands at 1,180 km used to be impossible; they now cost the same as bands at 250 km, and low arcs subtend a wide, shallow strip along the horizon where the terrain silhouette is busiest. Worst case to measure is the same as the headset-gate item above.
- [ ] **The curvature drop assumes she is standing at sea level on a sphere.** `drop = d^2 / 12742` is applied per band with no reference to her altitude, so climbing a 600 m ridge does not extend her horizon and does not raise a distant arc. Correct behaviour is that it should -- a 600 m viewpoint pushes the geometric horizon out by ~87 km. Cheap to add (subtract `sqrt(2 R h)`-worth of drop) and worth doing only if a mountaintop at night ever feels wrong.
- [ ] Four forms are retired rather than deleted (`diffuse patches`, `pulsating patches`, `SAR arc`, `smoke plume`), each with a `retired:` reason in `aurora-patterns.js`. They still get swept by the gate's geometry checks, so restoring one is a one-line change -- but each was retired for a look reason that a headset might overturn, particularly `smoke plume`, which was cut for being static rather than for being wrong.
- [ ] Substorm activity is a pure function of in-world time (`clock.js`, `AURORA_ACTIVITY`). When §10 weather exists, cloud cover should gate it -- an overcast night should hide the aurora entirely.
