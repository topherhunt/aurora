FLUX.2 Klein 4B via OpenRouter. The prompt orders each leaf mass opaque but the masses apart, real proportions, and a trunk clause per species -- one closed canopy comes back as modelling clay.

3. mesh

Tripo image-to-model on the picked candidate, at a prop's face count rather than a hero's.
Off by default: every face is repainted downstream, so a bought atlas is generated and then discarded. Turn it on only to judge whether the reconstruction is right before deciding the paint job is wrong.

4. decimate

src/mesh/decimate.js, here in the tab. Free and re-runnable. Runs in drop mode: Tripo's unwrap is thrown away by the paint stage anyway, so there are no seams to pin and no reduction ceiling.

5. paint

Click or drag on the model to paint the active slot. UVs are projected from world position, so texel size is constant and the texture tiles -- nothing is unwrapped.

slots — what each painted region wears, and how it wraps

planar projects off each face's dominant world axis, which is what every rock in this world already wears -- right for anything without a direction (leaf mats, moss, stone). cylindrical wraps about the vertical axis through the slot's own faces, for the one texture that has one: bark runs up. Its repeat count around the trunk is rounded to a whole number, because a fractional count is a visible stripe down one side, so the size it achieves differs from the size you asked for by a few percent -- the achieved number is in the table.

this bench

Two orange buttons and nothing fires on its own. The image step bills OpenRouter (~$0.015); the mesh step bills Tripo credits at $0.01 each. Decimating and painting are free and local and can be re-run until they look right.

Why solid. Alpha-tested foliage costs the whole draw its low-resolution-Z on Adreno, which halves the frame rate on a Quest 2 -- the reason a generated tree that looks superb on a desktop cannot ship. Every stage here exists to end up with an opaque shell. But solidity is asked for one level down, per leaf mass rather than per tree: ordering the whole canopy closed buys a smooth symmetrical lump that reads as clay. Clumps opaque, clumps apart, and nothing in the tree thinner than a finger.

Why projection, not unwrap. A solved unwrap scatters into small islands and a 128px texture over small islands is mush. Here a face's UV is its world position divided by how big one tile is, so texel density is constant, there are no islands and no gutters, and the atlas's RepeatWrapping does the tiling. It is also why the decimator has no ceiling: with the unwrap discarded there are no seam vertices left to pin.

Working files land in tools/trees/v9/work/<id>/ (gitignored). Tripo's result URLs expire, so the mesh step downloads before it returns; the task ids in state.json are the only handle on work already paid for.
