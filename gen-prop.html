Our decimator (src/mesh/decimate.js) on the selected mesh candidate — click one above to switch. Free, local, re-runnable. LOD tiers

        

        

          Click a tier to load it into the preview; the camera holds its angle across the swap, so
          the silhouette is comparable frame to frame. Tiers belong to the mesh candidate they were
          decimated from -- selecting a different candidate shows that one's ladder.
        

        

          A tier that stops well short of its target is not a bug in the decimator: seam and
          boundary vertices are pinned, so the reduction ceiling is a direct measurement of how
          few, how large and how well-closed this mesh's UV islands are -- the same property that
          decides whether the texture survives 128px.
        

      

    


    

      
this bench

      

        Every orange button spends real money and nothing fires on its own. The image step bills
        OpenRouter (~$0.015); the mesh step bills Tripo credits at $0.01 each. Everything after the
        mesh -- LOD tiers and the card cross -- is cut here, for free, as many times as you like.
      

      

        Working files land in tools/props/gen/work/<id>/ (gitignored). Tripo's result
        URLs expire, so the mesh step downloads its output to disk before returning; the task ids in
        state.json are the only handle on work already paid for.
      

      

        A prop is judged at sixty metres. Most of a prop's screen life is the card
        cross and the coarsest tier, so bake the cards early and look at them before spending on a
        second mesh. The texture panel is the gate on the near tiers, as it is for creatures: P1's
        native quads give fewer, larger UV islands that survive the 128px downrez.
