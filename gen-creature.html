A tier that stops well short of its target is not a bug in the decimator: seam and boundary vertices are pinned, so the reduction ceiling is a direct measurement of how few, how large and how well-closed this mesh's UV islands are -- the same property that decides whether the texture survives 128px.

this bench

Every orange button spends real money and nothing fires on its own. The image step bills OpenRouter (~$0.015); the mesh, rig and animation steps bill Tripo credits at $0.01 each. rig-check is free and is the only way to find out a mesh cannot be rigged without paying 25 credits to discover it -- always run it first.

Working files land in tools/creatures/work/<id>/ (gitignored). Tripo's result URLs expire, so each step downloads its output to disk before returning; the task ids in state.json are the only handle on work already paid for.

Why P1 is the default. The texture panel is the real gate on this pipeline, not the triangle count. A decimated mesh gets auto-unwrapped into a scatter of small UV islands, and at 128px the small ones vanish while bilinear filtering bleeds neighbours across every seam. P1 generates native quad topology with coherent edge flow, so the islands come out fewer and larger and survive the downrez. Judge a candidate on the 128px panel, not the full-size one.
