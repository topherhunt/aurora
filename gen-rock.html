the LOD ladder

Every tier is the same displacement field sampled on a coarser solid, so a tier change loses corners rather than swapping in a different rock. There is one ladder and every rock is on it -- what differs between a cobble and a tor is not which tiers they ship but the DISTANCES, which are ROCK_LOD_AT metres per metre of measured width. The ranges above are this rock's. The greyed model figure beside each is where that tier stops earning its triangles at ~3 px each on a Quest 2 eye buffer (16.2 px/°); shipping inside it is a deliberate choice to spend less.

can it be a card?

A billboard's defect is parallax: it refuses to turn, and the error that leaves is an angle. Under ~2° it stops reading as wrong at walking pace, which puts the crossover at depth × 28.6. A rock is a much worse candidate than a fern -- it is an opaque convex lump, so two crossed planes show their intersection as a visible X, and a single plane vanishes edge-on. The number to beat is the handover distance above: if the coarsest mesh is still honest out to further than the crossover, the card is not earning its layer.

the tint palette

Every rock in the world wears the one 128² granite tile. These are per-instance colours, applied through BatchedMesh.setColorAt -- which the shared prop material already honours, so all six are one material and one draw call. A tint hands you hue and value for free; what it cannot change is grain, and that is the test a second stone tile would have to pass.
