the LOD ladder

Every sampled tier is the same displacement field sampled on a coarser solid, so a tier change loses corners rather than swapping in a different rock; the last rung, T6, is five vertices fitted to the measured box with the mesh's silhouette, and it is what every rock is from there out. There is one ladder and every rock is on it -- what differs between a cobble and a tor is not which tiers they ship but the DISTANCES: the ×n on each row is ROCK_LOD_AT, metres of camera distance per metre of the ladder size named above, and the metres are that product for this rock. The greyed model figure is where that tier stops earning its triangles at ~3 px each on a Quest 2 eye buffer (16.2 px/°); shipping inside it is a deliberate choice to spend less. LOD ladder lays the four out left to right, finest first; world LOD walks them by distance, and there a row is a button that parks the camera just past its boundary.

the tint palette

Every rock in the world wears the one 128² granite tile. These are per-instance colours, applied through BatchedMesh.setColorAt -- which the shared prop material already honours, so all six are one material and one draw call. A tint hands you hue and value for free; what it cannot change is grain, and that is the test a second stone tile would have to pass.
