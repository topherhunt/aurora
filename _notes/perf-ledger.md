# Quest 80 FPS ledger

The standing record of what the trace battery has established. Read this before proposing a perf experiment. Edit facts in place; per-trace chronology lives in `2026-10-05-perf-trace-findings.md` and `design/history/`.

Goal: 80 Hz on Quest 2 in every environment (12.5 ms budget; 90 Hz is 11.1 ms, so a CPU-bound frame snaps to 22 ms). `main.js` asks the session for 80 Hz at `sessionstart` (`?hz=` overrides); the trace header's `xr` line shows the rate actually granted.

## How to measure
- Pull with `bash ./devops/fetch-traces.sh`, read with `node scripts/trace-report.mjs <id>`. The battery is `src/v2/perf-suite.js`; it must stay near two minutes (the user will not sit through seven).
- `calls` are two-eye sums. `gpuMs` is null on this headset. `waitMs` includes idle to vsync. "cpu" (js + rend) is the smooth column; "saved" steps in jumps.
- The battery drifts thermally (about 19 to 23.6 ms over a run). A single state is good to about ±1 to 2 ms. Trust only a group that repeats across both rounds (spread under about 1 ms); baselines are interpolated per state.
- The battery's second step is a census (`src/v2/perf-census.js`, printed as "per-layer census"): with everything on, per layer per frame, `update()` ms, main/offscreen/shadow draws, instances, material and program switches, attribute uploads (upper bound in KB). It explains a group's saved ms; hobs, villagers and leafkin appear there whether or not they have a toggle. Baked-puppet draws are shared and count under `baked`.
- Census checks: `empty` (draws with 0 instances) must be 0 -- three issues the draw anyway, so every count-driven mesh hides itself at 0 (`mesh.visible = count > 0`; `InstancedArena` drops its layer mask; `TriFlames.update`); trace-report prints `!!` for any layer that does not. `upl` is attribute uploads a frame: a still scene should write about nothing, trace-report lists layers at 1 or more.
- Recordings and ambient captures show per-window stages, `wait`, uploads and the worst frames, plus `parts`: builds per scheduler source (`trees`, `rocks.<bed>`, ...) and animals per layer, as mean / slowest frame; a recording also runs the census over its first window (marked `census hooks on`). Use them for hitches and flight, the battery for steady-state cost.

Across the 11 baselines of T20-08-49, frame ms rises 0.14 ms per upload call (r 0.75, confounded with NPC count and draws); mean frame is cpu + 8 to 9 ms in every state, so the wait is not plain slack (GPU utilisation needs the OVR Metrics Tool). The 80 Hz request did not take (header still 90 Hz); `main.js` now logs the granted rate.
- scatter 3.8 / 3.2 (ladder: boulders 1.5, grass+trees 1.5, litter 0.6, ferns about 0)
- animals 1.8 / 2.9 (drills: insects 0.5, water critters noise; stage lap animals 0.7 to 1.2)
- village 1.8 / 0.4 (townsfolk drill 2.0 with 16 calls and 13 upload calls)
- terrain 1.9 / 1.4; water, sky+fire 0.2 to 1.8 and not repeatable
- Ambient town (12:09): js 6 to 8 + rend 9 = 15 to 17 ms cpu, worse than the battery spot. Forest (08:30): rend 7.3 to 8.5, grass 1.3 and rocks 0.8 mean.

## Tile placement budget
`BuildScheduler` (build-budget.js, `v2build`) drains every layer's tile queue by distance ring (40 m, doubling), then layer rank (trees, boulders, ferns, grass, litter), then distance; a job's distance is to the nearest point of its tile and each queue is scanned over its last 512 entries, because layers append level-change jobs unsorted. `BUILD_MS = 5` in `main.js` caps the frame at 5 ms of builds (Infinity is the slideshow: record 2026-10-11T06-39-44, Quest 2, `builds` 250 to 840 ms a frame in flight; 1 gave 0.9 ms mean on Quest 3, 2026-10-10T21-18-01). A dear tile borrows against up to 6 frames of debt. Layers have `buildBudgetMs = 0` once it owns them. Single tiles can still overrun: grass up to 3 to 4 ms desktop, litter 2.1, ferns 1.1, rocks 1.0; a tile is the unit of work and is not sliced (owner's call). Trees and rocks add `eyeLift` squared to the thinning and near-set distances of tiles within `LIFT_REACH` (tile-pool.js): aloft, about half the instances (trees 3684 vs 6920 at 300 m, rocks 105 vs 229 at 200 m) and no visible one lost (`check-trees`, `check-rocks` "from the air"). Prebaking full-density placement (`tmp/size-bake.mjs`, real world, desktop, level 0 per tile): rocks 0.52 M records (boulders 0.48 ms/tile, scree 0.13, embedded 0.24, giants 0.24, hollow 0.32, sunken 0.05, shore 0.03), trees 1.08 M at 0.044 ms/tile; `heightAt` (0.8 us) is 25 to 52 calls a rock tile, so a per-tile height cache saves at most about 40 us of a boulder tile.

## Boulders standing still
The cost was a re-queue loop: `thicken = near2 < loSq[q]` is always true at level 0, so every nearby tile was queued each walk and `_growTile` returned early (331 no-op calls per 90 still frames in rocks). Fixed with `q > 0 &&` in all seven tile layers. What remains of rocks while still is small (`blockTopAt`/`columnAt` about 0.03 ms each desktop) and mostly other systems asking: butterflies `_wander`, `player.flySpeedAt`, townsfolk. The `_nearestInto` path queries (0.1 ms/frame desktop) come from `ambience.js` `sense.sample -> lakeShore` and `_rivers`, not from rocks.

## Reported, not yet investigated

Which creature is unknown; the next flight recording names it (`animals` parts). Part of the walk cost was two rules pulling far buckets off their turn: the camera covering NEAR_MARGIN since a bucket's walk (every bucket every 3 to 5 frames past ~300 m/s) and the rim's `need` rising by any amount (millimetre step jitter, float rounding: 27 to 102% of tiles a frame at 300 m up, `tmp/walk-jitter.mjs`). Now `_walkNear` looks up only the tiles around the near set, and a bucket is forced only past the quantised slack it was walked at: 15 to 21% (`check-trees` "the walk in flight"). Unmeasured on the headset.
- A forest-looking spot with birdsong showed no trees nearby after a long flight: either a treeless spot or the tree queue starved; check with `tmp/sched-order.mjs` style probes at that spot.

## Hitches (they cost more than the means)
- `probes` 4 to 5 ms on most windows in town and forest; `grass` 3 to 6 ms and `rocks` 2 to 7 ms tile growth while moving; `ferns` 2 to 4.5; `pebbles` 5 to 6 ms in the forest; a 79 ms `sky+ambience` frame; 15.9 ms `animals` spike; `other` (GC, A-Frame, GPU wait) 10 to 78 ms on single frames.

## Falsified (do not retry)
- Halving litter, grass or the card pool: saves 0 within noise. Dropping the alpha discard on litter or grass: 0 within noise. Wind off: 0 or negative.
- Upload bytes: about 50 KB standing and 1 MB walking per frame. Not the cost.
- Per-triangle cost: pebbles at 56k tris cost 0.3 to 0.8; mushrooms at 500 tris and sticks at 4.5k cost 0.3 to 0.6. The earlier "sticks and mushrooms 1 ms each" did not repeat.
- Hiding empty instanced meshes (`PropMeshes` hides at `count 0`): baseline unchanged (22.3 ms before and after, render 8.4 vs 8.2). Three does still run `setProgram` and the VAO setup for a visible mesh with `count 0` (`renderBufferDirect`), but it is not the lever.
- Fill: `fb 0.7` changed nothing in an earlier night trace.
- Fireflies: 0.8 in one trace, -0.8 in the next. Noise.

## What the frame is made of (Quest 2, town, 2026-10-11 battery)
Baseline 20.4 ms: js 4.3, rend 6.5, wait 9.7. Across every A/B group the ms a layer saves tracks its draw calls (about 0.12 ms per two-eye call: scatter 32 calls 3.4 ms, village 17 calls 2.7, litter parts 6 calls 1.1, water 2 calls 0.25) and not its triangles (terrain 82k tris in 1 call saves 0). The per-layer GL census (`gl`, `gl/draw` in the report; desktop probe `tmp/census-probe.mjs`) explains it: a draw costs 12 to 50 WebGL commands (program setup, about 7 `uniform*`, texture binds, VAO bind, state toggles), about 500 a frame per eye, uniform calls the largest kind (53%). rend 6.5 ms is the main thread issuing roughly 1000 commands (two eyes), and the 9.7 ms wait is plausibly the GPU process draining them: frame ms scales with command count, floor (24 calls) sits at vsync. Quest census (2026-10-11T16-18): 86 draws, 1052 GL calls a frame (633 `uniform*`, 87 `bindVertexArray`, 81 `useProgram`, 70 `bindTexture`), 80 of 86 draws switch program; rend 6.5 ms is about 6 us a call, and mean frame is cpu + 8.5 to 9.8 ms in every state, so every cpu ms is a frame ms. The lever is fewer commands a frame: `?multiview` (one pass for both eyes, halves them; opt-in; on the Quest 2 it ran smooth but terrain was missing and rocks/fern cards were junk: A-Frame's three reads `gl_ViewID_OVR` in fragment shaders under `GL_OVR_multiview` v1, so every material reading `viewMatrix` in its fragment stage breaks; `render/multiview-shim.js` moves that read to the vertex stage and the trace context lists `failedPrograms`; unverified in the headset), then merging draws (baked 12 draws, group 10, rocks 8, trees 6, strider 6, entrance 6) and cutting per-draw state. JS (js 4.3) is the smaller share of the frame.

## Per-draw state cuts (mandate: fewer WebGL calls a frame)
- `src/uniform-block.js`: `lighting.useBlock(renderer)` puts every non-sampler `WorldLight` uniform (sun/sky, night lift, air, lamps, torches, hearth glow, caustics) in one std140 UBO, packed from the same `{ value }` objects and uploaded once per change; `renderer.render` is wrapped to sync first. Verified on desktop: driver offsets and size match, no failed programs. Pages that never call `useBlock` keep plain uniforms.
- `src/uniform-latch.js`: sun, hemisphere and fog creep every frame and three caches per program, so each creep was re-sent ~16 times; the latch holds them until the drift passes 0.4% (colours/intensities) or 5e-4 (sun position). Left: view-space light direction/colour change with head motion and cost one call per program.
- `main.js` `setOpaqueSort`: grouped by program id before material id.
- Desktop census (one eye, town): `uniform*` 277 to 115 a frame, `useProgram` 35 to 29.5. Unmeasured on the headset.
- Next: merge LOD-tier meshes (rocks t0..t3, ferns lod0/lod2, wildlife cards per species) into one draw; UBO the remaining per-frame scalars (`uPropClock`, `uTime`).

## Open, ranked by evidence
1. Townsfolk: 2 to 3 ms, 21 small upload calls (`baked-puppet`, `v2-strider-reins`). `flushBaked` now re-uploads a batch's matrix, colour, rows or fade buffer only when this frame changed it (unmeasured on the headset). The full autopilot (per-instance p0, velocity, t0, clip phase and rate, a time uniform in the VAT shader, CPU rewriting an instance only when its phrase changes) is not built; it also serves animals (item 4).
2. Probes, grass and rocks tile-growth hitches while moving; a shared per-tile height/path cache (`heightAt`, `carveRivers`, `_nearestInto` dominate); gate litter loops on camera movement.
3. Boulders about 1.5 ms (10 calls, draw side); throttle the ambience `lakeShore` samples that feed `_nearestInto`.
4. Animals (desktop headless `update()` ms standing still, `tmp/creature-cost.mjs`: townsfolk 0.37, butterflies 0.12, dragons 0.08, wildlife 0.07, wild striders 0.06, crabs 0.04, frogs 0.03, spiders 0.03, grasshoppers now about 0 (GPU motion), leafkin and fireflies about 0 by day; Quest is 3 to 4x that). GPU motion exists as `render/gpu-motion.js` (`PhraseTable`: one RGBA32F row of closed-form phrases a creature, a binary search against the room clock in the vertex shader, the CPU rewriting a row only when its 8 s segment expires) and grasshoppers use it (parity 1.1e-4 against the CPU twin, 0 uploads and 0 empty draws standing still). It fits creatures whose motion is closed-form per segment. Not converted, and why: butterflies, dragons, wildlife, fish, snowmen integrate noisy 20 Hz phrases on the CPU, so the GPU would only save the matrix compose and upload, not the integration; crabs and spiders cost 0.03 to 0.04; frogs have LOD tiers and live chase phrases; every baked-puppet layer (townsfolk first) runs per-frame foot IK and has bones read on the CPU for tools and reins, so it needs the VAT autopilot (p0, v, t0, clip phase and rate per instance, a time uniform) plus a CPU-side fallback for held and IK'd instances. Far wildlife still walks every spawn each frame and refills its card buffers; persistent card slots with swap-remove and a coarse distance timer are the cheap next step.
5. A SharedTri pool for distant single trees.

## Structural facts
- `LitterCards` is the shared far-card pool (12,288 instances; real use 5 to 6k). Clumps in it get no snow whitening and no wind; mipmaps are on for all users.
- `PropMeshes` and `PropArena` use swap-remove packing and range uploads, and every `PropArena` setter skips a write whose value is already in the buffer (the shared litter-card pool was re-uploaded every frame by bridges, striders and crabs rewriting identical values); `InstancedArena` (instanced-arena.js, used by grass) tracks dirty ranges. three's ranged `bufferSubData` passes the whole array, so count `length * BYTES_PER_ELEMENT` (`gl-uploads.js` does).
- Battery A/B rows (`cardsHalf`, `litterDiscard`, `grassHalf`, `litterPebbles`, and so on) are trace-only toggles in `main.js`; the helpers are `src/v2/render/ab-switch.js`.
