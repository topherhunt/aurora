# Creature sync: every client sees the same creature doing the same thing

**Goal.** When one person looks up and says "a dragon", the other looks up and sees the same dragon at the same place, flying the same way, at the same moment. A late joiner, or someone who flies back into range, meets the creatures exactly as the others see them. Interactions (a fish in her hand menaced by a dragon, a carrot walking a stag after her, a spider luring fish and frogs, later a leafkin startled into dropping its mushrooms, an NPC's mood) reach every client cheaply: nothing per frame, nothing that grows without bound, and a resolution of one second at the coarsest.

**Where it stands.** On the score and over the relay: dragons (`render/dragons.js`), wildlife (`render/wildlife.js`), frogs (`render/frogs.js`), fish (`render/fish.js`), routed by `creature-net.js`. Still on local time: butterflies, spiders, crabs, grasshoppers, snowmen, the leafkin (`render/leafkin.js`, a peer's work in progress); NPC moods are not built.

**Non-goal.** Bit-exact lockstep. Two engines integrating the same numbers can differ in the last ulp; the design re-anchors often enough that no drift can accumulate into something the eye reads.

## The mental model

1. **Creature time is the room's clock.** `WorldClock.seconds` is real seconds since the relay's `anchorMs` plus 60 s per skipped world hour (`skipHours`), so every client in a room reads the same number off `Date.now()` with nothing sent per frame. Solo play uses the local anchor. Nothing about a creature is ever timed from a local timestamp, a frame count or a tile's entry.

2. **A creature's life is a score: chapters of phrases.** Time is cut into fixed chapters (`CHAPTER_S`, ten minutes, each creature's grid offset by its key's hash so a valley does not turn over at once). Inside a chapter the creature plays a chain of phrases rolled from `hash(key, chapter, phraseIndex)`. Every phrase carries a closed-form duration and a closed-form end pose, computed from plan data and deterministic world inputs (height field, water, roost plane, herd tiles), never from integrated motion. The last phrase of every chapter ends at the creature's home pose, so at the chapter turn every creature is home and the next chapter starts from nothing.

3. **Inside a phrase, motion is a fixed-step integrator on world time.** `TICK_HZ` (20) steps per world second, each the creature's own rate-limited flyer or walker fed by its own per-phrase PRNG. The render frame interpolates between the last two ticks. Over the phrase's last `EASE_S` seconds the integrated pose is blended onto the planned end pose, and at the boundary it is exactly that pose: the chain of phrases is the truth, the integrator only fills in the motion between two points that every client already agrees on.

4. **Decisions are made on time, never on integrated position.** A dragon does not "reach" a spot and then decide; the plan says it loiters over the spot until `phrase.end` and then the next phrase begins. A stag does not walk until it "arrives"; its walk is a phrase from one pose to another at its gait, and the plan sized it. A hunt's strike is a moment in both the dragon's and the stag's plans.

5. **What a creature may read during a phrase:** the phrase, its own PRNG, the height field and water (pure functions of position), the clock's dayness, the relayed poses of players. What it may never read: its LOD tier, the frame count, whether it is drawn, wall time, another creature's live integrated position (only that creature's *scheduled* pose, `poseAt(T)`), Map iteration order, a shared PRNG stream.

6. **Live mode is a creature reacting to a player.** Lured, menacing, fleeing. It has nothing to do with residency, animation or visibility; the vast majority of creatures are never live. A lure is an input every client already has (peer poses at 20 Hz in the pose message, held slots through the `hold` messages), so the follow rule runs locally on every client against relayed data and sends nothing. What a live creature sends is an **anchor**.

7. **Anchors replace; nothing appends.** An anchor is the smallest state sample that lets any client resume the creature: `[key, T, x, y, z, heading, phraseIndex, mode, by, ...layerFields]`. The authority for a live creature (the lurer's client; two lurers, the lowest client id) publishes one per second while the creature is live and one final anchor (`mode: "rejoin"`) when the lure ends. The relay keeps the latest per key, drops any a chapter old, and caps the map (`ROOM_ANCHORS_CAP`). A creature no anchor names is on its plan.

8. **Rejoining the plan is itself a phrase.** After a lure ends the creature is somewhere the plan never put it. The final anchor's pose and time define a rejoin phrase, closed-form like any other: go home at the travel rate with slack, then rest until the next plan phrase that starts from home. Every client derives it from the same anchor, so no more traffic follows.

9. **Swarms have no per-creature anchors.** Fish, frogs, butterflies, spiders, crabs, grasshoppers: a bed is a pure function of its tile (critters.js `tileSeed`), and each creature's life is cut into segments of `GRID_S` (eight seconds) on a grid offset by its key's hash, with a pose at every segment boundary that is closed-form in `(key, segment)`. Two shapes of segment exist. A **frog** is closed-form throughout: a segment runs from one post to the next, the bout between them laid at plan time (hops as parabolas), so its pose at any second is arithmetic and a frog out of view costs one plan a segment. A **fish** is integrated: its school's anchor is a chain of hops walked closed-form from the chapter start (`Fish._anchorAt`), each fish steps at `TICK_HZ` from `phraseRand(key, segment)`, is eased onto its station over the segment's last `EASE_S` and put back on it at the boundary, so what one client integrates never strays from another's for longer than a segment, and a joiner replays at most a segment. A lure is published per bed as a lured set, `[bedKey, layer, by, indices]`, sent by the lurer on change and at most once a second per bed (`LURED_EVERY_S`), an empty set sent at once when the last lured creature is let go, and a set held by a client only until it leaves the bed or the entry expires at the relay. A peer's set keeps the named creatures to that peer's lure from `LURE_M` on; without it a peer's lure is noticed only at `LURE_M` like her own. When the lure ends a swarm creature goes back to its next station and sends nothing. Accepted gaps: a client spawning the bed while a lure holds sees the plan pose until the set lands; a fish let go near a boundary is snapped to its station at the turn; a glimmerfin fright straddling a turn is cut there.

## The spawn procedure

Any client, at any time, for a creature with key K at world time T:

1. Look K up in the room's creature anchors (sent in snapshots like `boats`). None, or one older than the current chapter's start: the creature is on its plan. Walk the chapter's phrases from the chapter start to T (each phrase's duration is closed-form, so this is a sum, not a simulation), place the creature at the phrase's start pose, and replay fixed steps from the phrase start to T. The replay is bounded by one phrase and is spread over frames at `CATCH_UP_TICKS` per frame.
2. Anchor with `mode: "rejoin"`: place at the anchor's pose, build the rejoin phrase, and replay fixed steps from the anchor's T.
3. Anchor with a live mode: place at the anchor's pose and run the follow rule against `by`'s relayed pose, correcting toward each new anchor over `CORRECT_S` the way a boat rider's peers do.

## Contract for a new creature layer

- Key every creature by something every client agrees on: the roost's tile key, a herd's tile seed and index, a bed's key and slot index.
- Plan phrases with `score.js`: `phrases(key, chapter)` returns the chapter's list, each `{ kind, dur, to, ... }` with `dur` and `to` closed-form. Sum durations to find the phrase at T. Keep every plan input a pure function of position and time.
- Step with `score.js`'s `stepTo(rec, now, fn)`: it runs `fn(tick)` at `TICK_HZ` on absolute ticks from the record's last tick to `now`, bounded by `CATCH_UP_TICKS` a call, and sets `rec.alpha` for the render to blend the last two ticks by. A phrase is entered on `tickAfter(start)`; a joiner placed at a phrase's start pose sets `rec.tick` to the tick before that. Compute a phrase's end as the next phrase's start by the same sum, so the two agree to the bit. Roll motion noise from `phraseRand(key, chapter, index)`, never from the layer's shared PRNG.
- Ease onto `to` over the phrase's last `EASE_S` seconds -- a rate-capped pull, so a body that is far is not yanked -- and snap at the boundary. Every pose carries `speed`, so a joiner placed at a start pose steps from the same state a resident had there.
- Live: detect the lure locally from relayed inputs; if this client is the authority, publish an anchor every `ANCHOR_S` and a rejoin anchor when the lure ends; if not, apply incoming anchors as corrections. Everything else in live mode is the same follow rule on every client.
- Register the layer with `CreatureNet.add(layer, prefixes)` (creature-net.js) after the dragons boot in main.js: an animal layer gives `pending(into)` and `apply(anchor, now)`, a swarm layer `pendingLured(into)` and `applyLured(set)`, and the prefixes are the first words of its keys, one owner each. In use: wildlife `st`, `fx`, `hr`; dragons `dr`; frogs `fg`; fish `fs`.
- Gate it with two instances stepped on different dt sequences, one joining mid-chapter, and assert identical poses at every phrase boundary (`check-dragons`, `check-wildlife`, `check-frogs`, `check-fish` each do; `check-creature-net` and `check-net` cover the routing and the relay).

## Relay schema

- `anchors: Map<key, anchor>` per room, `anchor = [key, T, x, y, z, heading, phraseIndex, mode, by, ...layerFields]`, validated on receipt (key in `[a-z0-9:,-]`, `T` finite and within `[room seconds - CHAPTER_S, room seconds + ANCHOR_AHEAD_S (60)]`, x/y/z/heading finite, `phraseIndex` an integer, `mode` a short word, `by` null from the client and stamped with the sender's id, layer fields null or finite, at most 24 fields and `ANCHOR_MAX_JSON` 256 bytes), replaced on every write, delivered to a client only when changed since it last heard (like `things`, and never the sender's own), whole map to a newcomer.
- `lured: Map<layer/bedKey, set>` per room, `set = [bedKey, layer, by, indices]` (up to 64 non-negative integers), same delivery. An empty set stands until it expires: it is the tombstone that tells a late client nothing in that bed is lured.
- Expiry: the relay stamps each entry with the room seconds it arrived at and on every tick drops those a `CHAPTER_S` old; a client rejects anything before the creature's own chapter start exactly, so the relay only bounds memory. `ROOM_ANCHORS_CAP` (256) and `ROOM_LURED_CAP` (64) forget the entry written longest ago. Nothing tells a client of a deletion: what it holds it drops on its own at the chapter turn.
- Messages: `{ type: "anchor", anchor }` and `{ type: "lured", set }` from clients; the snapshot carries `creatures: { anchors: [...], lured: [...] }` when anything changed.

## Costs

Per creature per second on the wire: zero unless live; live, one anchor of under 200 bytes. Per bed: one lured set per second while a lure moves through it. Relay memory: bounded by the caps and the chapter expiry. Client CPU: the fixed step is the same work the frame did, at a steadier rate; a join replays at most one phrase.
